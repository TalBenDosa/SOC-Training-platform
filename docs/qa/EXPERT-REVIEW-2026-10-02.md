# Expert review: rendered live-feed logs (2026-10-02)

Reviewer stance: Tier-3 / IR / detection-engineering lead reviewing training material.
Input: `docs/qa/rendered-logs-sample-2026-10-02.md` (4 runs: nexacorp CS+FortiGate+Proofpoint, medcore defaults S1+Check Point+MDO, quantumbank S1+PAN, globallogis MDE+Check Point+MDO). All sampled stories are **foundation** tier, so this review does not cover core/advanced stories.
Method: I read the evidence for each story and reached my own verdict before reading its answer key. I also cross-checked record epochs against row times, entity IDs between rows, and the renderers in `src/lib/logs/native/sources/`.

## Executive summary

**Verdict: the schema work is strong, but the stories do not hold together as evidence yet.** Taken one at a time, most records would pass a quick look from someone who knows the product. Field sets, envelopes, and vendor quirks are mostly right. Once an analyst starts *pivoting*, the stories fall apart. A host's IP changes from row to row. Process IDs don't link the writer to the persistence. In one case a SentinelOne row shows a CrowdStrike record. Answer keys claim facts that the rendered evidence does not contain. Pivoting is the core skill this platform has to teach, so this is the most important thing to fix.

| Dimension | Score | One-liner |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Vendor fidelity | **7/10** | Native envelopes are excellent (CS PR2, FortiGate kv, PAN CSV, CP exporter, S1 SDL, MDE AH, Okta). Problems: one cross-vendor record, Defender threat names inside CS/S1, and placeholder strings in vendor fields. |
| 2. Internal consistency | **4/10** | Times are consistent. Host IPs, process lineage, tenant/domain names and mitigation states are not. |
| 3. Investigability | **5/10** | A TP verdict is trivially reachable because every story ends in a critical EDR prevention. Many answer-key details (SPF/DKIM, domain age, family, task name, inbound session, unsigned) cannot be found in the evidence. |
| 4. Technical correctness | **6/10** | TTPs are plausible commodity tradecraft. Several mechanics are wrong: extension → powershell, AnyDesk → cmd, a "scheduled task" whose parent is the installer, "blocked from execution" on a running process. |
| Overall | **5.5/10** | About 2 weeks of renderer plumbing would get this to 8+. The authoring is not the problem; the cross-row identity threading is. |

## What's done well

- **Native envelopes are genuinely production-grade.** CS PR2 has `ConfigBuild`, `Tags`, large `TargetProcessId`/`ParentProcessId`, the real all-zero `SHA1HashData` quirk, and `ImageSubsystem` 2 vs 3 for GUI vs console. FortiGate uses kv with nanosecond `eventtime` and correct webfilter logid/categories. PAN-OS THREAT CSV field order and referer slot are right, and the Check Point Log Exporter syslog repeats `layer_name`/`rule_name` per layer the way real multi-layer logs do. S1 SDL dotted fields have correct storyline semantics (explorer-launched processes are roots, children inherit). MDE and MDO use the Event Hub streaming envelope (`time`/`tenantId`/`category`/`properties`), and Okta System Log has `debugData.behaviors`.
- **Times line up.** Every epoch I checked (CS `ProcessStartTime`, FortiGate `eventtime`, CP `time`, S1 `event.time`) matches the row time. Ingest lag (MDE `time` a few seconds after `Timestamp`, CS `created_timestamp` vs `context_timestamp`) looks realistic.
- **NAT is consistent between EDR and firewall.** EDR `externalIp`/`aip` equals firewall `xlatesrc`/NAT source in medcore, quantumbank and globallogis. This is a good teaching hook.
- **Attack-row descriptions are neutral**, as designed ("explorer.exe started …", "connected to …"). Apart from #1, the evidence lives in the record, not in the row line.
- **IOC continuity across stacks.** The same sender IP 45.148.10.77, the same payload SHA256 b233d6… across Proofpoint/MDO/CS/S1/MDE, and the same C2 IPs mean a trainee's IOC notes carry over between stacks.
- **fake-browser-update** shows what every story can look like with the same engine: multi-source, a referer-linked chain, and a blocked stage.
