The Cyber Kill Chain: stages of an intrusion

Lockheed Martin, 2011. Break any link and you break the whole attack.

HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain6 / 82
An attacker must finish each stage before the next. Break any link and you break the whole attack.

Catch the intrusion at Delivery and you have prevented Exploitation, Installation, C2, and the theft or encryption that would have followed.

That is the whole reason it is called a kill chain. The model's value is prediction and context, not academic precision.

Seven stages, and where you can actually see them

StageWhat the attacker doesYour telemetry
ReconnaissanceHarvests names, scans IP ranges, finds the email formatMostly invisible; perimeter scans, VPN probes
WeaponizationPairs an exploit or macro with a delivery vehicleNone, it is on the attacker's side
DeliveryPhishing email lands, link served, USB plugged inMail gateway, proxy, web filtering
ExploitationMacro runs, exploit fires, user double-clicksEDR, Sysmon
InstallationFoothold that survives reboot: backdoor, task, Run keyPersistence artifacts
Command-and-controlHost calls home for instructionsDNS, HTTPS beaconing, outbound connections
Actions on objectivesSteal data, deploy ransomware, move laterallyDamage is often already occurring
Delivery is the first stage where you reliably have telemetry. Catching it there prevents everything after it.

One alert becomes a thread you pull both ways

A host just executed a suspicious macro: that is exploitation.

Look backward

There was a delivery event. Find the email or download that brought this in.

Look forward

Installation and C2 come next. Check persistence and outbound connections right now, before the attacker digs in.

Handoff in shared vocabulary: "we are at installation, no C2 observed yet."

The next analyst instantly knows the state of the intrusion and what to hunt for next.

What the Kill Chain does well, and where it runs out

Strengths

A simple narrative anyone can follow, prediction in both directions from any alert, and a shared vocabulary for handoffs.

Limits

Strictly linear and coarse. Actions on Objectives lumps together weeks of credential theft, discovery and lateral movement inside the network.

ATT&CK fills in the detail the seven stages lack; the Unified Kill Chain in section 12 adds the missing internal loop.

MITRE ATT&CK: tactics, techniques, procedures

A community-curated catalog of real adversary behavior, built from observed incidents.

HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain12 / 82

Three levels, from why to exactly how

LevelWhat it answersExample
Tactic (the WHY)What is the adversary trying to accomplish right now?Execution (TA0002)
Technique (the HOW)How did the adversary accomplish the tactic?PowerShell (T1059.001)
ProcedureThe exact way a particular adversary or tool implements itBase64-encoded PowerShell that downloads a Cobalt Strike beacon
You cannot write a signature for the abstract idea of PowerShell. You can alert on the flags, encoding and parent-child relationship a procedure produces.
HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain13 / 82

Technique vs procedure: which one can you detect?

"The attacker used PowerShell"

Technique

True, but abstract. PowerShell is used legitimately all day, so there is nothing specific to write a signature for.

The exact implementation

Procedure

A base64-encoded command with -EncodedCommand and -WindowStyle Hidden, started by Word, that downloads a beacon. Every part of that is a field you can match.

Detection gets concrete at the procedure level: command-line flags, encoding, and the parent-child relationship.

Fifteen Enterprise tactics (v19), not a straight line

  • Before your networkReconnaissance (TA0043) and Resource Development (TA0042) are largely invisible, so coverage realistically starts at Initial Access.
  • The restInitial Access, Execution, Persistence, Priv Esc, Stealth, Defense Impairment, Cred Access, Discovery, Lateral Mvmt, Collection, C2, Exfil, Impact
  • Not linearAttackers cycle through Discovery, Lateral Movement and Privilege Escalation repeatedly as they go deeper.
  • One technique, many tacticsValid Accounts (T1078) can give Initial Access, Persistence, Privilege Escalation and Stealth at once.
The T1078 point is why stolen credentials are so dangerous: one technique serves four goals.

The fifteen Enterprise tactics in v19, with IDs

v19 split Defense Evasion into two tactics

Up to v18 there were fourteen tactics; Defense Evasion (TA0005) mixed two different goals.

keeps TA0005

Stealth

Hiding and blending in. Something you have to hunt for.

  • Obfuscated Files or Information T1027
  • Masquerading T1036, Process Injection T1055
  • Timestomp T1070.006

new: TA0112

Defense Impairment

Directly disabling or degrading security controls. Usually one of the highest-severity signals you will see.

  • Disable or Modify Tools T1685 (was T1562.001)
  • Event logs T1685.001, T1685.005
  • Disable or Modify System Firewall T1686

Heads up: attack.mitre.org still shows Defense Evasion and T1562 today; TA0112/T1685 are this course's forward-looking v19 numbering.

T1685.001 and T1685.005 were formerly T1562.002 and T1070.001. Defense Impairment is a deliberate act against your own tooling, so treat it as high severity.

Using ATT&CK during triage

Same alert, two analysts: one closes it, one pulls the thread.

HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain21 / 82

Same alert, two analysts

EDR: WINWORD.EXE started powershell.exe with -EncodedCommand.

Closes it

"Suspicious PowerShell, no clear malware, benign." One alert, judged alone, and the intrusion continues unseen.

Pulls the thread

Maps it to techniques, looks backward for the delivery, predicts the next tactics and hunts them, until the incident is confirmed or genuinely ruled out.

ATT&CK is what makes the second approach systematic instead of a matter of intuition.
HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain22 / 82

From one alert to a predicted next-stage hunt

  1. 1Map the evidenceWINWORD.EXE spawns powershell.exe -EncodedCommand: T1566.001 then T1204.002 then T1059.001.
  2. 2Look backwardMail gateway for attachments to that user in the minutes before; proxy for any download.
  3. 3Predict forwardAfter Execution come Persistence, Stealth, Defense Impairment, Discovery and C2. Check the host for each now.
  4. 4Escalate on any hitIf any forward hunt hits, this is a live intrusion, not a one-off.
The backward sweep confirms it is a real attack and yields sender, subject and hash: your pivot to every other recipient.
HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain23 / 82

The triage worksheet

  • Persistence: Run keys T1547.001, tasks T1053.005, services T1543.003
  • C2: beaconing T1071.001, DNS anomalies T1071.004
  • Discovery: T1087, T1082, T1016
You are no longer reacting to one alert; you are hunting the moves the attacker has not made visible yet.

What tactic is this serving? What had to happen before? What will they do next?

Classify the intent, confirm the chain, predict forward. An alert becomes a coordinate on the attacker's map.

Section 4 of 12

From technique to hunt

The detection guidance on each technique page is the bridge from a technique ID to a real query.

HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain26 / 82

Start from the detection model, not the technique name

Since ATT&CK v18 (October 2025) the old detection notes and Data Sources objects are gone.

  1. 1Detection StrategiesEach technique links to one or more strategies, with IDs like DET0001.
  2. 2AnalyticsEach strategy holds platform-specific analytics (IDs like AN0001) describing the behaviour to look for.
  3. 3Data ComponentsEach analytic names the components it needs, like Process Creation and Command Execution; these replace the old Data Sources box.

"Process: Process Creation" in older posts is the retired Data Source format; the component carried over.

The technique name is a human abstraction that never appears in telemetry, so searching for it in your logs finds nothing.
HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain27 / 82

T1059.001 becomes concrete events and strings

  • Process creationWindows Security 4688 with command-line auditing on, or Sysmon Event ID 1, where image is powershell.exe or pwsh.exe.
  • Decoded scriptScript Block Logging, Event 4104.
  • Procedure strings-enc, -w hidden, -ep bypass, -nop, Net.WebClient, Invoke-WebRequest.
  • Add contextAn Office app spawning PowerShell turns a noisy signal into a high-fidelity one.
One flag might be a legitimate admin. Encoded plus hidden plus a Word parent is almost never benign.
HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain28 / 82

How the hunt scores one command line

SignalPointsWhy
Encoded command (-enc, -encodedcommand)+1Hides the real script from casual review
Hidden window (-w hidden)+1Keeps the user from seeing it run
Policy bypass or no profile (-ep bypass, -nop)+1Skips local guardrails
Download cradle (Net.WebClient, Invoke-WebRequest)+1Fetches a second stage
Office parent (Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook)+2The context that makes it high fidelity
Alert thresholdscore >= 2One admin flag alone stays quiet; Office parent alone fires
The weights encode the lesson's point: context beats any single string. Tune them against your own baseline.

T1059.001 as a scored Splunk hunt

Notice the Office parent counts double: the parent-child context turns a noisy signal into a high-fidelity one.

Same logic, two platforms

Splunk over Sysmon

Event ID 1. Fields Image, CommandLine, ParentImage. Matching with match() on a lowercased command line.

KQL over Defender for Endpoint

DeviceProcessEvents. Fields FileName, ProcessCommandLine, InitiatingProcessFileName. Matching with matches regex and has_any.

Field names change between platforms; the behaviour you score does not.

Translate the logic, not the syntax: process name, command-line signals, parent context, a threshold, then a summary per host and user.
HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain31 / 82

The same hunt in KQL over Defender for Endpoint

Same scoring idea as the SPL: each procedure string adds a point and an Office parent adds two. Thirty days across every device is the environment-wide sweep.
HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain32 / 82

Widen the hunt, then baseline it

Sweep the environment

Run the logic across all endpoints for the last thirty days. Other hosts with the same pattern are candidates for the same campaign.

Validate against benign

Admins, installers, SCCM and Intune use PowerShell constantly. Baseline, then exclude known-good accounts, tools and jump hosts.

Who legitimately does this here? What does their version look like? How is the malicious one different?

The difference, the encoding, the parent process, the destination, is your detection.
HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain33 / 82

From hunt to always-on detection

  1. 1Run it and read what matchesLook at everything that normally scores, not only the hits you expected.
  2. 2Name the legitimate usersAdmins, installers, SCCM and Intune, jump hosts.
  3. 3Exclude the known-goodBy service account, deployment tool and host, not by blanket string.
  4. 4Sweep 30 days, every endpointOther hosts with the pattern join the investigation.
  5. 5Promote to a detectionOnly once the remaining matches are ones you would act on.
Adversaries abuse legitimate functionality, so the exclusions are as important as the logic. Write down why each one exists.

Skimmable

Feeds intelligence

From mapped incidents to a coverage decision

  • ATT&CK Navigator colors each technique: green covered, red or blank blind
  • Overlay a group's known techniques: red cells are where you would miss
When leadership asks 'are we protected against ransomware operators?', you answer with this overlay, not a feeling.
HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain38 / 82

Answering 'would we catch group X?' with evidence

  1. 1Map your detectionsEvery rule tagged with the technique IDs it covers, in a coverage layer.
  2. 2Overlay the groupLoad the techniques that group is known to use.
  3. 3Read the red cellsThe group's techniques you would miss today.
  4. 4Plan the next sprintBuild detections for the red techniques the adversaries you face actually use.
This is how a mature SOC decides where its next detection-engineering hours go, instead of chasing whatever was in the news.
HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain39 / 82

Questions only a mapped SOC can answer

  • Which techniques do we detect reliably, and which have we never once seen? Never seen may mean safe, or blind.
  • Which tactics are weakest? Many SOCs are strong on Execution and C2 but weak on Discovery and Collection.
  • Are detections concentrated on a few noisy techniques while whole tactics go uncovered?
  • When a new threat report drops, which of its techniques would we catch today?

The habit: map as you go. Seconds per case, compounding into a coverage map.

Tag every alert, investigation and detection rule with its technique IDs.

The Pyramid of Pain, briefly

David Bianco: rank indicators by how much it costs the attacker when you take one away.

HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain41 / 82

Hashes are trivial to change, TTPs are agonising

Walk the tiers bottom to top: recompile, new IP, new domain, change baked-in artifacts, replace a tool, re-learn how they operate.
HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain42 / 82

Bottom tiers are disposable, the top is not

Indicator tierWhat happens when you block it
Hash valuesReplaced by a recompile in seconds
IP addressesReplaced by a new server in minutes
Network/host artifactsA mutex, a user-agent string, a file path the tool always uses
TTPsA behaviour detection keeps working; evading it means changing how they operate
This is why hash and IP feeds age out so fast. Domain names and tools sit between these tiers.
HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain43 / 82

Block an indicator, or detect a behaviour

Block the IOC

Block the hash: a recompile replaces it in seconds. Block the IP: a new server replaces it in minutes. Useful today, stale tomorrow.

Detect the TTP

"A Word document spawned encoded PowerShell" keeps firing across new files, IPs and domains. Evading it forces the attacker to change how they operate.

Do both, but spend your engineering effort on the right-hand column. Quiz 3 tests exactly this.
HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain44 / 82
ATT&CK is a catalogue of the top of the pyramid. Every technique ID names a behaviour.

"A Word document spawned encoded PowerShell" keeps working regardless of file, IP or domain.

When you choose what to hunt or how much an alert is worth, push your effort as high up the pyramid as the data allows.

D3FEND: defenses mapped to attacks

ATT&CK's deliberate counterpart: a knowledge graph of defensive techniques.

HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain46 / 82

ATT&CK names the move, D3FEND names the response

ATT&CK

Offensive techniques with codes like T1055. What attackers do.

D3FEND

Countermeasures in categories such as Harden, Detect, Isolate, Deceive, Evict, each linked to the offensive techniques it counters.

  • Process Spawn Analysis: flag unusual parent-child launches
  • Network Traffic Analysis: inspect flows for C2 and exfil

When you learn an attack technique, ask what counters it.

You do not need to memorise D3FEND. Internalise the logic: every offensive technique has nameable countermeasures.
HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain47 / 82

D3FEND's seven tactics, in plain words

CategoryGoalWhere an analyst meets it
ModelKnow what you defend: assets, networks, usersAsset inventory, network maps, baselines
HardenMake the technique harder to use before an attackHardening recommendations after an incident
DetectNotice the technique when it happensYour daily work: Process Spawn Analysis, Network Traffic Analysis
IsolatePut barriers between the attacker and what they wantHost isolation, segmentation
DeceiveLure the attacker into revealing themselvesDecoy accounts and honeytokens
EvictRemove the attacker from the environmentKilling processes, revoking stolen credentials
RestoreReturn systems to a known-good stateReimaging, restoring from backup, re-enabling accounts
Detection is one tactic of seven. The rest is knowing the estate, prevention, containment and recovery, which is where a mature incident report's recommendations live.
HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain48 / 82

Why it matters to a front-line analyst

  • Closes the loopAnswers "we know they use technique X, now what?" with the categories of control that address X.
  • Shared defensive vocabularyTeams describe controls precisely for architecture reviews, gap analysis and reporting.
  • Defense is designDetection is one tactic of seven; Model, Harden, Isolate, Deceive, Evict and Restore are the rest.
Same theme as segmentation and least privilege elsewhere in the course.
HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain49 / 82

From a technique to a defensive plan

  1. 1Pick a technique you care aboutFor example one from your coverage gap or a recent incident.
  2. 2Look up its countermeasuresD3FEND links defensive techniques to the offensive techniques they counter.
  3. 3Check which you haveDetect, harden, isolate, deceive or evict: what is in place today?
  4. 4Plan the gapThe missing countermeasures become the recommendation.
This turns 'we know they use technique X' into 'here is what we do about X', which is what leadership actually needs to hear.

Adversary emulation and purple teaming

Prove the detections you believe you have actually fire.

HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain51 / 82

How detections break without anyone noticing

  • Wrong fieldThe rule watches a field that was renamed or never populated.
  • Lost in a migrationThe rule was disabled when the SIEM or EDR changed.
  • Silent log sourceThe data feeding it stopped flowing, so it can never fire.

You often find out only when a real attacker sails through.

Every one of these leaves the coverage map showing green. That gap between believed and actual coverage is what emulation measures.
HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain52 / 82

Two ways to run real techniques safely

Atomic Red Team

Atomic tests

Small, precise, per-technique tests mapped to ATT&CK IDs. One safe command exercises one technique, say T1003, to confirm the detection fires.

CALDERA, emulation plans

Full emulation

Chains many techniques to mimic how a specific real-world group operates end to end, testing detection across a whole intrusion.

Rules break quietly: wrong field, disabled in a migration, log source stopped flowing. You often find out only when a real attacker sails through.
HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain53 / 82

The purple-team loop

Red and blue work together; the goal is better detection, not scoring points.

  1. 1Red executesA technique is run in a controlled, authorised way.
  2. 2Blue watchesWas it detected?
  3. 3Fix the gapIf not, fix it immediately.
  4. 4Re-testConfirm the fix fires, then move to the next technique.

A coverage map is a claim until emulation proves each covered technique alerts.

Bonus: emulation produces safe, labelled training data. You know exactly which technique ran and when.
HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain54 / 82

Purple team vs a traditional red team

Traditional red team

A secret engagement. The goal is to see whether defenders can be beaten.

Purple team

Red and blue in the same room. The goal is to find and close blind spots deliberately, technique by technique, so defenders cannot be beaten.

Both are useful. Purple teaming is the one that directly improves detection coverage week by week.
HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain55 / 82

What emulation gives the analyst

  • A verified mapEach 'covered' technique is proven to alert, not just believed to.
  • Labelled training dataYou know exactly which technique ran and when, so you see precisely what it looks like in your logs.
  • Proof at the top of the pyramidIt tests behaviour detections, the durable ones that matter most.
Emulation is always authorised and controlled. The atomic tests are designed to exercise a technique safely, not to cause damage.

What the mapping buys you

Time

Mapping well: pitfalls and the Navigator

A coverage heatmap is only as honest as the mapping behind it.

HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain62 / 82

Three mistakes behind most bad ATT&CK data

not by alert title

Map by evidence

"Mimikatz detected" does not prove T1003.001. A handle to lsass.exe with read access does. A matched file that never ran supports T1105 at most.

sub-technique only if proven

Right granularity

Encoded powershell.exe is T1059.001. A scheduled job seen only in a summary log stays T1053, not T1053.005 or T1053.003.

one or two IDs per behaviour

Do not over-map

Tagging one encoded PowerShell download with four tactics inflates the heatmap and buries the technique that matters.

Write the observable next to each ID: "T1003.001: Sysmon EID 10, GrantedAccess 0x1010 on lsass.exe".

Guessing a sub-technique makes the data look precise when it is not. Record follow-on techniques when you observe them, not before.

The Unified Kill Chain

Paul Pols, 2017: Kill Chain narrative plus ATT&CK granularity in one model.

HACKTHESOCLesson 18ATT&CK and the Kill Chain74 / 82

Why a third model: each of the first two has a gap

Cyber Kill Chain

Tells a story, but strictly linear and too coarse for what happens inside the network.

MITRE ATT&CK

Exhaustively granular, but a flat matrix of tactics has no inherent order and tells no story.

Unified Kill Chain

Keeps the narrative and slots ATT&CK's tactics and techniques into ordered phases, including the internal loop.

The UKC is additive: it tells you where in the arc a tactic sits, while ATT&CK still names the exact technique and procedure.

The UKC as a triage decision aid

In

Foothold, not yet control. Find delivery, kill persistence, block the C2 channel.

Through

Expanding, likely mid-loop. Assume more hosts are touched; hunt Discovery, Credential Access, Lateral Movement everywhere now.

Out

Encryption or exfil imminent or happening. Contain at network and identity level immediately.

Through is a loop: one lateral-movement sighting almost always means there are more.

Scope the whole loop before assuming the intrusion is contained.

Key takeaways

  1. 01The Kill Chain's seven stages let you look backward to confirm and forward to predict from any alert.
  2. 02ATT&CK v19 has fifteen tactics: Defense Evasion split into Stealth (TA0005) and Defense Impairment (TA0112).
  3. 03At triage: map the evidence, hunt prerequisites backward, then hunt Persistence, C2 and Discovery forward.
