Section 1 of 8

Why dynamic routing: convergence and protocol families

Routers share reachability, rebuild tables and reroute around failures without a human.

HACKTHESOCLesson 104OSPF, EIGRP and HSRP6 / 48

Convergence has four parts

Convergence: the time from a topology change until every router holds a consistent, loop-free view.

  1. 1DetectionInterface down (milliseconds) or hellos stop (seconds, bounded by the dead or hold timer). BFD can cut this under a second.
  2. 2PropagationThe news is flooded or advertised to the other routers.
  3. 3ComputationEach router recomputes best paths (SPF run or DUAL).
  4. 4InstallationThe new best route is written to the RIB and then the FIB.
Static routes fail at step one: they keep pointing into the hole until a human edits them. Interior protocols (OSPF, EIGRP, IS-IS, RIP) run inside one organization; BGP runs between organizations. Longest prefix match is assumed knowledge from the routing fundamentals lesson.

Section 2 of 8

OSPF design: areas, the LSDB and SPF

One shared map per area, one shortest-path tree per router.

HACKTHESOCLesson 104OSPF, EIGRP and HSRP10 / 48

Every OSPF router floods LSAs and builds the same map

Open Shortest Path First (OSPFv2, RFC 2328) is the dominant link-state IGP.

  • LSAEach router describes its own interfaces and neighbors in a Link State Advertisement and floods it to its area.
  • LSDBAll LSAs are stored in the link-state database. Within an area, every router's LSDB must be identical.
  • SPFEach router runs Dijkstra with itself as the root of the tree, giving the best path to every prefix.
  • Loop-freeSame LSDB means consistent trees, so OSPF is loop-free inside an area.
  • CostReference bandwidth divided by interface bandwidth. Path cost is the sum of outgoing interface costs.
The LSDB is the single source of truth. If two routers in one area disagree on a type 1 LSA, something is broken: duplicate router-id, filtering or a flapping link.
HACKTHESOCLesson 104OSPF, EIGRP and HSRP11 / 48

Fix the reference bandwidth or fast links look equal

  • Default reference is 100 Mbps: FastEthernet, Gigabit and 10G all cost 1
  • The value is in Mbps: 100000 makes a 1 Gbps link cost 100 and 10 Gbps cost 10
  • Set it identically on every router; a mismatch gives inconsistent, suboptimal paths
This is the classic OSPF pitfall. You can also override a single link with ip ospf cost, but the reference bandwidth must be consistent across the domain.

        OSPF neighbors, DR/BDR, router-id and configuration

        How routers become neighbors, who gets elected, and how to set it up.

        HACKTHESOCLesson 104OSPF, EIGRP and HSRP15 / 48

        Hello parameters must agree before routers become neighbors

        Hellos go to multicast 224.0.0.5. Defaults: hello 10 s, dead 40 s (30 and 120 on non-broadcast types).

        • Area IDBoth ends of the link must be in the same area.
        • Hello and dead intervalsThe timers must match on both ends.
        • Subnet and maskBoth routers must share the same subnet on the link.
        • AuthenticationSame type and key on both sides.
        • Stub-area flagsMust agree for the neighbor to form.
        • MTUNeeded for a successful database exchange.
        Keep this list in mind: the last section turns it into a troubleshooting checklist. The MTU item is the one that fools people because hellos are small and still get through.
        HACKTHESOCLesson 104OSPF, EIGRP and HSRP16 / 48

        The neighbor state machine, step by step

        1. 1Down, then InitInit: I saw a hello but you did not list me yet.
        2. 22-WayBidirectional hellos. 2-Way as DROTHER is normal on multiaccess.
        3. 3ExStartAgree who is master for the database exchange.
        4. 4ExchangeSwap database descriptors.
        5. 5LoadingRequest the missing LSAs.
        6. 6FullThe adjacency is complete.

        Stuck at Init, ExStart or Exchange is a fault.

        Teach the state as the diagnostic: where a neighbor is stuck tells you which class of problem to look for. This is exactly how the final worked example is solved.
        HACKTHESOCLesson 104OSPF, EIGRP and HSRP17 / 48

        DR and BDR keep a shared segment from flooding redundantly

        On Ethernet-style segments a full mesh of adjacencies would flood the same updates many times.

        Designated Router

        DR

        Elected on the segment. Every other router (DROTHER) becomes fully adjacent only to the DR and BDR.

          Backup DR

          BDR

          Takes over if the DR fails. DROTHER routers send their updates to 224.0.0.6.

            Non-preemptive

            Election rules

            Highest interface priority wins (default 1; 0 means never eligible). Highest router-id breaks ties.

            • A better newcomer does not displace a sitting DR
            • Two routers on a link: ip ospf network point-to-point
            The DR changes only when it fails or the OSPF process is cleared. Priority 200 arriving after a priority 1 DR does nothing, a favourite exam question.
            HACKTHESOCLesson 104OSPF, EIGRP and HSRP18 / 48

            Basic OSPF configuration and verification

            Router-id order: manual, else highest loopback IP, else highest active interface IP. Always set it by hand.

            • passive-interface sends no hellos: right for user LANs, silently breaks an uplink
            • FULL/DR and FULL/BDR are healthy; 2WAY/DROTHER is normal on a shared segment
            Two routers with the same router-id make LSAs overwrite each other, so adjacencies flap and routes go missing. Interface-level config (ip ospf 1 area 0) is more explicit than network statements. OSPFv3 carries IPv6 with link-local addresses and FF02::5 and FF02::6, and still needs a 32-bit router-id.

            Section 4 of 8

            EIGRP: DUAL, successors and the composite metric

            An advanced distance-vector protocol with precomputed backup paths.

            HACKTHESOCLesson 104OSPF, EIGRP and HSRP20 / 48

            EIGRP keeps three tables and sends only partial updates

            Neighbor table

            Adjacent routers, found by hellos to 224.0.0.10 (every 5 seconds, hold time 15 on fast links).

              Topology table

              Every route learned from every neighbor, including the backups.

                Routing table

                Only the best route for each destination.

                  Reliable transport (RTP) carries partial, triggered updates, not a full map.

                  EIGRP is published as informational RFC 7868. Contrast with OSPF: no flooded map, but a topology table that already holds the backups.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 104OSPF, EIGRP and HSRP21 / 48

                  DUAL vocabulary every analyst must know

                  TermMeaning
                  Reported distance (RD)The metric a neighbor reports to reach the destination (also called advertised distance)
                  Feasible distance (FD)The best total metric from this router to the destination
                  SuccessorThe next hop with the lowest FD; it goes into the routing table
                  Feasible successor (FS)A precomputed backup whose RD is strictly less than the successor's FD

                  The feasibility condition guarantees the backup path cannot loop through us.

                  If the successor fails and an FS exists, EIGRP switches instantly with no recomputation. With no FS the route goes Active and queries are sent, which is slower and the origin of stuck-in-active in large networks.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 104OSPF, EIGRP and HSRP22 / 48

                  Reading the topology table

                  • Path one: FD 28416, RD 28160. It is the successor
                  • Path two: RD 25856 is below FD 28416, so a feasible successor despite a worse total
                  Each via line shows total metric then reported distance. The trap is comparing total metrics: the test is the neighbor's RD against the successor's FD.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 104OSPF, EIGRP and HSRP23 / 48

                  The composite metric, neighbor rules and configuration

                  • Metric256 x (10,000,000 / lowest bandwidth in kbps + sum of delays in tens of microseconds), with default K1 and K3 = 1.
                  • Worked exampleSlowest link 100 Mbps gives 100; total delay 110 microseconds is 11 tens: 256 x (100 + 11) = 28416.
                  • Steer with delayBandwidth is the minimum along the path, delay is cumulative. Tuning delay is preferred; bandwidth also affects QoS.
                  • Neighbor rulesAS number, K values, subnet and authentication must agree. An AS mismatch is silent: no error, no neighbor.
                  Configuration is router eigrp 100 with eigrp router-id, network statements, then passive-interface default and no passive-interface on the real uplink. Named mode uses 64-bit wide metrics for very fast links.

                  Choosing a protocol and administrative distance

                  When sources compete, trust decides before metrics are compared.

                  HACKTHESOCLesson 104OSPF, EIGRP and HSRP25 / 48

                  Metrics from different protocols are never compared

                  Same prefix, two sources: the router compares administrative distance (AD) and the lower one wins.

                  AD 110

                  OSPF route

                  Cost 20 for the prefix. Stays in the OSPF database, but not in the routing table.

                    AD 90

                    EIGRP internal

                    Metric 28416. The lower AD wins, so this route is installed.

                      The rule

                      Only routes from the winning source are installed. Metrics are compared only among routes from the same source.

                        An OSPF cost of 20 and an EIGRP metric of 28416 are simply not comparable. Do not let students conclude that 20 is smaller so OSPF wins.

                        Floating static: a backup that waits for the dynamic route

                        • The trailing 130 is the administrative distance
                        • Unused while the dynamic route exists, active once it disappears
                        A normal static route has AD 1 and beats every dynamic protocol. Raising the AD turns it into a backup.

                        Section 6 of 8

                        The default gateway problem and HSRP

                        Hosts know one gateway IP; a first-hop redundancy protocol keeps it alive.

                        HACKTHESOCLesson 104OSPF, EIGRP and HSRP30 / 48
                        Hosts do not run routing protocols: the default gateway is a single point of failure.

                        If the gateway router fails, the host keeps sending frames to the dead router's MAC address until DHCP or an administrator changes the gateway.

                        Even with two healthy distribution routers and OSPF or EIGRP between them, the PC only knows one gateway IP. The failure is on the host side of the router, which dynamic routing cannot fix.
                        HACKTHESOCLesson 104OSPF, EIGRP and HSRP31 / 48

                        Routers share a virtual IP and MAC; HSRP gives them roles

                        Hosts point at the virtual IP. Whichever router owns it answers ARP and forwards traffic.

                        Active

                        Owns the virtual IP and virtual MAC and forwards traffic.

                          Standby

                          Monitors the Active router and takes over when hellos stop.

                            Listen

                            Other routers in the group wait in the Listen state.

                              Hello is 3 seconds and hold is 10 seconds by default. A group is per interface and per group number.

                              HSRP is Cisco proprietary, described in informational RFC 2281. Hosts never change their configuration when the takeover happens.
                              HACKTHESOCLesson 104OSPF, EIGRP and HSRP32 / 48

                              HSRP versions and how the Active router is chosen

                              • Version 1Virtual MAC 0000.0C07.ACxx (xx is the group number in hex), multicast 224.0.0.2 on UDP 1985.
                              • Version 2Multicast 224.0.0.102, virtual MAC 0000.0C9F.Fxxx, groups up to 4095 and IPv6 support.
                              • ElectionHighest priority (default 100, range 0 to 255) wins. The highest real interface IP breaks ties.
                              • Design practiceAlign the Active router with the spanning-tree root for the VLAN, and use several groups to balance load.
                              Misaligned Active and root means traffic crosses the inter-switch link twice. One group per VLAN with a different Active router each balances load.
                              HACKTHESOCLesson 104OSPF, EIGRP and HSRP33 / 48

                              Preemption is off by default

                              Priority only decides who wins an election, not who takes over later.

                              1. 1R1 reloadsR1 has priority 110 and no preempt. R2 has priority 100.
                              2. 2R2 becomes ActiveR2 takes the virtual IP and MAC while R1 is down.
                              3. 3R1 finishes bootingR1 finds R2 already Active.
                              4. 4R1 quietly stays StandbyThe intended primary never resumes its role.

                              Fix: configure preempt. Tracking lowers R1's priority below R2's when the uplink drops, and R2 needs preempt to take over.

                              The most common HSRP misunderstanding. Tracking completes the design: if R1's uplink fails but its LAN side is fine, R1 stays Active and black-holes traffic, unless a decrement pushes it below R2.

                              HACKTHESOCLesson 104OSPF, EIGRP and HSRP35 / 48

                              A two-router HSRP design and how it verifies

                              • Hosts use virtual gateway 10.10.10.1; the real addresses are 10.10.10.2 and 10.10.10.3
                              • If the Gi0/1 uplink drops, priority falls by 20 so that R2 can preempt
                              • P means preempt is on; this router is Active, its partner 10.10.10.3 is Standby
                              R2 gets the same virtual IP and group number, its own real address, default priority 100 and preempt. Without preempt on R2 it would never reclaim Active when R1 degrades through tracking.

                              Cisco-only networks

                              HSRP

                                Multivendor

                                  Worked troubleshooting: OSPF stuck in ExStart

                                  Do not guess: walk the state machine and let the state tell you where to look.

                                  HACKTHESOCLesson 104OSPF, EIGRP and HSRP40 / 48

                                  Walk the state machine instead of guessing

                                  Scenario: R1 (1.1.1.1) and R2 (2.2.2.2) share 10.1.12.0/24 in area 0, but remote prefixes never appear.

                                  1. 1Read the neighbor stateshow ip ospf neighbor shows R2 at EXSTART/DR: hellos agreed, so the fault is later.
                                  2. 2Confirm on the interfaceArea, router-id and timers are fine, but the adjacent neighbor count is 0.
                                  3. 3Compare MTU on both routersR1 has MTU 1500 and R2 has MTU 1400.
                                  4. 4Confirm with a debugdebug ip ospf adj: neighbor has a smaller interface MTU. Use sparingly in production.
                                  5. 5Fix and verifyEqualize the MTUs, then see FULL in show ip ospf neighbor and O routes in show ip route.
                                  The neighbor appears at all only because hellos are small and carry no MTU check. EXSTART therefore points straight at the database exchange.

                                  The evidence: EXSTART, adjacent count 0, and two different

                                  • EXSTART means hellos already agreed: the fault is in the database exchange
                                  • One neighbor but zero adjacent, and the MTUs differ: 1500 versus 1400
                                  • DBD packets carry each side's MTU; a router rejects one larger than its own
                                  Have students read each output aloud and say what it rules out. The restart loop is why the state never leaves EXSTART.

                                  Fix the MTU, not just the symptom

                                  Preferred

                                  Fix: equal MTUs

                                  A real MTU difference also breaks large traffic, so make the MTUs equal.

                                    Hides the symptom

                                    Workaround

                                    ip ospf mtu-ignore on both ends gets the adjacency up without fixing the path.

                                      Timers and area

                                      Other classes

                                      A timer mismatch shows in debug ip ospf hello: Dead R 40 C 120, Hello R 10 C 30 (received versus configured).

                                      • Fix with ip ospf hello-interval and dead-interval

                                      Key takeaways

                                      1. 01Dynamic routing repairs tables after failures; convergence is detection, propagation, computation and installation.
                                      2. 02OSPF floods LSAs inside an area and runs SPF on an identical LSDB; set the reference bandwidth and router-id by hand.
                                      3. 03OSPF neighbors need matching area, timers, subnet, authentication and MTU; stuck in Init, ExStart or Exchange is a fault.
                                      4. 04EIGRP's DUAL picks the successor by FD; a feasible successor needs an RD strictly below the successor's FD.
                                      5. 05Administrative distance chooses the source before metrics matter: internal EIGRP (90) beats OSPF (110).
                                      6. 06HSRP, VRRP and GLBP share a virtual IP; HSRP preemption is off by default, so priority alone never forces a takeover.

                                      Check your understanding, question 1 of 3

                                      R1 and R2 share a GigabitEthernet link in area 0 with matching hello and dead timers. R1 shows the neighbor stuck in EXSTART, and R2's MTU is 1400 while R1's is 1500. What explains it?

                                      EXSTART comes after hellos matched. DBDs carry each side's MTU and a router rejects a larger one, so the exchange restarts. Priority only affects DR choice, one-way hello loss leaves INIT, and reference bandwidth only affects cost.

                                      Choose an answer, or press R to reveal it.

                                      HACKTHESOCLesson 104OSPF, EIGRP and HSRP46 / 48

                                      Check your understanding, question 2 of 3

                                      An EIGRP router's successor has FD 28416. A second neighbor reports RD 25856, with a total metric of 33792 through itself. What is the second path?

                                      RD 25856 is strictly below the successor's FD 28416, so the path is a feasible successor despite its worse total metric. Total metric is the wrong test, and the topology table keeps non-best paths too.

                                      Choose an answer, or press R to reveal it.

                                      R1 has HSRP priority 110 and R2 has 100, with no preempt on either. R1 reloads and R2 becomes Active. R1 finishes booting. Which router is Active afterwards?
