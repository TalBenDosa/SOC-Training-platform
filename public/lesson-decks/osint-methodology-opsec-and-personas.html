What OSINT is and where a SOC uses it

Intelligence from publicly available sources, built to answer a specific question.

HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC6 / 47

Three words in the definition do the work

OSINT is intelligence derived from sources anyone can lawfully access.

  • Publicly availableNo bypassing a login, a paywall you are not entitled to, or any technical access control.
  • IntelligenceThe output is analyzed and tied to a question, not a pile of screenshots.
  • For a decision makerSomeone will act: block a domain, file a takedown, warn an executive, or scope an incident.
Sources include websites, public registries, certificate logs, code repositories, news, public social posts, archived pages and leaked-data reporting.

Use caseQuestion being answeredTypical output
Threat intelligenceWho is behind this campaign, and what else do they use?Related domains, infrastructure clusters, TTP notes
Phishing and domain investigationIs this domain malicious, who registered it, when did it appear?Verdict, IOCs, takedown package

The fifth row is the safest and often the highest value: authorization is straightforward and the findings are directly fixable.

Ask which row students would start with. Investigating your own footprint is the easiest place to learn the method.

Open does not mean harmless

Every fact may be public, and collecting it can still create obligations.

What is important to know

Collecting information about a person creates privacy obligations. A registrant, profile or developer handle may belong to an innocent bystander.

    What is important to ask

    Three questions before you collect anything.

    • What decision does this support?
    • What is the minimum I need to collect?
    • Who is affected by what I write down?

    WHOIS, passive DNS and scanners are covered in the enrichment-tools and threat-intelligence lessons. This lesson is the method around them.

    A compromised server or a stolen name makes the visible owner a victim. Keep that in mind every time you write a name in a note.

    The intelligence cycle applied to OSINT

    A repeating loop that separates an investigation from random browsing.

    HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC10 / 47

    Five phases, repeated until the question is answered

    It protects you from the most common failure: collecting endlessly without a goal.

    1. 1Direction (planning)Set the requirement: question, scope, deadline, audience.
    2. 2CollectionGather raw data from chosen sources, driven by a source plan.
    3. 3ProcessingNormalize and organize: defang, deduplicate, UTC, translate.
    4. 4AnalysisTurn data into judgments with stated confidence.
    5. 5DisseminationDeliver to the right audience in a usable form.

    Ongoing feedback: the consumer says whether the product helped, and the next requirement is refined.

    Collection should be driven by the requirement and a source plan, not by the tool that happens to be open.
    HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC11 / 47

    Each phase has an OSINT job and a typical failure

    PhaseOSINT exampleCommon failure
    DirectionIs the reported domain part of a campaign against our customers? Due in 4 hoursNo written question, so the work never ends
    CollectionRegistration records, certificate transparency entries, archived pagesCollecting what is easy instead of what answers the question
    ProcessingDefang URLs, deduplicate, convert timestamps to UTC, translate textMixed time zones, lost source links
    AnalysisThree domains share a registrant pattern and hosting, likely one actorTreating correlation as proof
    DisseminationIOC list to the SOC, takedown request to legal, summary to managementOne report format for every reader
    Walk the failures one by one and ask students which they have seen. Analysis is where correlation quietly becomes proof.
    HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC12 / 47

    A good requirement has a question, a boundary and a deadline

    Bad requirement

    Look into this domain.

    • No question to answer
    • No boundary on method
    • No deadline

    Good requirement

    Determine whether the domain in ticket 4412 is operated by the same actor as the three domains we blocked last month, using only passive public sources, and report by 15:00.

      Also ask: what would change my conclusion, when is this good enough, and who reads it?

      Direction deserves the most care. Analysts who collect before writing the question over-collect, which raises both legal exposure and noise.
      HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC13 / 47

      Processing is underrated

      • Convert to UTCSources arrive in different time zones and formats; normalize every timestamp.
      • Defang indicatorsWrite hxxp and [.] instead of live links so nobody clicks by accident, including a colleague reading your notes.
      • Keep the source linkPreserve the original link next to every extracted fact; later analysis depends on it.
      • Collect to the requirementCollecting before writing the question over-collects: more legal exposure and more noise.
      These habits cost minutes. Mixed time zones and lost source links are the two failures that hurt most later.

      Section 3 of 8

      Legality and ethics: what you may collect

      Your methods must be lawful and authorized, whatever the target did.

      HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC15 / 47

      Four legal boundaries that apply almost everywhere

      General education, not legal advice: your legal or privacy team owns the final answer.

      Non-public data

      No guessed, stolen or leaked credentials, exposed admin panels or private open buckets. Stop, record the minimum, escalate.

        Terms of service

        Many platforms forbid scraping, fake accounts or profiling. A breach can get accounts banned and make evidence look sloppy.

          Privacy law

          Personal data can fall under GDPR-style regimes even if public. Have a purpose, collect the minimum, delete at case close.

            Scope and authorization

            Work under a written ticket. Investigating a colleague, ex-partner or competitor out of curiosity is misuse of access.

              Computer misuse laws such as the US CFAA and UK Computer Misuse Act, or local equivalents, can apply to unauthorized access.

              A phishing operator committing fraud does not give an analyst permission to commit a crime in return. If you stumble on exposed private data, do not browse it.
              HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC16 / 47

              Ethical limits go further than the law

              • No entrapmentDo not lure a person or actor into a crime they would not otherwise commit.
              • No harassment or doxxingDo not contact, shame or publish a suspect's details. Findings go to the case owner, legal or law enforcement.
              • No impersonation of real peopleNever pose as a real person.
              • ProportionalityThe harm of collecting must be justified by the risk you are addressing.
              These limits protect third parties. Findings leave the investigation only through the case owner, legal or law enforcement.
              HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC17 / 47

              Observation is passive; interaction is where the risk starts

              Passive observation

              Reading what is public, without the target noticing.

              • Reading a public page
              • Reading a public record

              Interaction

              Can alert the target, change the evidence, or cross legal lines. Making your system connect to the target counts.

              • Sending a message
              • Joining a private group
              • Filling a form
              • Clicking a tracking link

              Default to passive. Anything more needs explicit approval from legal and your manager.

              The key distinction of the whole lesson. Whenever a plan includes interacting, the next step is approval, not a clever workaround.
              HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC18 / 47
              Would you explain this step to a court, a regulator and the person being investigated?

              Also ask: is this source public to everyone, and does this step need me to deceive anyone?

              Use this as a gut check before any collection step that feels borderline.

              Section 4 of 8

              Analyst OPSEC: never investigate as yourself

              Stop the target learning that they are watched, or who is watching.

              HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC20 / 47

              Two bad outcomes OPSEC must prevent

              They learn they are watched

              Operators watch logs for repeated visits from a brand. That tells them a takedown is coming, and they move.

                They learn who you are

                The server logs your source IP, which is your company's known egress range, plus user-agent, language, screen size and sometimes a referrer.

                  Many kits check visitors against lists of security vendors and corporate ranges, then serve defenders a harmless decoy.

                  Opening a suspicious site from a normal work laptop produces both outcomes at once, and may only show you the decoy.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC21 / 47

                  Six attribution leaks that catch analysts most often

                  • Corporate IP or proxyBrowsing from the corporate IP range or the corporate network proxy.
                  • Shared browser profileLogged into a personal or corporate account, so cookies and single sign-on link you to the visit.
                  • Real name or work emailUsed on any research account.
                  • Profile-view notificationsViewing a professional-network profile while logged in can show the owner that your company looked.
                  • Extensions and autofillSaved passwords, autofill and extensions can expose identity details.
                  • Mixed investigationsTwo unrelated cases in one browser are cross-linked through shared cookies.
                  Most of these are habits, not tooling. A clean profile per case removes four of the six.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC22 / 47

                  A layered research environment

                  ControlPurpose
                  Dedicated research machine or VM, clean snapshot per caseNo personal data, no history carried between cases
                  Separate browser profile, no sign-ins, minimal extensionsPrevents account and cookie linkage
                  Approved commercial VPN or dedicated research egressSource IP is not your corporate range
                  Compartmentalization: one case, one profile, one set of notesA leak in one case does not expose the others
                  Isolated network segment with no route to internal systemsIf something is malicious, it cannot reach your network
                  Device and time-zone hygieneAvoid leaking local language or time zone through the browser

                  Check that your organization approves the tooling and the network egress.

                  Ask for each row what the target would see without it. Every control closes one specific leak from the previous slide.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC23 / 47
                  The best OPSEC for passive work is not visiting the target at all.

                  Use sources that never connect to it. A VPN is only one layer: it moves trust to the provider and does nothing about fingerprinting or logged-in accounts.

                  Archived copies, certificate logs and registration records leave no footprint on the attacker's server. A VPN is not magic.

                  Section 5 of 8

                  Research accounts and personas: hard limits

                  A defensive concept for passive viewing, with firm boundaries.

                  HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC25 / 47
                  A research account is an OPSEC measure, not a way to deceive a real person.

                  A viewing account named after your security team announces your interest to an actor who watches profile views. An anonymous one reveals nothing.

                  Teams use personas because some content needs sign-in, some services show the owner a viewer list, and some fraud channels need weeks of passive following.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC26 / 47

                  A defensible persona versus a prohibited one

                  Defensible persona

                  Passive reading of public content, nothing more.

                  • Generic, clearly fictional profile
                  • Approved registration, inventory recorded
                  • Used only from the isolated environment
                  • Passive viewing only

                  Never

                  Interaction moves into social engineering and possible entrapment.

                  • Impersonating a real person or brand
                  • Befriending, messaging, joining private groups
                  • Evading a platform ban on your organization
                  • Collecting sensitive data on private people

                  Get legal sign-off and check the platform's current rules first: a persona that breaks them can taint your evidence.

                  Using someone's real photo or identity is both unethical and often illegal. If the answer to 'am I only reading?' is no, stop and escalate.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC27 / 47

                  Persona hygiene pitfalls

                  Ask first: can this be done without logging in at all?

                  • Reused identifiersA shared phone number, recovery email or profile photo links personas together.
                  • One wrong loginLogging in from the wrong browser profile once permanently ties the persona to your real network history.
                  • Suspicious activityActivity that looks automated triggers verification and bans.
                  • No retirementForgetting to retire the persona when the case ends.
                  Also ask: is a persona approved for this case, and am I only reading, or about to interact?

                  Section 6 of 8

                  Source reliability, verification, and bias

                  A fact is only as useful as your confidence in it.

                  HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC29 / 47

                  The Admiralty scale grades the source and the information

                  A letter for the source, a number for the specific information. Also called NATO evaluation.

                  SourceMeaningInfoMeaning
                  ACompletely reliable, proven track record1Confirmed by other independent sources
                  BUsually reliable2Probably true, consistent with other information
                  CFairly reliable3Possibly true, not confirmed
                  DNot usually reliable4Doubtfully true
                  EUnreliable5Improbable
                  FReliability cannot be judged6Truth cannot be judged

                  The ratings are independent: a trusted registry (A) can hold a stale record (3); an anonymous post (F) later confirmed twice becomes F1.

                  A rating like B2 tells the reader how much weight to give a statement. Practise by rating three invented sources aloud.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC30 / 47

                  Five verification techniques

                  • Corroborate independentlyTwo articles copying one press release are one source; two tools on one data feed are one source.
                  • Check provenanceWhere did it originate, and who benefits if you believe it?
                  • Check the timeOld data passed off as current is the most common error. Record capture dates.
                  • Verify mediaReverse image search can reveal a stolen photo; metadata can be faked, so it is a lead, not proof.
                  • Treat attribution carefullyShared hosting, registrars and templates are weak signals: unrelated actors use the same providers.
                  The independence point is the one students miss. Agreement between echoes is not corroboration.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC31 / 47

                  Cognitive bias is a bigger threat than any technical limit

                  Confirmation bias

                  Noticing only evidence that supports the first theory. List what would disprove it.

                    Anchoring

                    Letting the first data point dominate. Hold at least two competing hypotheses.

                      Availability bias

                      Assuming the actor you read about last week is behind everything.

                        Mirror imaging

                        Assuming the attacker thinks and behaves as you would.

                          Ask: how do I know this, is this one source or many, and what would prove me wrong?

                          Name the bias you are most prone to. The counter for the first two is procedural, so build it into the report template.
                          HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC32 / 47
                          Separate facts from judgments, and state your confidence.

                          Fact: the certificate was issued on this date. Judgment: the domain was likely prepared for a campaign. Say 'assessed with moderate confidence'.

                          Stating confidence is not weakness. It is what allows the reader to make a good decision.

                          Section 7 of 8

                          Recording and reporting: hashes and custody

                          If evidence cannot be shown authentic and unaltered, it may be worthless.

                          HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC34 / 47

                          Write notes while you work, not afterward from memory

                          • Case and requirementThe case number, the requirement, and your name.
                          • When and whereDate and time in UTC, the research machine used, the egress IP.
                          • Source and queryThe source visited and the search terms used.
                          • What you sawThe observation itself, written at the time.
                          • Changes of courseIf you changed direction, note why.

                          A timestamped, sequential log shows your method and lets another analyst reproduce it.

                          Findings often feed takedowns, legal action, insurance claims or law enforcement. Good habits cost minutes and protect weeks of work.
                          HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC35 / 47

                          From capture to chain of custody

                          1. 1Capture the pageFull-page screenshot with URL and time, plus a saved page, PDF or raw record.
                          2. 2Keep the contextCapture the thread around a claim; a lone post can mislead.
                          3. 3Hash every fileCompute SHA-256 and store the hash in your notes.
                          4. 4Protect originalsKeep originals read-only and work from copies.
                          5. 5Log the custodyWho captured it, where it is stored, who accessed it, any transfers.

                          ISO/IEC 27037 and NIST SP 800-86 describe this thinking; your legal team names the exact standard for a case.

                          A changed file, even by one byte, produces a different hash. That is the proof of integrity. Annotate copies, never the original.
                          HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC36 / 47
                          Asking an archive service to save a live page makes that service connect to the target.

                          Use it only when that interaction is approved, and first check whether an existing snapshot already exists.

                          Archives are useful, but a fresh capture request is interaction by proxy. Preferring an existing snapshot keeps the work passive.
                          HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC37 / 47

                          A report structure that works

                          • TLP tells recipients how widely they may redistribute the report
                          • Remove personal data the reader does not need; encrypt sensitive evidence
                          The skeleton is a template, not a quoted report. Ask: could a colleague reproduce this, can I prove files are unchanged, and who may see it?

                          Worked example: a passive phishing investigation

                          A customer forwards a phishing email to hxxps://secure-c0rp-login[.]example. Investigate without touching the attacker.

                          HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC39 / 47

                          Steps 1 to 4: direction, OPSEC, evidence, source plan

                          1. 1Direction and scopePassive sources only, four hours. Confirm the ticket authorizes it; no visiting, no forms, no contacting the registrant.
                          2. 2OPSEC setupClean research VM, isolated segment, approved egress, fresh profile. Notes start with case, UTC time, machine, egress IP.
                          3. 3Extract from the report itselfHeaders, sender domain, links, attachments: preserve the message, hash it, record who forwarded it and when.
                          4. 4Passive source planChoose sources that never connect to the suspect server (next slide).
                          The requirement: assess whether the domain impersonates our brand, identify related infrastructure, and recommend blocking and takedown, passively, within four hours. Defang the indicator in all notes.
                          HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC40 / 47

                          Passive sources that answer each question

                          QuestionPassive source type
                          When was it registered, by whom, through which registrar?Registration data lookup (RDAP or WHOIS) and historical records
                          What certificates were issued and when?Certificate transparency logs
                          What IPs has it resolved to, and what else shared them?Passive DNS data
                          Has it been reported already?Public blocklists and reputation feeds
                          Does it look like a brand lookalike?Your own lookalike-domain monitoring, similarity comparison with your brand
                          What did the page look like?Existing archived snapshots, if any

                          Trap: a public URL scanner is interaction by proxy. The target still sees a visit, and public results can be seen by the attacker.

                          A scanner may be acceptable if approved, using the private scan option, but it is not purely passive collection. Tool details are in the enrichment-tools lesson.

                          Step 5: test the alternative before you conclude

                          Supports phishing

                          Together these strongly support phishing.

                          • Registered days ago
                          • Certificate issued the same day
                          • Brand imitated by character substitution
                          • Hosted with other lookalikes

                          Alternatives to test

                          Rate each fact with a source and an information grade.

                          • A legitimate brand-protection domain
                          • A partner
                          • A parked domain

                          Never name a person or group as the actor based on hosting overlap alone.

                          This is where confirmation bias bites. Make students state which finding would change their conclusion.
                          HACKTHESOCLesson 120OSINT methodology and OPSEC42 / 47

                          Steps 6 to 8: document, disseminate, close

                          1. 1Document and hashTimestamped screenshots, raw records and SHA-256 hashes, stored in the case folder with a custody note.
                          2. 2DisseminateIOCs to the SOC, takedown package via legal or brand protection, assessment with confidence to management, TLP label.
                          3. 3CloseRevert the VM, retire temporary accounts, apply retention, and record what you did not check, such as active content analysis.

                          Do not contact the registrant: the name may belong to an innocent person whose details were abused.

                          Recording what was not checked lets a separate approved sandbox process pick it up if needed.

                          Key takeaways

                          1. 01OSINT is intelligence from lawfully accessible sources, analyzed to answer a question for a decision maker.
                          2. 02Follow the cycle: direction, collection, processing, analysis, dissemination. Write the question before you collect.
                          3. 03Stay lawful and passive: no non-public data, respect terms and privacy law, work under a ticket, never interact without approval.
                          4. 04Never investigate as yourself: isolated VM, clean profile, approved egress; best of all, do not touch the target.
                          5. 05Personas are for passive viewing only, approved and recorded. Rate sources with a letter and a number, and state confidence.
                          6. 06Take notes as you work, hash evidence with SHA-256, keep originals read-only, and log the chain of custody.
