An agent that decides on the device

Autonomous detection without the cloud round trip.

HACKTHESOCLesson 13SentinelOne6 / 49

Three engines, three contributions

Before execution

Static AI

Evaluates a file's structure before it runs, without needing a signature match. Scanning at the door.

While running

Behavioral AI

Tracks what a process reads, writes, spawns and connects to. Catches fileless attacks through tools like PowerShell.

Recording

Deep Visibility

Full-fidelity telemetry of everything every process does. Blocks nothing; raw material to hunt and investigate.

Knowing which engine produced a verdict tells you where it came from and how to weigh it.
HACKTHESOCLesson 13SentinelOne7 / 49

Detect mode vs Protect mode

Detect mode

Observes and logs everything, takes no automatic blocking action. Every response waits for a human. Common during initial rollout.

Protect mode

Acts on its own verdict immediately: kill a process, quarantine a file, even trigger Rollback, without waiting for an analyst.

Leaving large parts of the fleet in Detect mode trades automatic protection for human review latency.

Give that tradeoff the same scrutiny as any other prevention-policy decision.
HACKTHESOCLesson 13SentinelOne8 / 49

What autonomous buys you, and what it does not

Offline still protected

A laptop on an airplane still evaluates behavior and blocks locally, then syncs events when it reconnects. Offline is not automatically unprotected.

Bounded by local state

The verdict reflects the model and policy loaded at that moment, not live cross-customer correlation like CrowdStrike's Threat Graph.

The incident-review question: was this endpoint actually protected during the gap, or just logging? Check its mode and its local action log after reconnection.

Storyline: one narrative, not forty alerts

Automatic correlation under a shared StorylineID.

HACKTHESOCLesson 13SentinelOne10 / 49

Eight raw events, one Storyline

Point at the right side: the Storyline supplies the tree and one ID to pivot on, but it does not decide whether the activity is malicious.
HACKTHESOCLesson 13SentinelOne11 / 49

Same causal chain, same StorylineID

  • The agent tracked parent-child cause and effect on the device, live
  • One Threat card is one story
  • A different StorylineID is a different story
Storyline does the timestamp-and-PID matching a tired analyst is most likely to shortcut. You interpret an assembled narrative instead of proving which events belong together.
HACKTHESOCLesson 13SentinelOne12 / 49

Reading a Storyline correctly

  • Start at the rootThe event with no causal parent in the chain is your best initial-access candidate.
  • Downstream is consequenceEverything after the root is part of the same incident, not a separate one.
  • Judge in contextA connection from a macro-spawned process weighs differently from the same connection by a clean browser.
  • Verify the deciding stepTrust it as a starting point, but check timestamps and command lines where your response hinges.
Storyline is more reliable than statistical correlation because the agent saw lineage directly, but ambiguous cases exist, such as a reused parent with unrelated children.

Threats vs Alerts, and mitigation states

How confident the engines are, and what has already been done.

HACKTHESOCLesson 13SentinelOne14 / 49

Alert vs Threat

AspectAlertThreat
ConfidenceLower: unusual enough to mention, not certainHigher: enough signal to act
VerdictNot scored malicious outrightMalicious, Suspicious, or Potentially Unwanted Application
Where triage happensThe raw Alerts feedThe Threats page, the primary daily workspace
Each Threat carries a Classification, a Confidence Level as a percentage, and a Risk Level from Low to Critical. The Action Taken field records what the agent already did.
HACKTHESOCLesson 13SentinelOne15 / 49

Mitigation status is a state, not an event

Not Mitigated

Ran with no automatic response: Detect mode, or confidence below the auto-action threshold.

Mitigated

Agent or analyst already took a containing action such as Kill or Quarantine.

Marked Benign

Analyst judged it a false positive. Feeds back into future scoring.

Rollback

Rollback Available / Rolled Back: ransomware file damage actively reversed.

A Not Mitigated Critical sitting untouched deserves very different urgency than a Mitigated one.

At shift handoff, read Classification, Confidence and mitigation status together rather than reacting to the Risk Level color alone.
HACKTHESOCLesson 13SentinelOne16 / 49

Working a Threat: verdict, status, notes

Two analyst-owned fields sit beside the engine's classification.

Analyst Verdict

Your conclusion: True positive, Suspicious, False positive, or Undefined until someone decides.

Incident Status

Unresolved, In progress, Resolved. Set In progress when you pick it up, add notes as you go, Resolved only when verdict, mitigation and notes agree.

Beyond this endpoint

True positive: blocklist the hash at the right scope. False positive: create an exclusion from the Threat. A folder-path exclusion silences anything dropped there later.

Blocklist and exclusions follow the Global, Account, Site and Group hierarchy.

A hash or signing-certificate exclusion silences one known-good file. A folder-path exclusion is a hole an attacker can use. The Threat's notes are what the next shift reads.

Section 4 of 13

Rollback: what VSS snapshots undo

The most famous feature, and the one analysts most often overstate.

HACKTHESOCLesson 13SentinelOne18 / 49
Rollback restores file content that a stopped threat encrypted or deleted. Kill and quarantine cannot.

The agent keeps shadow-copy-style snapshots, built on the same technology as Windows VSS, and can recover thousands of files in minutes.

Kill and quarantine stop further damage but leave already-encrypted files exactly as encrypted. Rollback can run automatically in Protect mode or be triggered by an analyst.
HACKTHESOCLesson 13SentinelOne19 / 49

What Rollback structurally cannot do

Recall exfiltrated data

Double-extortion operators copy files out before encrypting. Rollback restores your copy, not theirs.

Remove persistence

Restoring files does not clean a registry key, scheduled task or rogue service. The door stays open.

Recover lost snapshots

Attackers who delete shadow copies first defeat it. That is why vssadmin delete shadows is a red flag.

Rollback is a fast recovery for one problem, not an undo-the-breach button.

Treating it as a general undo leads directly to under-scoping incidents that also involved data theft, persistence or credential compromise.

Section 5 of 13

Deep Visibility and the response ladder

Ask a specific question, then match the action to the damage.

HACKTHESOCLesson 13SentinelOne21 / 49
"Show me everything suspicious" is not a query.

"Every process that wrote a .lockbit file in the last 24 hours, and what created it" is. Long retention lets you look back before the first alert.

Example fleet-wide questions: which endpoints ran a given hash, which processes connected to an external IP last week, which created a ransomware extension in the last day.
HACKTHESOCLesson 13SentinelOne22 / 49

The response ladder

ActionWhat it addressesWhat it leaves
Disconnect from NetworkCuts all traffic except the management console: C2 and lateral movement stopNothing removed yet; release with Reconnect to Network
Kill ProcessA malicious process running in memory nowFiles already dropped or modified
Quarantine ThreatMoves the malicious file where it cannot executeAnything it already did before quarantine
RemediateReverts system changes: registry, persistenceFile content
RollbackRestores encrypted or deleted file contentExfiltrated data and anything outside the snapshot
Disconnect from Network is an endpoint action, not a Threat action: mitigation, Remote Shell and telemetry keep working. Rollback is the only rung that addresses file-content damage already done.
HACKTHESOCLesson 13SentinelOne23 / 49

Pick the rung by how far it got

  1. 1Spread, live C2 or hands-on-keyboard?Disconnect from Network first, then keep going down the ladder.
  2. 2Still running in memory only?Kill Process.
  3. 3File written to disk?Quarantine Threat.
  4. 4Config or persistence changed?Remediate to revert system changes.
  5. 5Files encrypted or deleted?Rollback to restore content from snapshot.
  6. 6None of the above?Done. No further action needed.
Network quarantine comes first when the attack is live, because the rungs below only remove what you have already found. An unnecessary Rollback can revert legitimate user changes made after the snapshot.
HACKTHESOCLesson 13SentinelOne24 / 49

Remote Shell: live response on one host, gated on purpose

A full interactive PowerShell or Bash session on the endpoint, from the console.

  • When to use itThe Storyline leaves a question only the live machine answers: what else runs, what sits in a folder, which scheduled tasks exist.
  • The gatesEnabled in the endpoint's policy, role permission plus two-factor authentication, a session password you set, every session logged.
  • How to use itAgainst a quarantined host where you can, recording each command in your case notes.
  • Many hosts at onceUse RemoteOps instead, covered later in this lesson.
Anything you change on the host is a change to evidence. That is why each command belongs in the case notes.

Hunting raw telemetry with PowerQuery

The Storyline is the agent's answer. PowerQuery is how you ask your own questions.

HACKTHESOCLesson 13SentinelOne26 / 49

What querying the raw layer lets you do

  • Hunt a behavior fleet-wideEvery endpoint where a parent-child pair occurred, even when nothing alerted.
  • Pivot at scaleTake one indicator and query every endpoint that saw it, across months of telemetry.
  • Aggregate for outliersGroup and count to surface the rarest: the frequency-analysis backbone of hunting.

Know which event types hold which facts, filter hard on fields, and scope tightly by time and endpoint.

Scope tightly by time and endpoint before you read. A query that reliably finds something should not stay a one-off: that is STAR.
HACKTHESOCLesson 13SentinelOne27 / 49

In PowerQuery, src is the actor and tgt is what it acted on

Process Creation

The actor src.process. is the parent; tgt.process. is the child it created. Office spawned PowerShell: Office in src.process.name, tgt.process.name = 'powershell.exe'.

File or network event

The actor is the process that wrote the file or opened the connection. The target is tgt.file.path, or dst.ip.address and dst.port.number.

The common mistake

Filtering on src.process.name = 'powershell.exe' finds PowerShell creating something. That is a different question.

This naming rule trips up almost everyone. Read the event type first, then decide which side of the src/tgt pair holds the process you care about.

Worked example: Office-to-PowerShell

A real PowerQuery hunt in SentinelOne's own dot-notation fields.

HACKTHESOCLesson 13SentinelOne29 / 49

Office apps spawning PowerShell, last 7 days

  • Actor (the parent) is Office; target (the child) is PowerShell
  • Keep the Storyline ID column: it is your next pivot
  • sort -event.time: the minus means descending; there is no desc keyword
Names are stored as the system reported them, so WINWORD.EXE and winword.exe both appear. In production use the case-insensitive operator form, for example contains:anycase.
HACKTHESOCLesson 13SentinelOne30 / 49

Same shape, two different stories

  • Row 1: hidden window plus base64 -enc: escalate
  • Row 2: readable is not benign; check role, add-in or change record
  • tgt.process.cmdline separates them, not the parent-child shape
Outlook does not normally launch admin scripts, so row 2 is checked against context before it is closed. Storyline ID and event.time columns trimmed for width.
HACKTHESOCLesson 13SentinelOne31 / 49

Stack it, then pivot on the Storyline ID

  • estimate_distinct() counts distinct hosts per Office parent
  • One Storyline ID filter returns every event tied to that chain
  • No minus: oldest first, the order you read an attack in
An IP Connect to 203.0.113.77:443 in the chain is the next pivot: query dst.ip.address = '203.0.113.77' fleet-wide, and network-quarantine the endpoints that talked to it before digging further.

STAR: turning a hunt into a rule

A hunt that works should graduate into a standing detection.

HACKTHESOCLesson 13SentinelOne33 / 49

From one-time hunt to permanent coverage

  1. 1Validate a PowerQueryProve it reliably identifies the malicious or unwanted behavior.
  2. 2Save it as a STAR ruleThe platform evaluates it continuously against incoming telemetry.
  3. 3Start in alert postureCheck fidelity before wiring in an automatic response.
  4. 4Maintain itA noisy query becomes a noisy alert, or a false positive acted on at machine speed.
STAR is detection-as-code's cousin: a reviewed, saved, repeatable artifact that outlasts the analyst who wrote it. Hunting finds it once; STAR finds it every time after.

Two extensions, two blind spots

Unmanaged devices

Ranger

Console hierarchy and policy inheritance

Where a setting lives decides what it really applies to.

HACKTHESOCLesson 13SentinelOne37 / 49

Four nested scope levels

LevelScopeTypical use
GlobalThe entire tenantBaseline defaults everyone inherits unless overridden
AccountA business unit or customer (MSSP)Org-wide policy distinct from other accounts
SiteA location, business unit or logical groupingLocation- or department-specific needs
GroupA cluster of similar endpoints within a SiteServers vs workstations vs kiosks
Each level inherits from the one above unless overridden. Exclusions and blocklists follow the same hierarchy.
HACKTHESOCLesson 13SentinelOne38 / 49

An override hiding under correct-looking settings

When mitigation looks softer than expected, check which level set the mode for this endpoint's Group. Same trap as Falcon Host Group policy mismatches.

Firewall Control and Device Control

Network and peripheral policy that never raises a Threat card.

HACKTHESOCLesson 13SentinelOne40 / 49

First match wins, and USB by class or serial

  • Rule 2 is never reached for HTTPS: rule 1 matched first
  • Firewall Control overrides the OS firewall, e.g. Windows Firewall
  • Device actions: Allow Read & Write, Allow Read, Block
A USB copy runs no malicious code, so Storyline has nothing to tag. These modules sit beside the detection engines; check their own policy and rule order.

Scripts and forensic collection across many endpoints at once.

HACKTHESOCLesson 13SentinelOne42 / 49

Forty endpoints, one operation

  1. 1Deep Visibility confirms scopeSame malicious PowerShell on 40 endpoints across 3 Sites in ten minutes.
  2. 2Target all 40 in RemoteOpsBuilt on Remote Script Orchestration (RSO), across Windows, macOS, Linux.

Singularity Identity: deception for AD

Making malicious reconnaissance identify itself.

HACKTHESOCLesson 13SentinelOne44 / 49
No legitimate user or process ever touches a decoy, so touching one is the detection.

Singularity Identity plants fake credentials, decoy accounts and false information in Active Directory and Entra ID.

A fundamentally different philosophy: not scoring behavior, but luring reconnaissance into a trap. It hides real privileged credentials and steers theft and lateral movement toward dead ends.

Key takeaways

  1. 01The agent decides on the device; offline is not automatically unprotected, but check Detect vs Protect mode.
  2. 02Storyline links causally related events under one StorylineID: read from the root forward and verify key steps.
  3. 03Mitigation status drives handoff priority; set Analyst Verdict and Incident Status and keep notes as you work.
  4. 04Rollback restores file content only: not exfiltrated data, not persistence, not deleted shadow copies.
  5. 05Disconnect from Network first when the attack is live, then match Kill, Quarantine, Remediate or Rollback to the damage.
  6. 06PowerQuery: src is the actor, tgt the object; STAR makes a hunt permanent, Ranger and XDR cover blind spots.

Storyline automates the correlation you would otherwise do by hand. It links events but does not prove each one malicious, it does not replace hunting the raw telemetry, and it does not choose or apply a response.

Check your understanding, question 2 of 3

Rollback restores a laptop's ransomware-encrypted files. Twelve minutes before encryption, the attacker exfiltrated copies to an external server. What does Rollback accomplish, and what not?

Rollback restores file content from local snapshots. Data already off the network stays exfiltrated, persistence is outside its scope, and the restore still works even though copies were taken earlier.

Check your understanding, question 3 of 3

An endpoint's effective policy shows Detect mode, yet the Site and Account policy pages above its 'Legacy App Servers' Group both show Protect mode. What explains this?

In the Global > Account > Site > Group hierarchy any level can override what it inherits, so a Group override wins for that Group. Exclusions do not change the mode, the agent decides on the device without the cloud, and Global does not outrank overrides.
