How engines index the web and why operators matter

Your query is answered from a stored index, never from the target server.

HACKTHESOCLesson 121OSINT search and dorking6 / 54

Three consequences of searching an index, not the web

Crawlers fetch pages, parse them and store a processed copy in an inverted index that maps terms to documents.

Unlinked files get indexed

Anything a crawler reaches without authentication can be indexed: a public-folder spreadsheet, a backup beside a web root, a staging site with no password.

    The index lags reality

    A page removed today may still be returned, snippet included, for days or weeks.

      robots.txt is only a request

      robots.txt and noindex are polite requests, not security controls. A Disallow line even advertises a sensitive path to anyone who reads it.

        The key defender point is the second one: a stale hit and a live hit look identical in the results list until you verify.
        HACKTHESOCLesson 121OSINT search and dorking7 / 54

        Operators expose the fields the engine already stores

        • Plain keyword queryRanked by relevance and popularity, which buries the long tail where accidental exposure lives.
        • OperatorsFilter on host, URL, title, body text and file type instead of scrolling ranked pages.
        • Attack surface discoveryFind what your own organization exposed, usually by accident, so you can fix it.
        • Threat intelligenceResearch malicious infrastructure, phishing kits, brand impersonation and mentions on leak or paste sites.
        Example question an operator answers: which PDF files on any subdomain of my company contain the word confidential?
        HACKTHESOCLesson 121OSINT search and dorking8 / 54
        Typing a dork sends nothing to the target. Opening a result does.

        Searching is passive collection. For anything suspected malicious, use an isolated browser or an archived copy.

        This is why search is the lowest-risk place to start, and why clicking through is a separate decision.
        HACKTHESOCLesson 121OSINT search and dorking9 / 54

        Five questions to ask about every result

        A present result is a lead, not a finding, until you have verified it.

        1. 1Is this asset actually ours?Confirm ownership before you assign anything.
        2. 2Is it live or a stale index entry?The index lags reality.
        3. 3What does it expose, and to whom?Sensitive data, and who can reach it.
        4. 4How did it get indexed?Find how it became reachable and indexable, not just the symptom.
        5. 5Who owns the fix?Every finding needs an owner.

        Engines cap results, personalize ranking and change without notice. An absent result is not proof of absence.

        Make students say these five out loud on the next lab result. It becomes the habit for the rest of the lesson.

        Each added operator narrows the result set.

        Combining rules and pitfalls

        • More operatorsOR (or pipe), * wildcard inside a phrase, and allintitle: for all terms in the title.
        • Case and quotesOR must be uppercase. Quotes give precision and stop silent synonym rewriting.
        • Retired featuresGoogle retired cache: and cached-page links in 2024. related: and + no longer behave as documented.
        • Test and splitTest an operator on a known page before trusting empty results. Split long queries.
        The common mistake: building a workflow on cache:. Point students to web archives instead (section 5).
        HACKTHESOCLesson 121OSINT search and dorking14 / 54

        Other engines see different files

        Bing

        Supports site:, filetype:, intitle:, inurl: and adds inbody:, contains: and ip: (pages hosted on an IP address). Index and ranking differ.

          DuckDuckGo

          A smaller core set: site:, filetype:, intitle:, inurl:, quotes and minus. Draws on other providers, so coverage and freshness differ.

            A clean result on one engine says little about the others. Run important queries on both.

            Different indexes see different files. This is the root of the false-negative pitfall in the last section.

            Dorking for attack-surface discovery

            Stored, reusable queries aimed at known risk patterns, scoped to your own domains.

            HACKTHESOCLesson 121OSINT search and dorking16 / 54

            Four exposure families to review on a schedule

            Defensive dorking shows what an outsider sees before a criminal does.

            Exposed documents

            Office files, PDFs and exports that were meant to be internal.

              Directory listings

              A server that lists a folder shows every file, even ones never linked.

                Login and admin portals

                Not vulnerabilities, but the inventory feeds patching and MFA coverage.

                  Misindexed config

                  Env files, config backups, logs and error pages that leak paths or credentials.

                    The GHDB, maintained by Exploit-DB, is a pattern library, not a script to run blindly.

                    GHDB groups thousands of dorks by category. Each entry tells you a class of mistake; you adapt it with site: and your own naming.
                    HACKTHESOCLesson 121OSINT search and dorking17 / 54

                    One dork per family, scoped with site:

                    Every query is anchored by site: to our own domain. Remove that anchor and you are looking at other people's data.
                    HACKTHESOCLesson 121OSINT search and dorking18 / 54

                    Triage each hit before you escalate

                    A hit proves the engine indexed something, not that it is dangerous. Ask: is it sensitive, reachable without credentials, still live?

                    Cleanup task

                    Stale index entry

                    A deleted file the engine still returns. Not dangerous, but still needs cleaning up.

                      False positive

                      Boilerplate disclaimer

                      Public marketing PDFs legitimately contain the word confidential in a footer. Read before you escalate.

                        Incident

                        Live unauthenticated backup

                        Sensitive data, reachable by anyone, still live.

                          Three axes decide severity. Walk one example of each column before moving on.

                          Pitfalls specific to dorking

                          • False positivesRead the hit before you escalate. Disclaimers match sensitive keywords.
                          • Third-party scopeWithout site: you find other organizations' data. Stop, record URL and timestamp only, do not download or share, report to the owner or a CERT.
                          • AutomationScraping violates terms and triggers CAPTCHAs. Run curated dorks manually or via an official API.
                          • AuthorizationPassive search is generally fine. Logging into a portal with default credentials without written approval is not.
                          The authorization line applies even to your own company's portal. Written approval first.

                          Infrastructure search engines: Shodan and Censys

                          They index what services answer on IPs and ports, not page text.

                          HACKTHESOCLesson 121OSINT search and dorking21 / 54

                          A library catalog versus a census of every door

                          Google

                          Indexes page text that crawlers can read: a library catalog of documents.

                            Shodan and Censys

                            Connect to IP addresses and ports and record what each door looks like. Refreshed on scan cycles.

                            • Banners and open ports
                            • TLS certificates
                            • Software versions and HTTP titles

                            Ideal for perimeter questions: which ranges expose RDP, which hosts present our certificate, which run end-of-life versions?

                            Use the street analogy. Google reads the signs in the windows; these engines record every door and the sign hanging on it.
                            HACKTHESOCLesson 121OSINT search and dorking22 / 54

                            Perimeter queries on Shodan and Censys

                            • Last Shodan query: certificate names our domain on a port other than 443
                            • Often a forgotten admin interface or an appliance published without telling security
                            • Censys field names changed between generations: read current docs
                            Study the fourth Shodan line with the class. Censys fields have changed between product generations, so never copy old queries blindly.
                            HACKTHESOCLesson 121OSINT search and dorking23 / 54
                            Every publicly trusted certificate is logged, so certificate names reveal subdomains no web page mentions.

                            Certificate transparency pivots turn a subject name into a new domain to dork.

                            Ties back to pivoting in section 7: a certificate subject becomes the next site: value.
                            HACKTHESOCLesson 121OSINT search and dorking24 / 54

                            Read infrastructure results responsibly

                            • Check the timestampScan data can be days or weeks old. The service may already be fixed or gone.
                            • Confirm ownershipShared hosting and CDNs mean an IP in the results may not be yours. Check the asset inventory.
                            • A banner is a claimVersions can be spoofed or backported. A listed version is a lead for the vulnerability team.
                            • Do not probeReading stored engine data is passive. Connecting to a stranger's device is not.
                            Treat a nine-day-old banner showing an old version as a lead to verify against inventory and your scanner, never as a confirmed vulnerability.

                            Historical and cached sources: deleted content persists

                            Removing a page from your site does not remove it from the internet.

                            HACKTHESOCLesson 121OSINT search and dorking26 / 54

                            Where deleted content lives on

                            Web archives

                            The Wayback Machine keeps timestamped snapshots by URL. On-demand services preserve a page, even a phishing page deleted later.

                              Search engine caches

                              Google retired cached links and cache: in 2024. Treat any cache as unreliable and archive what matters yourself, now.

                                Other copies

                                Social shares, forum quotes, link-shortener previews, scrapers and mirror sites often keep content after the original is gone.

                                  Threat intel angle: a domain parking a blank page today may have hosted a phishing kit last month. The snapshot shows page structure, brand targeted and form actions.
                                  HACKTHESOCLesson 121OSINT search and dorking27 / 54

                                  Wayback wildcard URLs list archived paths

                                  • The wildcard form lists every archived URL under a path
                                  • Historical directory discovery: old admin paths, retired API routes, removed files
                                  Show a live example in class if possible. Students are often surprised how much of a forgotten path is still listed.
                                  HACKTHESOCLesson 121OSINT search and dorking28 / 54
                                  Deleting is not remediation.

                                  If a file with credentials was public for six weeks, assume it was copied. Rotate the credentials; do not rely on deletion.

                                  Removal from archives and search results is a process with varying outcomes, so track the request as a ticket with an owner.
                                  HACKTHESOCLesson 121OSINT search and dorking29 / 54

                                  Archive pitfalls and evidence handling

                                  • Snapshots are partialMissing snapshots do not prove a page never existed. Scripts, images or dynamic content may be missing.
                                  • Timestamp is capture timeIt shows when the archive captured a page, not the first-exposure date.
                                  • Live links still go liveLinks inside a snapshot of a malicious site can still contact the malicious server. Use an isolated environment.
                                  • Save the evidenceExact archive URL, capture time and, where policy allows, a local copy and its hash.
                                  Evidence you cannot reproduce later is weak evidence. This sets up the research log in section 7.

                                  Code and paste search: hunting leaked secrets

                                  Bots harvest published secrets within minutes, so find them first.

                                  HACKTHESOCLesson 121OSINT search and dorking31 / 54

                                  GitHub code search scoped to your company

                                  • Lines 1-2: repositories your organization owns
                                  • Lines 3-5: leaks in repos you do not own, such as a contractor's or former intern's
                                  The last three lines matter most. A former intern's coursework can contain real endpoints.
                                  HACKTHESOCLesson 121OSINT search and dorking32 / 54

                                  Pivot terms, paste sites and high-signal patterns

                                  • Pivot termsInternal hostnames, email domain, product code names, distinctive prefixes of your own key formats.
                                  • Paste sitesPastes vanish quickly: set alerts on domain, brand and employee email format, and review hits daily.
                                  • High-signal patternsCloud access keys with fixed prefixes, private key headers, connection strings, combo lists.
                                  • False positivesPlaceholders in docs and test fixtures match real key patterns. Check entropy, format and context.
                                  Private keys show up as files named id_rsa or with a .pem extension. Combo lists are credential pairs for your email domain.
                                  HACKTHESOCLesson 121OSINT search and dorking33 / 54

                                  Handling a found secret: the order matters

                                  1. 1Record the locationURL, repo, commit hash, timestamp, and a minimal redacted note. Never paste the secret.
                                  2. 2Revoke or rotate firstTreat it as compromised from the moment it was public.
                                  3. 3Do not test itChecking is use. Ask the owner to confirm status and review logs for the exposure window.
                                  4. 4Remove the contentGit history, forks, caches and archives persist. Rewriting history is cleanup only.
                                  5. 5Find the root causeWhy was it in code? Could a pre-commit scanner or push protection have stopped it?
                                  Students tend to jump to deleting the repo. Rotation is the step that actually removes risk.
                                  HACKTHESOCLesson 121OSINT search and dorking34 / 54
                                  Do not test whether a leaked credential still works. Checking is use.

                                  It may be unauthorized or trigger alarms. If the secret belongs to another organization, do not use it, do not test it, notify the owner or a CERT.

                                  This is the exact trap in the third quiz question.

                                  Section 7 of 9

                                  Building effective queries: iterate, pivot, log

                                  Your first query is a hypothesis; the results make the second one better.

                                  HACKTHESOCLesson 121OSINT search and dorking36 / 54

                                  The search rhythm

                                  1. 1Start broadBegin with site: plus a single risk term.
                                  2. 2Narrow or loosenThousands of hits: add exclusions and file types. Zero: drop quotes or a filter.
                                  3. 3Pivot on findingsEvery hit contains new search terms.
                                  4. 4Rerun and diffKeep dorks in version control and rerun monthly. New results are new exposure.
                                  5. 5Log everythingRecord each query, engine and outcome so others can reproduce it.

                                  A zero-result query is informative only if the query would have matched a known positive.

                                  The diffing step is where the value is: a result set that did not exist last month is far more actionable than a one-off scan.
                                  HACKTHESOCLesson 121OSINT search and dorking37 / 54

                                  Every finding gives you the next query

                                  When you find...Pivot to...
                                  A new subdomainUse it as the next site: value
                                  Document author or last-modified-byEmployee names and username formats
                                  Internal hostname or project code nameAn exact-phrase query on code and paste sites
                                  Certificate subject name on an infra engineA new domain to dork
                                  An IP address found anywhereAn org: or net: query on Shodan or Censys
                                  Walk the chain once: subdomain, certificate, IP, repository. That is how a small start becomes a full picture.
                                  HACKTHESOCLesson 121OSINT search and dorking38 / 54

                                  The research log entry

                                  • Exact query string and UTC time make the work reproducible
                                  • Also your defense if someone asks why you accessed something

                                  HACKTHESOCLesson 121OSINT search and dorking39 / 54

                                  Practical pitfalls

                                  Rate limits

                                  Slow down on a CAPTCHA. Rotating proxies to evade it violates terms and signals automation.

                                    Result drift

                                    Rankings and indexes change. Screenshot or save evidence at discovery time.

                                      Confirmation bias

                                      Vary vocabulary: credentials, passwd, secret, token, and non-English equivalents.

                                        Scope creep

                                        Write down domains, IP ranges and brand terms first. Stop when results leave them.

                                          Scope creep is the one that turns research into a legal problem, so it is flagged red.

                                          Legal and ethical boundaries, and common pitfalls

                                          Defender and criminal differ only in intent, authorization and conduct.

                                          HACKTHESOCLesson 121OSINT search and dorking46 / 54

                                          Three boundaries to anchor your practice

                                          Scope and authorization

                                          Searching public indexes is generally lawful, but accessing, downloading or using what you find may not be. Confirm policy and contract cover the work.

                                            Other people's data

                                            Dorks sometimes surface third-party exposure. Behave the same way every time, as the next slide shows.

                                              Your own team's data

                                              A log that pastes live credentials or personal records into a shared ticket creates a second exposure. Redact and limit access.

                                                For threat-intel work on malicious infrastructure, follow the team's safe handling procedure: isolated browser, no forms, archived copies.
                                                HACKTHESOCLesson 121OSINT search and dorking47 / 54

                                                Found someone else's data: what to do

                                                1. 1StopDo not browse further.
                                                2. 2Keep the minimum recordLocation and time only. Do not download, store or forward the content.
                                                3. 3Report to the ownerUse security.txt or a disclosure policy, or contact a national CERT.
                                                4. 4Never use or test itNot credentials, tokens or personal data, not even to confirm.
                                                This is the first quiz question in procedural form.

                                                Key takeaways

                                                1. 01Engines answer from a lagging index, so a hit is a lead to verify, and no hit is not proof of absence.
                                                2. 02Scope operators like site:, filetype:, intitle: and inurl: to your own domains and review four exposure families on a schedule.
                                                3. 03Shodan and Censys show what services answer on IPs and ports; a banner is a claim to verify, never to probe.
