Why a SOC analyzes images

Four recurring situations, and the two questions to ask every time.

HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT6 / 52

Four reasons images land on a SOC desk

Phishing and fraud lures

Fake-recruiter or romance profile photos. A stolen photo means a synthetic persona, often reused under several names.

    Fake or inflated leak claims

    Breach 'proof' screenshots are often recycled, from a vendor demo, or edited. Weigh the image; only telemetry proves a breach.

      Threat-actor media

      Backgrounds, window views and signage can narrow a location. Embedded timestamps can contradict a claimed timeline.

        Your own exposure

        Marketing, event and job-ad photos can leak GPS, serials, badges and screens. Attackers check this in recon, so check first.

          The first three are inbound claims to test. The fourth turns the same skills on your own organization before an attacker does.

          Two independent questions on every image

          The file and the picture are separate evidence streams.

          • What does the file say?Metadata, structure and compression history.
          • What does the picture show?Content that can be placed in space and time.
          • When the streams agreeConfidence rises.
          • When they disagreeThis is the most valuable finding of all.
          A photo whose metadata says one place and whose skyline says another is worth more than either clue alone.
          HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT8 / 52
          Every technique here produces leads and probabilities, not proof.

          A stripped file, a clean error-level scan and a reverse search with no hits each prove nothing. Say 'consistent with', 'inconsistent with' or 'not determinable'.

          Analysts who use graded language are right far more often than those who state verdicts. Make students say the three phrases aloud.

          Section 2 of 9

          Image metadata: EXIF, IPTC and XMP

          Data about the image stored inside the file, like the luggage tag on a suitcase.

          HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT10 / 52

          Three metadata families and where they live

          • EXIFWritten by the camera or phone, built on the TIFF tag structure.
          • IPTCEditorial fields: caption, creator, copyright, keywords, added by photo software or news desks.
          • XMPAn XML packet recording creator tool, edit history and rights information.
          • Storage in a JPEGEXIF sits in an APP1 segment near the start, with sub-tables for GPS and maker-specific notes.
          • PNG filesNormally no camera EXIF (an eXIf chunk is possible), so a true screenshot rarely shows a camera model.
          The luggage tag describes the suitcase without being the suitcase. A screenshot often matches a known display resolution instead of a camera.
          HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT11 / 52

          Reading metadata with ExifTool

          ExifTool is the standard tool and reads nearly every format.

          • -a -G1 -s: all tags incl. duplicates, labelled with the block they came from
          • -n -gps:all: raw decimal GPS, avoiding degree-minute formatting errors
          • -csv -r: whole folder for bulk review; -ee: per-sample data in video
          Show the group name from -G1: it tells you whether a tag came from EXIF, XMP or another block, which matters when hunting inconsistencies.
          HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT12 / 52

          The fields worth reading, and how each one misleads

          FieldWhat it tells youTypical pitfall
          Make, ModelCapture deviceEasily edited; absent after most platform uploads
          DateTimeOriginalLocal time of shutter pressNo time zone unless OffsetTimeOriginal exists
          CreateDate, ModifyDateFile creation and last editA later ModifyDate suggests editing, not proof
          GPSLatitude, GPSLongitudeWhere the device thought it wasCan be absent, spoofed, or from a stale fix
          GPSDateStamp, GPSTimeStampUTC time from satellitesCompare with DateTimeOriginal to spot clock tampering
          Software, CreatorToolEditor or firmwareAn editor present means a re-save happened
          SerialNumber, LensModelDevice-level identifiersLinks multiple images to one camera
          Every row has a catch. The habit to teach: for each field ask who could have written or edited it, and what else in the file should agree.
          HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT13 / 52

          Check internal consistency before trusting any value

          Model versus lens

          A Model that does not support the stated lens is a stronger signal than any single value.

            Thumbnail versus image

            ThumbnailImage may show the pre-edit picture. A thumbnail that differs from the main image is a flag.

              GPS time versus local time

              An impossible offset between the two points to clock tampering. GPSImgDirection can confirm the lens heading.

                Forgers fix the obvious fields and forget the quiet ones. Disagreement between fields beats any one value.

                Section 3 of 9

                Why platforms strip metadata

                A missing tag set is the normal state of a copy found on social media.

                HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT15 / 52
                Stripping is the norm, not a signal.

                Most large services re-encode uploads and remove EXIF to save bytes and protect users' home coordinates. An image with no EXIF tells you almost nothing.

                Students tend to read missing metadata as suspicious. Correct that early: the same image as an original attachment may be rich in metadata.
                HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT16 / 52

                The closer to the source, the richer the metadata

                Copy from a social post

                Re-encoded, tags removed or partly kept. Behavior differs between web upload, mobile app and send-as-file in messengers.

                  Original file

                  Email attachments, share links, forum uploads and extortion sites often serve the file unmodified.

                  • Hash it before opening or converting
                  • Work on copies only
                  • Re-saving or exporting destroys evidence

                  Always ask: what is the closest copy to the source I can obtain?

                  Also ask who benefits if tags were removed or added. Tell the class that a screenshot of an image is the worst copy of all.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT17 / 52

                  Auditing your own organization's images

                  Run it the way an outsider would.

                  1. 1Inventory public image sourcesWebsite media, press kit, job ads, galleries, social accounts, repos, docs sites.
                  2. 2Download originals as an outsiderRun exiftool recursively with -if '$GPSLatitude' -filename to list coordinate files.
                  3. 3List identifying tagsArtist, Copyright, SerialNumber, Software, and internal paths or usernames in XMP history.
                  4. 4Fix the pipeline, not the fileStrip with exiftool -all= or the CMS image processor, then re-test.
                  5. 5Remember that content survivesBadges, monitors, whiteboards, diagrams and window views are not metadata.

                  Phones record GPS by default: a home-office prototype photo can reveal a home address.

                  Stress step 4: fixing files one by one never scales, and step 5: stripping removes tags but not what the picture shows.

                  Reverse image search: origin and reuse

                  Ask a very large library: where have I seen this before?

                  HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT19 / 52

                  What reverse search is used for in a SOC

                  • Finding first appearanceWhere an image was first seen online.
                  • Stolen profile photosProve a persona's photo was lifted from another person.
                  • Recycled leak screenshotsSpot a screenshot reused from an older incident.
                  • Stock or vendor-demo imagesIdentify images passed off as proof.
                  The result turns a vague suspicion about a recruiter or a leak into a documented reuse chain.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT20 / 52

                  Four engines, four different indexes

                  EngineStrengthsWeaknessesBest for
                  Google Lens / ImagesHuge index; object and text recognitionSimilar not exact matches; weak on obscure forumsFirst pass, products, text in images
                  Yandex ImagesStrong similarity; landmarks; Eastern Europe sitesResults change over time; regional biasLocating scenes, persona reuse on regional sites
                  TinEyeExact and modified-copy matches; sort by oldest crawlSmaller index, little semantic understandingEarliest known appearance
                  Bing Visual SearchDecent object cropping and shopping matchesSmaller coverage of social sitesCross-checking and cropped regions

                  No single index covers the web, so use several engines.

                  TinEye is the one to reach for when the question is who had it first. Yandex is the one for scenes and regional persona reuse.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT21 / 52

                  Technique: get more from every search

                  1. 1Crop to the interesting regionBackground clutter lowers match quality.
                  2. 2Try flipped versionsMirrored reposts are common.
                  3. 3Search several sub-regionsThe face region, a distinctive building, a logo.
                  4. 4Use the best copySearch the highest-resolution copy you have.
                  5. 5Record every matchURL, crawl date and a hash or screenshot, since pages disappear.
                  Step 5 is the one beginners skip. A match you cannot show again next week is not evidence.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT22 / 52

                  Four pitfalls of reverse search

                  No hits is not original

                  It means absent from the indexed set. Private apps, closed groups and paywalls are not indexed.

                    First match is not creation

                    The first match date is when the engine first saw the image, not when it was made.

                      A clean face may be new

                      A clean result for a face may mean an AI-generated persona, not a genuine one.

                        Face search needs counsel

                        Face matching raises biometric-privacy issues and may be unlawful for an employer. Prefer image-level matching.

                          No hits goes in the report as inconclusive. Never use that gap to declare the person real or the image generated.

                          Section 5 of 9

                          Geolocation from image content

                          Place a photo on the map when metadata is missing or untrusted.

                          HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT24 / 52

                          Four steps from country to street

                          Work coarse to fine, like a detective narrowing a suspect list.

                          1. 1Country and regionText, driving side, road markings, plates and utilities.
                          2. 2EnvironmentVegetation, terrain, climate, architecture and sun direction.
                          3. 3Anchor featuresSomething unique, confirmed on satellite and street-level imagery.
                          4. 4ValidateSeveral independent features match and no major element contradicts.
                          Hold more than one hypothesis until evidence eliminates it. A location is established only by independent matches, not by one striking clue.
                          HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT25 / 52

                          Step 1 clues: what narrows country and region

                          Text and formats

                          Language and writing systems on signs; how phone numbers and addresses are formatted.

                            Driving side

                            Traffic direction and the position of the driver eliminate large parts of the world.

                              Road markings

                              Center line color (yellow versus white), edge lines, dashed patterns and bollard shapes.

                                Plates and utilities

                                Plate shape, color and layout, flags or regional codes; poles, power lines and sockets.

                                  Each clue removes possibilities rather than naming a place. Ask which clues are mutually independent.
                                  HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT26 / 52

                                  Steps 2 to 4 in practice

                                  • EnvironmentSoil color, snow, dust, palm trees and rice paddies narrow latitude; roofs and shutters cluster regionally.
                                  • Anchor featuresA distinctive tower, bridge, skyline, ridge line or shop sign. Search business names in map services.
                                  • Match the vantageIf the camera stood at A looking at B, the skyline and roads must line up from there.
                                  • Check imagery datesSatellite and street-level imagery may be years old, and the scene may have changed.
                                  The vantage test is powerful: a wrong location fails to line up when you stand where the camera stood.
                                  HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT27 / 52
                                  Confirmation bias is the main error.

                                  Staged scenes, stock backgrounds and crops mislead, and a bilingual sign proves little. Ask what you would expect to see in the opposite direction.

                                  Also ask whether a second analyst could reach the same place without your notes. If not, the finding is not yet robust.

                                  Section 6 of 9

                                  Chronolocation: when a photo was taken

                                  A shadow is a clock whose face is the ground.

                                  HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT29 / 52

                                  From shadow to sun elevation

                                  • Direction gives where the sun is horizontally; length gives how high
                                  • Estimate heights from the scene: doors about 2 m, road signs, rail gauge, cars
                                  Walk the arithmetic on the board. The result is an elevation angle, not a time of day: it needs a location and a sun path to become a time.
                                  HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT30 / 52

                                  Testing a claim with SunCalc

                                  SunCalc shows sun azimuth and elevation for a place and date, plus sunrise and sunset lines.

                                  1. 1Fix a candidate locationThe sun path means nothing without a place.
                                  2. 2If the date is knownFind the time when elevation and direction match your measurement.
                                  3. 3If the time is claimedCheck whether the shadow geometry is consistent with that date.
                                  4. 4Look for falsificationThe claim fails if the angle cannot occur there, e.g. a too-short shadow on a high-latitude winter morning.
                                  Falsifying is the strongest outcome: a shadow that cannot exist on the claimed date is evidence against the claim.
                                  HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT31 / 52

                                  Other clues that bracket the date and time

                                  ClueWhat to compare
                                  WeatherRain, fog, snow, cloud, wet pavement against archives from the nearest station
                                  SeasonLeaf cover, crops, snow, holiday decorations, clothing
                                  Clocks and screensStation and dashboard clocks, shop hours, phone status bars, dated event posters
                                  Construction stateA building site or road project dated by news or imagery history gives a bracket
                                  ReflectionsMoon phase, if visible, constrains the date
                                  Weather archives come from national meteorological services. Sports fixtures and concerts have published dates, which makes posters useful.
                                  HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT32 / 52

                                  Four ways chronolocation goes wrong

                                  Slopes and tilt

                                  Shadows on sloped ground are distorted; camera tilt and lens distortion skew angles.

                                    Building shadows

                                    Hard to measure without a base reference.

                                      Time zones

                                      Daylight saving time and time zones cause one-hour errors when converting clock readings.

                                        Twin dates

                                        The same sun elevation occurs twice a year at a given time of day, so a second clue must choose.

                                          State your assumptions (object height, flat ground) and the error range.

                                          Ask: is the chosen date the only one consistent with all the clues? If not, say which dates remain.

                                          Section 7 of 9

                                          Detecting manipulation and tool limits

                                          Look for physical inconsistencies, not a magic stamp.

                                          HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT34 / 52

                                          Visual tells come first

                                          • Lighting and shadowsInconsistent lighting direction between objects; shadows that disagree.
                                          • EdgesHalos, or unusually sharp or blurred borders.
                                          • Repeated textureRepeated patterns indicate cloning.
                                          • Perspective and reflectionsPerspective that does not converge; reflections that do not match.
                                          • TextMismatched fonts, spacing or anti-aliasing.
                                          • Screenshot UICompare icons, fonts, buttons, strings and scroll bars with the real interface.

                                          These tells are more reliable than automated filters.

                                          For screenshots, compare against the real interface at the claimed version and date, including timestamp formats.
                                          HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT35 / 52

                                          ELA is a prompt to look closer, never a verdict

                                          Error level analysis re-saves a JPEG at a known quality and shows the difference.

                                          CaveatEffect
                                          High-contrast edges and fine texturesAppear bright with no editing at all
                                          Repeated re-compression by platformsFlattens differences, so a signal may vanish
                                          An edit followed by a uniform re-saveCan hide traces
                                          Resized images and screenshotsPoor candidates entirely
                                          The output is a pictureInvites over-interpretation; two people read it differently

                                          Noise residuals, quantization comparison and clone detection have the same status.

                                          Tie this to the quiz: bright edges and a detailed tree in an ELA image are exactly what an unedited photo looks like.
                                          HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT36 / 52

                                          Structural checks on the file

                                          Metadata can be forged, but forgers often slip in less visible fields.

                                          1. 1Compare the thumbnailDoes the embedded thumbnail match the main image?
                                          2. 2Read the Software tagDoes it show an editor?
                                          3. 3Compare the datesIs ModifyDate later than DateTimeOriginal?
                                          4. 4Check quantization tablesDo they match the claimed camera?
                                          Each check is a lead. Combine them with the visual tells before drawing a conclusion.
                                          HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT37 / 52

                                          Three outcomes, and most cases end as the third

                                          Evidence of manipulation

                                          Physical inconsistencies that several independent checks support.

                                            Evidence of authenticity

                                            Consistent content, structure and metadata, with provenance if available.

                                              Most real cases

                                              Inconclusive

                                              Say so, and say what extra data, such as the original file or the sender's account history, would change the assessment.

                                                AI tells (hands, text, background geometry) fade as models improve; content credentials help when present, their absence proves nothing.

                                                Teach students that an honest inconclusive is a professional result, not a failure.

                                                Section 8 of 9

                                                Safe handling, ethics and legal limits

                                                Treat actor media as untrusted input and keep the work proportionate.

                                                HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT39 / 52

                                                Handling actor media safely

                                                1. 1IsolateDownload in an isolated analysis environment, not your daily workstation.
                                                2. 2Hash on receiptSHA-256 the file and keep the original read-only.
                                                3. 3Work on copiesImages can exploit parsers in viewers and editors.
                                                4. 4Stay off their infrastructureNo browsing from the corporate network or logged-in accounts; prefer archived copies.
                                                5. 5Log everythingSource URL, retrieval time, tool versions and every transformation applied.

                                                Do not comment, like or download through accounts that identify you.

                                                Links to 'proof' hosted by an actor can track your visit. Chain of custody matters if the case goes to law enforcement, legal or insurers.
                                                HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT40 / 52

                                                Proportionate versus out of bounds

                                                Proportionate

                                                A legitimate defensive purpose, tied to the incident, using the minimum necessary information.

                                                • Share findings through incident and legal channels
                                                • Use graded language and state assumptions
                                                • Get legal advice before any facial recognition

                                                Out of bounds

                                                Anything beyond the incident, or beyond what is publicly available.

                                                • Geolocating third parties, such as family in a photo
                                                • Publishing precise locations or identities
                                                • Accessing private accounts or bypassing authentication
                                                • Copying or sharing illegal content: stop and escalate
                                                Geolocation skills can identify a private home. Facial recognition is regulated in many jurisdictions, for example under European data-protection law.
                                                HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT41 / 52
                                                Misidentification has real victims.

                                                Wrongly attributing a photo to a person or place can harm them, and online communities have repeatedly misidentified suspects. Invite review.

                                                This is why graded language is an ethical requirement as well as an accuracy one.
                                                HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT42 / 52

                                                Questions to ask before you start

                                                • AuthorityDo I have authority and a defensive purpose?
                                                • MinimumWhat is the minimum I need to learn?
                                                • HarmWho could be harmed if I am wrong?
                                                • StorageWhere will the findings be stored, and who can see them?
                                                Make these a checklist at the top of the ticket, answered before any searching begins.

                                                Worked example: verifying a breach-proof post

                                                A fictional extortion post against Northwind Freight, with a PNG screenshot and a JPEG photo.

                                                HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT44 / 52

                                                Six steps from two files to a written assessment

                                                Claim: an 'internal admin console' screenshot, and a photo 'taken at the HQ data room last week'.

                                                1. 1PreserveIsolated download, SHA-256 hashes, source URL and retrieval time; work on copies.
                                                2. 2MetadataRun exiftool -a -G1 -s on each file.
                                                3. 3Screenshot contentCompare the UI with the real console and reverse-search crops.
                                                4. 4Photo geolocationTram wires, a bilingual sign and a brick tower, matched through maps.
                                                5. 5Photo chronolocationShadow geometry and SunCalc for the identified city, plus weather records.
                                                6. 6Write the assessmentFindings with confidence levels, assumptions and error ranges.
                                                Leadership wants to know how credible the claim is before a public statement. Every earlier section of the lesson appears in these six steps.
                                                HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT45 / 52

                                                Step 2: what the metadata showed

                                                FileFindingReading
                                                PNG screenshotNo camera tags; pixel size matches a common laptop resolutionExpected for a screenshot
                                                JPEG photoPhone Make and Model; DateTimeOriginal five months ago; OffsetTimeOriginal +02:00The date predates the 'last week' claim
                                                JPEG GPSNo GPS blockMeans little: location may be off or removed
                                                JPEG thumbnailThumbnailImage shows the same sceneNo thumbnail mismatch

                                                Absence of GPS means little; the date is the useful metadata finding.

                                                Note what the metadata cannot do: it does not settle whether the breach happened. It only adds a date and rules out an obvious thumbnail edit.
                                                HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT46 / 52

                                                Steps 3 to 5: what the content showed

                                                • Screenshot UIMatches a vendor's public demo; toolbar icons are from an older version than Northwind runs; sample-data style records.
                                                • Reverse searchA crop of the table header matches a vendor documentation page crawled two years earlier.
                                                • Photo locationTram wires, bilingual sign and brick tower place it in a city on a different continent from HQ.
                                                • Photo dateA 1.8 m frame casting a 2.9 m shadow gives about 32 degrees; SunCalc fits mid-morning in spring, the EXIF date, not 'last week'.

                                                Historical weather for that date shows clear skies, matching the picture.

                                                Each finding rests on independent evidence. The shadow points north-west, so the sun is south-east, in the morning.
                                                HACKTHESOCLesson 122Image and geolocation OSINT47 / 52

                                                Step 6: the written assessment

                                                ItemFindingConfidence
                                                ScreenshotMatches a public vendor demo, not Northwind's systemHigh
                                                Photo locationNot the HQ; different cityMedium-high
                                                Photo dateAbout five months before the claimMedium
                                                Breach itselfNot determinable from these filesRequires internal telemetry

                                                Next: pull authentication logs, data-access records and egress telemetry, and check whether any claimed record exists.

                                                The images weaken the claim's credibility but cannot close the case. The last row is the one leadership must hear clearly.

                                                Key takeaways

                                                1. 01Test every image as a claim: ask what the file says and what the picture shows, then compare the two streams.
                                                2. 02Missing metadata is normal after platform upload; seek the closest copy to the source and hash it before opening.
                                                3. 03Use several reverse-search engines and crops; no hits is a coverage gap, not proof of originality.
                                                4. 04Geolocate coarse to fine, and use shadow geometry with SunCalc to test a claimed time and place.
                                                5. 05Treat ELA and other filters as prompts, not verdicts; most manipulation cases end as inconclusive.
                                                6. 06Work defensively and proportionately: isolated handling, legal advice for face matching, graded language.
