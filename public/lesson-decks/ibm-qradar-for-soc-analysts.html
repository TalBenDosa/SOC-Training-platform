The Offenses tab and the Offense Summary

Where every shift begins: a queue already triaged and ranked.

HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts6 / 59

The queue is ranked for you, highest Magnitude first

  • Offenses tabThe analyst queue, listed highest Magnitude first. Work the top of the list first.
  • No auto refreshUnlike Log Activity and Network Activity, it does not refresh itself. Watch the timer, refresh manually.
  • All / My OffensesThe global list, and what is assigned to you.
  • By Source IPGroups offenses so you spot one attacker hitting many targets.
  • By Destination IPOne asset under attack from many places. Also By Category and By Network.
  • View OffensesFilters by time; the Current Search Parameters pane clears filters.
The left navigation does not show different offenses, it reframes the same offenses by different keys. That reframing is a fast way to see campaign shape.

The first row says how important QRadar thinks it is

Double-click an offense to open the Offense Summary.

FieldWhat it means
MagnitudeRelative importance, calculated from relevance, severity and credibility
StatusActive (no icon), dormant, inactive, or hidden or protected flags
RelevanceImportance of the destination, from the network and asset weights
SeverityThe threat the attack poses relative to how prepared the destination is
CredibilityIntegrity of the offense, set by the log source, rising as more sources agree
The top panel below this row adds Description, Offense Type, Event/Flow count with clickable links, Source and Destination IPs, Start, Duration and Network(s).

Reading an Offense Summary top to bottom

  • Preceded by in the Description means the offense is chained
  • Offense Type is set by the rule and decides what the Source Summary shows

Bottom panel also has Top 5 Source IPs, Destination IPs, Log Sources, Last 10 Events and Flows, and Annotations, the text rules attach to explain why it is a threat. The Chained column flags a destination that is also the source of another offense.

Magnitude: severity, credibility and relevance

The single number that orders your queue, and the three inputs behind it.

HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts10 / 59

Three inputs, each scored 0 to 10

InputQuestion it answersWhere it comes from
SeverityHow damaging is this activity if it is real?The event's low-level category. DoS near 10, a routine login failure around 3
CredibilityHow confident are we that it is real?Set per log source by the admin; rises when multiple sources report the same thing
RelevanceHow much do we care about the target?The weight assigned to the network and the asset

The Magistrate combines them, with factors such as event count and traffic context. The weighting is proprietary; relevance weighs most.

The Magnitude graph on the Offense Summary shows the breakdown when you hover or click it.
HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts11 / 59

Same rule, same count, different Magnitude

  • Category drives event severity; the rule plus the asset model drive Magnitude
  • Low credibility on a critical asset may be a noisy rule on an important box
A SOC tunes Magnitude by maintaining the network hierarchy and asset model. A rule's response page sets Severity, Credibility and Relevance. A lower Magnitude with credibility 9 across several sources may be the real incident.

The offense workflow

Assign, add notes, protect, follow up, close with a reason.

HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts13 / 59

Every action lives on the Actions list

QRadar adds an icon to the Flag column when you act. Hold Ctrl to act on several offenses.

ActionWhat it doesWhen you use it
AssignSets the owner; assigning to yourself moves it into My OffensesThe moment you start, so two analysts do not both pick it up
Add NoteTimestamped note, up to 2000 characters, cannot be edited or deletedRecord every pivot and finding; shows in Last 5 Notes
Protect OffensePrevents removal when the retention period elapsesA confirmed incident you must keep as evidence
Follow upFlags it for later attention without closingWaiting on another team or more data
HideRemoves it from the lists; a search including hidden still finds itKnown, benign, not worth a close reason yet
CloseEnds the offense with a reasonThe verdict is reached
Notes cannot be edited or deleted, so write them as you would want an auditor to read them.
HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts14 / 59

States, the clock, and a closed offense that recurs

  • Active means the rule is still adding events
  • Default offense retention period is 30 days
  • A recurring problem shows up as a fresh item, not a reopened one
Students often expect a closed offense to reopen. It does not, which is why the closing note on the old one matters when the new one appears.
HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts15 / 59

Close with a reason a stranger could re-derive

  • Reason for ClosingDefaults: False-Positive, Tuned, Non-Issue, Policy Violation.
  • Custom reasonsAn admin adds them under Admin > Custom Offense Close Reasons.
  • Closing noteState the verdict and the evidence. The next analyst or an auditor reads it.
  • Repeated FP closesA signal to tune the rule, often with the False Positive button in Log Activity.

Assign first, note as you go. An offense with no owner and no notes is invisible work.

If one rule keeps closing as False-Positive, Tuned, closing by hand is treating the symptom.

Offense chaining and the offense index

Why one offense keeps growing for hours instead of spawning hundreds.

HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts17 / 59

The offense index: one offense per key

  • Index offense based onA field on the rule's response page. Events and flows with a matching value join the same offense.
  • Index by Source IPEvery event from 198.51.100.23 lands in one offense: one attacker, one incident.
  • Index by UsernameFollow a compromised account wherever it goes.
  • Custom propertyIndex on a non-normalised value, e.g. a file's MD5, so every host seeing it rolls into one outbreak offense.
Custom properties can be built from a regex, a calculation or an AQL expression. When an offense seems to grow forever, it is doing its job.
HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts18 / 59
A null index value creates no offense, even with Ensure the detected event is part of an offense ticked.

A rule indexed on hostname silently creates nothing for events whose hostname is empty, although every test matched.

When a rule you expected to fire produced nothing, check the index field for nulls before you blame the rule tests.
HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts19 / 59

Chaining: offenses that tell one story

  • Read the Description: a sequence of the rules that contributed
  • Host was scanned, then scanned others, then talked to C2
  • Chained column: a destination IP that became the source of another offense
Chaining links related offenses so you review fewer of them. The Chained column is how you watch an attacker move from victim to victim.

Log Activity: filtering, grouping, event details

Narrowing thousands of normalised events to the handful that matter, often without AQL.

HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts21 / 59

The View list sets the window

Real Time (streaming)

Events as they arrive, the last 50. No grouped views. Pause it (then it shows the last 1000) before you can double-click an event.

Last Interval

Auto refresh, about every 60 seconds.

Fixed or custom range

Start Time and End Time fields. The usual choice when an offense sends you looking.

Students try to open an event from the live stream and nothing happens. Pause first.
HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts22 / 59

Filter and group without writing AQL

  • Quick FilterFree-text search of the payloads: type an IP or a username.
  • Add FilterParameter plus operator, e.g. Source IP Equals 198.51.100.23.
  • Right-click > Filter onAdd any value in the results; More options gives DNS, WHOIS, Asset Profile, Search Events, Search Flows.
  • Display listGroup by Username, Low Level Category, Event Name, Source IP, Log Source, Custom Rule and more.

Grouping with the Display list is the point-and-click equivalent of a GROUP BY.

Group by Username to see which account has the most failures; group by Low Level Category to see the shape of an unfamiliar log source.
HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts23 / 59

Three time fields that are easy to confuse

  • A large Log Source Time to Start Time gap: delayed or misclocked source
  • Toolbar: Offense, Map Event, False Positive, Extract Property
The gap matters when you reconstruct a timeline. Habit: broad window and Quick Filter, group to find the dominant value, right-click to pivot, payload only when a normalised field is missing.

Network Activity and flows

Records of network conversations, which exist even when nothing wrote a log.

HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts25 / 59

Flows give you visibility events miss

  • A flow without a logTwo hosts talk and neither logs it: the flow still records the conversation.
  • Where they come fromA Flow Collector (QFlow) reads a tap or SPAN port; a Flow Processor stores them in Ariel.
  • Fields you querysourceip, destinationip, ports, protocolid, applicationid, sourcebytes, destinationbytes.
  • One session, many rowsLonger than a minute: one record per one-minute interval, same First Packet Time.
Flows answer the exfiltration question: how much data left and to where. Remind them a single long session is not one row when they count flows.
HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts26 / 59

Superflows: QRadar already spotted the shape

Many flows matching a common attack shape are bundled and count as one flow against the licence.

SuperflowShapeWhat it suggests
Type AOne source to many destinations, same portA network scan
Type BMany sources to one destinationA distributed denial of service
Type COne source and one destination, many portsA port scan
Seeing a superflow on the Network Activity tab is itself a finding.
HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts27 / 59

What is this host sending outside, and how much?

  • 1440 one-minute records to one external IP: about one per minute, steady
  • Steady low-jitter traffic or a sourcebytes spike: answers events cannot give
When an offense centres on a host, pivot here to ask what it talks to externally and how much it sends.

Section 7 of 12

AQL fundamentals: clauses, tables, time

The Ariel Query Language reads like SQL, with a fixed clause order and a trailing time clause.

HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts29 / 59

The clause order is fixed

Run it from Log Activity or Network Activity: Search, then Advanced Search.

  1. 1SELECT ... FROMColumns and functions, from events, flows or simarc. One table per statement.
  2. 2WHEREFilters individual rows before grouping.
  3. 3GROUP BY ... HAVINGHAVING filters the grouped result afterwards.
  4. 4ORDER BY ... LIMITSort ASC or DESC, then cap the rows.
  5. 5Time clauseLAST n MINUTES | HOURS | DAYS, or START ... STOP ....
simarc is aggregated data most analysts rarely touch. Events are device logs, flows are network conversations.
HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts30 / 59

WHERE filters rows, HAVING filters groups

  • failures only exists after grouping, so the threshold goes in HAVING
  • With START and STOP, LIMIT comes before them
STOP is optional and defaults to now; the window includes the start second up to just before the stop second. Sort on a simple time field like starttime, not a formatted one, because formatting the sort key slows the search.
HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts31 / 59
No time clause means QRadar searches only the last 5 minutes.

A common reason a hunt seems to return nothing. Always name the table, always add a time clause, keep the WHERE tight.

A query scoped to one IP over LAST 6 HOURS returns fast. The same query with no WHERE and no time clause is slow and silently limited to five minutes.

AQL functions, operators, custom properties

Turning numeric ids into names, testing membership, and reaching unparsed data.

HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts33 / 59

The functions you reach for daily

FunctionWhat it returns
QIDNAME(qid)The event's human name from its QID (QIDDESCRIPTION for the longer text)
LOGSOURCENAME(logsourceid)The log source (device) name; LOGSOURCETYPENAME(devicetype) gives the DSM type
CATEGORYNAME(category)Low-level category name; pass highlevelcategory for the high-level one
DATEFORMAT(starttime,'yyyy-MM-dd HH:mm:ss')A readable timestamp
INCIDR('10.0.0.0/8', sourceip)True if the IP is in the subnet: internal vs external

UTF8(payload)The raw payload as text, for hunting. Also PROTOCOLNAME, UNIQUECOUNT
QRadar stores many things as numeric ids, so these functions are what make output readable.
HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts34 / 59

Operators and the quoting rule that catches everyone

String matching

Plus =, <>, <, >, <=, >=.

  • LIKE: case-sensitive, % and _
  • ILIKE: case-insensitive
  • MATCHES / IMATCHES: regex
  • IN, BETWEEN, IS NULL

TEXT SEARCH

Fast full-text payload search, but it must be the first condition in WHERE and combines only with AND.

Quotes

Single quotes for values: sourceip = '198.51.100.23'. Double quotes for a property name with spaces or an alias: "MA Threat Severity".

Double quotes are also for an alias referenced in WHERE, GROUP BY or ORDER BY, e.g. ORDER BY "Event Count" DESC.
HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts35 / 59

Readable history, watchlists and payload hunts

Three habits in one slide: decode ids, test watchlists, hunt raw text. Point out TEXT SEARCH is first and joined with AND.
HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts36 / 59

Custom Event Properties reach unparsed data

Vendors send extra data that DSMs do not normalise; it sits only in the raw payload.

  • Extraction-basedA regex, JSON, LEEF, CEF, name-value or XML pattern against the payload.
  • Calculation-basedArithmetic on numeric fields.
  • AQL-basedCombines other properties with AQL functions.
  • Enable and parse itTick Enable for use in Rules, Forwarding Profiles and Search Indexing before rules or indexing can use it.

Create one from the event details with Extract Property. Events with no matching DSM show category Stored and empty sourceip.

Empty normalised fields are the visible symptom of a missing DSM or an unparsed CEP. A property name with spaces must be double-quoted in AQL.

Section 9 of 12

DSMs, log sources, QIDs and categories

How QRadar makes multi-vendor data uniform, and why a new source sometimes shows nothing.

HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts38 / 59

Normalisation makes cross-vendor AQL possible

Device Support Module

DSM

A parser for one vendor's format, running at the Event Collector. Whatever the vendor called it, after the DSM it is sourceip, username.

QRadar Identifier

QID

A number for a kind of event, vendor-independent. Derived from the DSM's Event ID plus Event Category.

The symptom

Missing DSM

Raw payloads, low-level category Stored, empty normalised fields. Flows use a separate pipeline, so the segment can still appear.

That is why one AQL query can span Cisco, Windows and a firewall at once. When events land as unknown, Map Event from the event details assigns the right category by hand.
HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts39 / 59

Low-level categories carry the base severity

Each QID maps to a low-level category, which rolls up into one of about 21 high-level categories.

Low-level categoryCategory IDBase severity
Host Login Succeeded30021
Host Login Failed30033
Admin Login Successful30141
SSH Login Failed30222
Brute Force Login20168

High-level examples: Recon 1000, DoS 2000, Authentication 3000, Access 4000, Exploit 5000, Malware 6000.

This is where an event's severity comes from, so it is one of the three inputs to Magnitude. Others include Suspicious Activity 7000, System 8000, Policy 9000 and Unknown 10000.
HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts40 / 59

Readable categories, and finding unparsed sources

  • Wrap numeric codes in QIDNAME and CATEGORYNAME
  • Nothing in AQL but Network Activity sees the segment: suspect the DSM
When events sit as Unknown or Stored, Map Event or build a CEP so they categorise and normalise correctly.

Section 10 of 12

Custom rules, building blocks and the CRE

The engine that creates your queue, and how to shape it.

HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts42 / 59

How the Custom Rule Engine fills the queue

  1. 1Normalised events and flowsEvery one is tested against the rules in real time.
  2. 2Building blocks evaluated firstReusable test groups referenced by the rules.
  3. 3Rule tests pass?If no, the data is still stored in Ariel and searchable.
  4. 4Rule response runsSet severity, credibility, relevance; add to a reference set.
  5. 5Create or update an offenseIt lands in the Offenses queue, ranked by Magnitude.
Learning to read and tune rules turns you from a consumer of the queue into someone who shapes it.
HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts43 / 59

Rule types and tests in the Rule Wizard

  • Four rule typesEvent, flow, common (both) and offense rules. The first three create offenses; offense rules commonly notify.
  • TestsA stack evaluated line by line, picked from the Test Group list, e.g. at least N events with the same Source IP in M minutes.
  • AQL filter testwhen the event matches this AQL filter query: one WHERE clause replaces a pile of tests.
  • Local or GlobalLocal runs on one Event Processor; Global goes to the Console so events across processors correlate.
Open the Rule Wizard from the Rules button on the Offenses, Log Activity or Network Activity tab. Global is needed for things like login failures seen on several processors.
HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts44 / 59

Responses act; building blocks never do

Rule response

The machinery behind every offense you triage. Dispatch New Event is also here.

  • Set Severity, Credibility, Relevance
  • Ensure part of an offense + index field
  • Add to / Remove from Reference Set
  • Annotate, Email, Notify, Response Limiter

Building block (BB)

A named group of tests that takes no action and creates no offense. Referenced inside rules like a subroutine, e.g. BB:HostDefinition: Mail Servers. Edit once, every rule updates.

A reliable AQL hunt becomes the seed of a rule: paste it into the AQL filter test and set the response.

BB:CategoryDefinition: Authentication Failures defines what counts as an auth failure across 100-plus log source types. Reusing BBs keeps a large rule base consistent.

Reference sets and maps

Maintained lists that both standing rules and ad-hoc AQL can check.

HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts46 / 59

A reference set is a living watchlist

  • What it holdsUnique, single-type values: IPs, usernames, domains or hashes. Admin > Reference Set Management.
  • TypeFor example IP, or Alphanumeric (Ignore Case), which lowercases every value.
  • Time to LiveLives Forever, or expiry from when each value was first seen or last seen.
  • Populated byHand, script or API, a threat-intel feed, or a rule response (Add to Reference Set).
The TTL is what keeps it a living watchlist rather than a stale file.
HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts47 / 59

The same set in a rule and in AQL

  • Add a confirmed-bad IP mid-investigation: every rule checking the set is protected
  • No rule edit, no redeploy
Example set from the lesson: Watchlist_Bad_IPs, type IP, TTL 7 days from last seen.
HACKTHESOCLesson 90IBM QRadar for analysts48 / 59

Key takeaways

  1. 01Start on the Offenses tab, highest Magnitude first, and read the Offense Summary before writing any AQL.
  2. 02Magnitude combines severity, credibility and relevance; relevance from the asset model weighs most.
  3. 03Assign, note, protect, close with a reason. A closed offense that recurs opens a NEW offense.
  4. 04The offense index rolls events into one offense; a null index value means no offense at all.
  5. 05AQL: WHERE filters rows, HAVING filters groups, and no time clause means only the last 5 minutes.
  6. 06DSMs normalise, the CRE correlates, and reference sets feed both rules and AQL at once.

Check your understanding, question 1 of 3

Two offenses come from the same rule with the same event count, one against a domain controller and one against a test VM. The domain-controller offense has a higher Magnitude. Which input explains it?

Relevance comes from the weight given to the network and the asset, and it is the most heavily weighted input. Credibility is set per log source, and the stem fixes event counts as equal.

Choose an answer, or press R to reveal it.

Check your understanding, question 2 of 3

An analyst runs an AQL query with no LAST, START or STOP clause and is surprised it returns almost nothing. What is the most likely reason?

With no time clause, QRadar bounds the query to the last five minutes, so a hunt can look empty. It is not rejected, the default is not 24 hours, and there is no silent ten-row cap.

Choose an answer, or press R to reveal it.

Check your understanding, question 3 of 3

After a spray from one source, a query shows one of the sprayed accounts with a successful logon from the same source. What is the strongest next pivot?

A success after a spray means the credential may be compromised, so the decisive question is what the account did next. Process creation after the success shows lateral movement or discovery.
