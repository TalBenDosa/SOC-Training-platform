Section 1 of 10

The two facts that make this detectable

A token outlives the password check, and every sign-in records where and when it came from.

HACKTHESOCLesson 51Impossible travel6 / 44

From ID check to visitor badge

  1. 1Sign-inYou prove who you are to an identity provider (Entra ID, Okta, Google Workspace): authentication.
  2. 2MFAIdeally a second proof: something you have or are, besides the password you know.
  3. 3Token issuedA small, cryptographically signed token is the badge apps check instead of your password.
  4. 4SessionThe period the token, or a refresh token that renews it, stays valid.
Entra ID was formerly Azure Active Directory. The badge is why you do not retype your password for every email.
HACKTHESOCLesson 51Impossible travel7 / 44

Two ways in, one evidence trail

Steal the password

Sign in as the user. The identity provider logs the sign-in.

Steal the token

Skip authentication entirely by reusing an already-issued badge.

Every sign-in records where and when. A real person can only be in one place at a time.

That single fact is what makes impossible travel detectable at all.

What impossible travel means

Distance divided by time, against a learned baseline.

HACKTHESOCLesson 51Impossible travel9 / 44

The maths: distance over elapsed time

  • Compares source IP geolocation and timestamp of consecutive sign-ins
  • An offline detection in Entra ID Protection: it needs the pair first
Offline means it can surface shortly after the events rather than instantly.
HACKTHESOCLesson 51Impossible travel10 / 44

Why it works, and what it needs

  • Targets behaviourA phished password plus satisfied MFA still cannot be in two places at once.
  • Needs a baselineProviders learn familiar locations, devices and travel. New users have little, so the system is cautious.
  • Rarely fires aloneUsually alongside an unfamiliar sign-in, an anonymous IP, or a new device.
Once a pattern of Tel Aviv weekdays and occasional Jerusalem exists, a session from Nigeria stands out sharply.

The wider family of sign-in anomalies

Attackers trip several detections at once.

HACKTHESOCLesson 51Impossible travel12 / 44

Five signals worth recognising

SignalWhat it indicates
Impossible travelTwo distant logins, too fast
Unfamiliar propertiesNew IP, device, browser or location combination versus learned history
Anonymous IP / TorOrigin deliberately hidden
Atypical tokenPossible session token replay: the badge itself stolen
MFA fatigueMany prompts in quick succession, most denied, then one approval
Also in the family: malicious IP address and password spray, where one password is tried against many accounts. MFA fatigue is a pattern, not a single log line.
HACKTHESOCLesson 51Impossible travel13 / 44
The diagnostic power is in stacking: several signals on one account in one hour.

Anonymous IP plus unfamiliar properties plus impossible travel plus denied MFA prompts is almost certainly compromise. MITRE ATT&CK T1078.004, Valid Accounts: Cloud Accounts.

The attacker is not exploiting a bug; they are using a stolen valid identity. Read the family together rather than chasing each alert alone.

Section 4 of 10

How identity providers score risk

Risk levels on the log record: inputs to policy, not verdicts.

HACKTHESOCLesson 51Impossible travel15 / 44

Risky sign-in versus risky user

Risky sign-in

One authentication event scored low, medium or high. You might block that single sign-in.

Risky user

Accumulated risk suggests the identity itself is compromised. Force a password reset and revoke sessions.

The distinction matters operationally: it changes the response from one event to the whole account.
HACKTHESOCLesson 51Impossible travel16 / 44

A scored sign-in, in real Entra field names

  • userPrincipalName is who; createdDateTime, ipAddress, location are when, where
  • status.errorCode of 0 means the sign-in succeeded
  • deviceDetail.isCompliant: does the device meet company policy?
riskLevelDuringSignIn is the score this whole lesson builds toward reading correctly.
HACKTHESOCLesson 51Impossible travel17 / 44

Real-time and offline detections act at different moments

Real-time

Evaluated during the sign-in. Can feed an immediate Conditional Access decision: demand MFA or block.

Offline

Computed after the fact by correlating events. Raises the user's risk state for the next sign-in. Impossible travel is here.

Treat the risk level as a prioritisation aid, then apply judgement and correlation.

A high-risk sign-in means the model estimated elevated likelihood, not that a breach is confirmed. Policy, e.g. MFA at medium and block at high, is the organisation's choice.

Atypical travel vs. impossible travel

Two separately named Entra detections from two different engines.

HACKTHESOCLesson 51Impossible travel19 / 44

Same idea, different engines

unlikelyTravel

Atypical travel

Entra ID Protection's native offline heuristic. Suppresses false positives from familiar devices and VPN ranges many users in the directory share.

mcasImpossibleTravel

Impossible travel

Sourced from Microsoft Defender for Cloud Apps (formerly Cloud App Security, hence mcas), fed back into Entra risk state.

Both are offline: they need a pair of events before judging distance over time.

Most SOC teams say impossible travel as an umbrella term. A careful analyst reads which engine fired.

HACKTHESOCLesson 51Impossible travel21 / 44

MFA does not retroactively clear an offline detection

  • Offline risk is computed after the sign-in, including its MFA, already succeeded
  • It raises risk for the next evaluation, so revoke sessions directly
The detection measures geographic plausibility, not whether credential and MFA checks passed. A satisfied MFA prompt did not close the gap.

Three rules for reading the catalog

Learning periods

Atypical travel learns for the earliest of 14 days or 10 logins; unfamiliar properties needs at least five days. Quiet new accounts are expected.

Deterministic first

Leaked credentials is always High and fires only on a match to the current password hash (PHS needed on-prem). Password spray needs a confirmed successful validation.

Real-time vs offline

Only real-time rows can gate a sign-in live. Offline rows, including both travel detections, inform the next decision.

Anomalous token is heuristic and can false-positive at low and medium risk, so a deterministic detection at the same band deserves faster action. Mature SOCs pair offline detections with automatic session revocation.

Section 8 of 10

False positives, investigation, and response

Prioritise by risk score, confirm by correlation, respond in proportion.

HACKTHESOCLesson 51Impossible travel31 / 44

Benign situations that mimic the pattern

  • VPNs and proxiesHome login plus a VPN exit node abroad looks like impossible travel.
  • Mobile roaming, CGNATPhones routed through IP space geolocated far from the user.
  • Cloud-to-cloud servicesMail clients fetching from data-centre IPs look like a second, distant actor.
  • Stale geolocationDatabases occasionally misplace an IP by thousands of kilometres.
Never treat an isolated impossible-travel alert as confirmed compromise.
HACKTHESOCLesson 51Impossible travel32 / 44

Investigate by correlation

  1. 1Known ranges?Are the two locations tied to known VPN or mobile ranges?
  2. 2DeviceCompliant, familiar device per deviceDetail.isCompliant?
  3. 3Companion signalsAnonymous IP, atypical token, denied MFA bursts in riskEventTypes_v2?
  4. 4Client appCompare clientAppUsed with the user's 30-day history.
  5. 5Follow-on actionsInbox forwarding rules, new MFA method, large downloads: the strongest confirmation.
A legitimate roaming user keeps a consistent client; an attacker often shows a different app, a scripted client, or an unusual mail protocol.
HACKTHESOCLesson 51Impossible travel33 / 44

Response scales with confidence

Likely false positive

Dismiss the risk and, if appropriate, add the VPN range to trusted locations.

Probable takeover

Revoke sessions and refresh tokens, force a password reset, require MFA re-registration, hunt for OAuth grants and mailbox rules.

Document the timeline, confirming signals and containment steps for this case and future tuning.

Attackers often add their own MFA methods, which is why re-registration matters. Revoking sessions invalidates the stolen badge.

Detection engineering: build the hunt yourself

Tune for VIPs, cover apps outside Entra, and automate the response.

HACKTHESOCLesson 51Impossible travel35 / 44

The full distance-over-time hunt in Sentinel

  • prev() over a serialized stream pairs each sign-in with the user's previous one
  • geo_distance_2points gives great-circle km; divide by the gap for speed
  • ASN != PrevASN drops same-carrier/VPN pairs, the dominant false positive
This does by arithmetic what you did by hand in the walkthrough. Tighten the 900 km/h jet floor for VIP accounts. Keep a watchlist of office and VPN CIDRs and filter them out too.
HACKTHESOCLesson 51Impossible travel36 / 44

Token theft needs more than geo-maths

  • Cover non-interactiveToken replay after an AiTM phish lands in AADNonInteractiveUserSignInLogs, not interactive SigninLogs. Union both or miss the key pattern.
  • Same token, two placesThe same SessionId or UniqueTokenIdentifier from two IPs/ASNs is session-cookie replay: alert on it independent of distance.
  • Token typeAn IncomingTokenType of primaryRefreshToken, or one session id reused across geographies, is worth an alert on its own.
  • Tune at the sourcemcasImpossibleTravel is tunable in Defender for Cloud Apps: sensitivity slider, suppress VPN ranges, exclude service accounts.
When the concern is a stolen session not a stolen password, pivot on the token-identity fields. Save the geo-hunt as a Sentinel analytics rule and wire a playbook to revoke sessions on a high-confidence match.

Recognise the pattern by its logic, not by Microsoft's field names.

Default severity Medium, data source CloudTrail management events. Resource.AccessKeyDetails.UserName replaces userPrincipalName; RemoteIpDetails replaces location and ipAddress.
HACKTHESOCLesson 51Impossible travel39 / 44

What Entra sees with AD FS, and what it does not

Entra still sees it

A federated user signing in to an Entra app still passes through Entra, which logs and scores it. tokenIssuerAnomaly inspects SAML tokens from the federation server.

The real blind spot

Sign-ins to apps federated directly with AD FS never touch Entra, so its risk engine never sees them at all.

AD FS substitutes

For that traffic: Extranet Smart Lockout, and Connect Health's Risky IP report of failed sign-ins per source above a threshold.

Only sign-ins Entra itself processes get unlikelyTravel and the rest, a concrete reason to move apps off AD FS.

Leaked-credentials detection also needs password hash synchronization. Risk-based Conditional Access stays effective with federation.

Key takeaways

  1. 01A sign-in issues a token, the visitor badge; stolen tokens are as dangerous as stolen passwords.
  2. 02Impossible travel divides distance by time between consecutive sign-ins; it is offline and maps to T1078.004.
  3. 03Stack the family: unfamiliar properties, anonymous IP, atypical token, MFA fatigue, on the same account.
  4. 04Read riskEventTypes_v2 exactly: unlikelyTravel is native Entra, mcasImpossibleTravel is Defender for Cloud Apps.
  5. 05Rule out VPN, roaming and stale geo by correlation, then revoke sessions, reset, and re-register MFA.
  6. 06Offline risk is not cleared by a satisfied MFA prompt, so pair it with automatic session revocation.
