What 'security' is actually protecting

Three plain concerns about your own house, given formal names.

HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles6 / 58

Three different concerns about your house

Only invited people see inside

Confidentiality

Only authorized people or systems can read the data. A burglar photographing documents breaks only this.

Nothing secretly changed

Integrity

The data has not been secretly altered; you can trust it is accurate. No swapped pills, no forged signature.

You can get in when needed

Availability

Authorized people can use it when they need to. A vandal smashing the door breaks only this.

The CIA Triad: nothing to do with the agency. A lens you run every incident through, not a product.

A threat to one property does not automatically threaten the others. That independence is what makes the checklist useful.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles7 / 58

Run the triad on one incident: a stolen laptop

An employee's laptop is stolen from a coffee shop. Check each leg separately.

PropertyIs it hit?Why
ConfidentialityYes, if the disk is unencryptedWhoever holds the laptop can read every file on it
AvailabilityMinor hitThe employee cannot work until a replacement arrives
IntegrityOnly if the thief edited somethingA quietly changed saved document would be a tampering concern
Most real incidents touch more than one leg. The skill is naming each affected leg precisely, not picking one label.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles8 / 58

Which property is threatened? It shapes everything

  • UrgencyA breach of credit-card numbers may trigger legal deadlines a minor availability blip never would.
  • EvidenceIntegrity: prove what changed and when. Confidentiality: prove what was accessed and by whom.
  • Communication'Availability incident affecting checkout' tells a manager something very different from 'confidentiality incident affecting PII'.
Worked example: a stolen unencrypted laptop is a confidentiality risk, a minor availability hit, and an integrity concern only if the thief edited a document. Most incidents touch more than one leg.

Confidentiality: keeping secrets secret

Only authorized eyes, in every state the data passes through.

HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles10 / 58

Confidentiality: only authorized eyes can read it

Think of a locked filing cabinet in a doctor's office: only staff with a key open it.

  • EncryptionReadable plaintext plus a secret key becomes ciphertext that looks like random noise without the key.
  • Access controlsPermissions on a shared folder or database decide which accounts may open it at all.
  • TLSTransport Layer Security, the browser padlock: scrambles traffic while it crosses the network.
All three are digital versions of the cabinet lock. When confidentiality breaks, unauthorized parties reach information they should never have touched.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles11 / 58

Encryption: plaintext in, unreadable ciphertext out

  • Stolen ciphertext without the key: confidentiality holds
  • Other mechanisms: access controls, TLS (the browser padlock)
This is why 'the data was encrypted' is often the most important sentence in a breach disclosure.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles12 / 58

Confidentiality attacks are about theft and exposure

Data exfiltration

An attacker copies a customer database out to an external server.

Credential theft

Phishing harvests a password and hands an outsider a valid identity.

Eavesdropping

Sniffing captures unencrypted traffic on the network.

Misconfigured storage

A public cloud bucket leaks files to anyone with the URL.

SOC signals: big outbound transfers to unusual places, sensitive files read by new accounts, impossible-location sign-ins, DLP hits.

DLP is Data Loss Prevention: tooling that flags PII or source code leaving the environment. Each attack here leaves a different trail, which is why the next slide reads one DLP alert.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles13 / 58

A DLP alert: did unauthorized eyes reach the data?

  • Attacks: exfiltration, credential theft, sniffing, misconfigured storage
  • Report what was exposed, to whom, over what channel
Also watch for large outbound transfers to unusual destinations, sensitive-file access by accounts that never touch them, and impossible-location sign-ins. The scope drives breach-notification obligations.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles14 / 58

Reading the DLP alert: what it proves, what to add

What the alert proves

Regulated personal data, in bulk, heading outside company control.

  • Content matched the SSN pattern 240 times
  • j.smith tried it with customer_export.csv
  • The channel was a personal webmail upload
  • This attempt was blocked

What your report must state

That scope drives breach-notification obligations, so document it precisely.

  • What data was exposed
  • To whom
  • Over what channel
The triage question is: did unauthorized eyes reach data they should not have? If yes, this is a confidentiality incident.

Three states of data, three places to enforce it

StatePrimary protectionExample attackSOC signal when it fails
At restDisk encryption (e.g. AES-256), access controls, key managementStolen unencrypted laptop, public bucket, dumped DBSensitive store accessed by an unusual account

A fixed set: there is no fourth state. Name the state and you point at the control that should have held.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles16 / 58

Triage a confidentiality incident: name the state first

  1. 1Stolen backup tapeData at rest. Ask: was it encrypted?
  2. 2Captured network trafficData in transit. Ask: was TLS enforced?
  3. 3Credential dumper on a serverData in use. A process reached decrypted secrets in memory.

The state you name points directly at the control that should have held and did not.

This turns a vague 'data leaked' into a precise finding: which control failed, and therefore what to fix.

Integrity: can you trust the data?

Tampering, fingerprints, and the log gap that gives the attacker away.

HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles18 / 58

Integrity: the data says what it is supposed to say

If you cannot trust a balance, a config or a log entry, it is worthless however secret or available it is.

  • HashingA function gives a fixed-length fingerprint that changes completely if even one byte of input changes.
  • Digital signaturesCryptographic proof that a specific party approved a specific piece of data.
  • File integrity monitoringSoftware that watches critical files and alerts the moment they change.
Integrity means data and systems are accurate and not altered by an unauthorized party. The next slide shows why a hash is such a sharp tool.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles19 / 58

One character changed, a completely different hash

Compare a stored hash with a freshly computed one and you know instantly whether anything changed. Integrity attacks: modified payroll, defacement, backdoored code, ransomware encrypting in place, and deleted or edited logs.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles20 / 58

Integrity attacks are tampering; here is how they show

What attackers change

Ransomware also breaks integrity when it encrypts files in place.

  • A payroll record
  • A website (defacement)
  • Code, by planting a backdoor
  • Logs, deleted or edited to hide tracks

What the analyst sees

Each is an integrity red flag worth a ticket of its own.

  • Unexpected changes to system files
  • New scheduled tasks
  • Unexplained permission changes
  • Gaps in log continuity
Log tampering is the most SOC-relevant integrity attack, because it targets the evidence you investigate with.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles21 / 58

The integrity toolbox

MechanismWhat it guaranteesWhere a SOC meets it
Hash / checksumDetects any change, but not who made itVerifying a downloaded tool against its published hash
HMACChange detection plus proof it came from a key holderAPI request signing
Digital signatureChange detection, a specific signer, non-repudiationCode signing, signed updates and emails
File Integrity MonitoringContinuous alerts on unauthorized file changesSystem binaries, config files, web content
Immutable / WORM loggingHistory cannot be rewrittenTamper-evident audit logs, compliance retention
Version control / change mgmtEvery change tied to an approver, rollback possibleUnapproved production or code changes
HMAC is a Hash-based Message Authentication Code. WORM is Write-Once-Read-Many.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles22 / 58

How hash plus WORM storage exposes log tampering

  1. 1Forward at write timeEach host's security log is copied to a central append-only (WORM) store the moment it is written.
  2. 2Hash hourlyThe SIEM stores an hourly hash of each host's local log.
  3. 3Recompute and compareA later recomputed hash that differs proves the local file was altered.
  4. 4Cross-check the WORM copyThe immutable copy shows exactly which events were removed.
Neither control can be defeated by editing the local file alone. That is the point of layering integrity mechanisms.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles23 / 58

Worked example: catching log tampering

Two controls together: the hash proved the file changed, the immutable copy proved which events vanished. A log gap the WORM store contradicts is often the clearest evidence an attacker was present.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles24 / 58
A missing chunk of logs is often more suspicious than any single alert.

A two-hour hole in an otherwise continuous record suggests someone specifically wanted that window hidden.

When the immutable store contradicts the local log, treat the gap as evidence of presence, not as noise.

Section 4 of 8

Availability: can you reach it when needed?

Denial: DDoS, ransomware, destructive wiping.

HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles26 / 58

Availability attacks are about denial

A locked, fireproof, unreadable safe is secure two ways out of three, and useless.

Distributed Denial of Service

DDoS

A service is flooded with traffic from many sources so real users cannot connect.

Locks the files

Ransomware

The business cannot operate. It hits integrity and availability at once.

Deletes data or backups

Destructive wiping

Sometimes a final act by an attacker who already reached their real goal.

Availability means authorized users can reach data and services when they need them. A system nobody can log into fails it even if the data is secret and intact.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles27 / 58

The shape of a DDoS: volume plus source count

A real spike from a campaign or viral post comes from a plausible spread of normal users. A DDoS shows an implausible number of sources hammering one endpoint.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles28 / 58

Real traffic spike or DDoS? Read volume and sources together

Campaign, viral post

Real spike

A large but plausible spread of normal-looking users across the site.

From the log above

DDoS

Requests jump from 1,200 to 18,500 per second and sources from ~40 to ~3,000, all on /login.

Consequence chain: CPU at 100%, responses past 30 seconds, customers cannot load the login page.

Volume alone is not enough. It is the combination with an implausible source count hammering one endpoint that marks the attack.

The triad in one table

PropertyQuestion it answersExample violation
ConfidentialityWho can read it?Data leak
IntegrityCan I trust it is unchanged?Tampered logs
AvailabilityCan I reach it when needed?DDoS outage
A fireproof, unreadable safe nobody can open is secure in two senses out of three, and useless.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles30 / 58
Ransomware can hit all three at once.

It denies availability and destroys integrity, and in double extortion it steals data first, then threatens to publish it.

Naming each affected property keeps the investigation and impact assessment complete.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles31 / 58

Ransomware across the triad

PropertyHow ransomware hits it
AvailabilityFiles are locked, so the business cannot operate
IntegrityFiles are encrypted in place; the data is no longer usable in its original form
ConfidentialityIn double extortion, data is stolen first and publication is threatened
Writing all three into the impact assessment keeps the investigation complete: the exfiltration leg is easy to miss while everyone is restoring systems.

Defense-in-depth and least privilege

The triad says what we protect; principles say how.

HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles33 / 58

Defense-in-depth: one phishing email, four layers

  1. 1Email gatewayScans the attachment; may miss a novel payload.
  2. 2Endpoint protectionMay block execution.
  3. 3Network segmentationLimits where the malware can spread if it runs.
  4. 4Logging / SOC monitoringRecords everything regardless, giving analysts a trail.
Moat, walls, guards, locked keep. An attack that trips several layers at once is a strong signal, not redundant noise.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles34 / 58

Least privilege turns deviations into loud alarms

Only the access needed for the job, which shrinks the blast radius.

  • Admin actionsA standard user account suddenly performing administrative actions.
  • Interactive service loginA service account logging in interactively when it should run in the background.
  • Privilege escalationAn account acquiring rights it never had before.
  • Related principlesNeed-to-know for specific data; separation of duties so no one completes a sensitive action alone.
A help-desk account should never be a domain administrator.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles35 / 58

Least privilege violations look like this in the logs

DeviationWhat you would seeWhy it is suspicious
Admin actions by a standard userA normal account changing settings only admins changeIts job never needs those rights
Interactive service loginA background service account logging in at a consoleService accounts should only run unattended
Privilege escalationAn account gaining rights it never had beforeAn attacker expanding access
Because every account holds only what its job needs, any use of extra rights stands out. Without least privilege the same actions blend into normal admin noise.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles36 / 58

Two principles that travel with least privilege

Need-to-know

Access to specific data, not just systems. You can reach the file server but only the folders your work requires.

Separation of duties

No single person can complete a sensitive action alone, which reduces insider risk.

With defense-in-depth, these mean even a successful intrusion meets friction, leaves traces and stays contained.

That containment is what buys the analyst both time and evidence to respond.

Zero trust and AAA

Never trust, always verify, and record everything.

HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles38 / 58

Zero trust drops the 'inside is safe' assumption

Traditional

Anything inside the corporate network is trusted. Attackers past the perimeter roam freely.

Zero trust

Every request authenticated and authorized: continuous verification, MFA, device health, micro-segmentation.

Each access decision is logged, giving the SOC richer visibility into lateral movement.

Zero trust is a modern extension of least privilege.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles39 / 58

Zero trust: how one request is decided

  1. 1Request arrivesInside or outside the network makes no difference: nothing is trusted by location.
  2. 2Verify identityStrong MFA (Multi-Factor Authentication) confirms the user.
  3. 3Check the deviceDevice-health checks decide whether this machine may connect.
  4. 4Authorize narrowlyMicro-segmentation grants this resource only, not the whole network.
  5. 5Log the decisionEach access decision is recorded for the SOC.
Never trust, always verify. The logged decisions are what give the SOC visibility into lateral movement.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles40 / 58

AAA: authenticate, then authorize, then account

Three functions behind the log you are about to read.

  • AuthenticationProves who you are. Here method=password+MFA with result=SUCCESS confirms d.rosen before any access.
  • AuthorizationDecides what you may do. The HR-FileShare request is DENIED because d.rosen is not in HR-Access.
  • AccountingRecords what you did. The attempt is logged=yes whether it was allowed or denied, so a trail always exists.
Accounting is the SOC's raw material: it logs the attempt regardless of the outcome, which is what lets you reconstruct an incident later.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles41 / 58

AAA in one sequence

  • Authentication proves who you are: MFA confirmed d.rosen
  • Authorization decides what you may do: no HR-Access, so DENIED
  • Accounting records what you did: logged=yes even for a denial
Authentication proves who you are; authorization decides what you may do; accounting records what you did. Accounting logged it regardless of the outcome.

Non-repudiation: proving who did what

Tying an action to its actor so denial is no longer credible.

HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles44 / 58

Authentication is now; non-repudiation is later

At login or action

Authentication

Is this really the claimed user, right now?

After the fact, under dispute

Non-repudiation

Can this person credibly deny having done this, later? Like signing for certified mail.

A shared or compromised password weakens non-repudiation: 'someone else had my password'. A private key only one person holds closes the gap.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles45 / 58

How a digital signature ties an action to one person

  1. 1One private keyOnly the approver holds it, for example on a hardware token protected by a PIN.
  2. 2Sign the actionThe private key cryptographically signs the approval or document.
  3. 3Anyone verifiesThe matching public key checks the signature without ever seeing the private key.
  4. 4Denial gets expensiveOnly the key holder could produce a valid signature, so 'it wasn't me' needs a far weaker story.
Certified mail is the analogy: once you sign for the package you cannot claim it never arrived. Public-key cryptography is that signature for digital actions.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles46 / 58

A plain log line versus a signed approval

A plain log line is evidence; a cryptographic signature is closer to proof. It matters most in privileged-account disputes and for critical controls like deployment approval or financial sign-off.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles47 / 58

Where a SOC needs proof, not just a log line

SituationWhy a plain log falls shortStronger evidence
Privileged-account disputeThe admin says someone else used the accountAction signed with a key only that admin holds
Production deployment approvalApproval must survive a legal or HR challengeCode-signing certificates
Financial sign-offA shared or leaked password lets the approver deny itHardware security key; S/MIME-signed email
A plain log line is evidence; a cryptographic signature is closer to proof. Ask whether the evidence behind a critical control would survive a dispute.

The Parkerian Hexad and security tradeoffs

Donn B. Parker, 1998: three more properties for the edge cases.

HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles49 / 58

Three properties the triad leaves out

  • Possession or ControlYou have lost custody of the asset even if no one can read it. A stolen encrypted tape loses this.
  • AuthenticityIs it genuinely from its claimed source? A spoofed sender violates this.
  • UtilityIs it still usable? An archive encrypted with a lost key is secret, intact, reachable, and useless.
You need not memorize the Hexad for certification; it sharpens CIA instincts in cases where the triad understates severity.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles50 / 58

Worked example: an encrypted backup tape is stolen

A CIA-only view says low severity; the Hexad says control is permanently lost. GDPR Article 34, HIPAA and US state laws exempt encrypted data only while the key stays uncompromised: if it leaks, the duty to notify can return.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles51 / 58

Same tape, two verdicts

CIA-only view

No confidentiality breach, minor availability impact. Low severity.

Hexad view

Control of a sensitive asset is permanently lost. Future confidentiality depends entirely on one key never leaking.

Breach laws (GDPR Article 34, HIPAA, US state laws) exempt encrypted data only while the key stays uncompromised.

If the key ever leaks, the risk returns and the duty to notify can come back. The exemption is conditional precisely because control was lost.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles52 / 58
Utility: data can be secret, intact and reachable, and still useless.

A company encrypts a critical archive, then loses the only copy of the key. No attacker touched it, yet nobody can use it.

This is the clearest case the triad cannot describe: every CIA check passes and the business has still lost the data.
HACKTHESOCLesson 55CIA triad and principles53 / 58

Strengthening one property often costs another

Strengthening this......often costs this
Confidentiality: strict encryption, tight accessAvailability: latency, legitimate users locked out
Availability: redundancy, more copies and access pointsConfidentiality: more attack surface to secure
Integrity: rigorous change control, mandatory approvalsAvailability: slower legitimate, time-sensitive changes
The properties are not enemies, but every control has a cost somewhere. Judging 'too tight or too loose' is this tradeoff analysis.

Key takeaways

  1. 01The CIA triad asks: who can read it, can I trust it is unchanged, can I reach it when needed.
  2. 02Confidentiality must hold at rest, in transit and in use; naming the state names the failed control.
  3. 03Integrity failures show as changed hashes or log gaps; WORM copies prove what was deleted.
  4. 04Availability breaks look like DDoS spikes or ransomware; ransomware can hit all three properties.
  5. 05Defense-in-depth, least privilege and zero trust contain attackers; AAA accounting provides the evidence.
  6. 06Non-repudiation and the Hexad's possession, authenticity and utility cover what CIA alone misses.
