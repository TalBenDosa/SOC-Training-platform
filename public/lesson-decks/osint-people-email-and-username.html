Why identifiers matter to a SOC

Phishing, BEC, breach exposure and vendor fraud all start from an address or a handle.

HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT6 / 52

Four situations that start with an identifier

Attribute a sender

Spoofed, an attacker lookalike domain, or a compromised real mailbox? Each leads to a different action.

    Triage a BEC

    Freemail or lookalike domain mimicking an executive or vendor. Shows how it was staged and who else got it.

      Check your exposure

      Staff use work addresses on third-party sites. Breach hits show where credential-reuse risk lives.

        Vet a counterparty

        A new vendor wants to change bank details. Domain age and mail setup cost minutes to check.

          A spoof means fixing authentication; a lookalike means block and takedown; a compromised partner means a call on a known number.

          Stress that the right action depends on the answer. A spoof, a lookalike and a compromised mailbox look similar in the inbox but need three different responses.

          Section 2 of 9

          Anatomy of an email address

          What each part can and cannot reveal, and how to check existence without being abusive.

          HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT10 / 52

          The domain holds the evidence, the local part holds little

          An address is local-part@domain. DNS resolves the domain; only the receiving mail system understands the local part.

          Most of the evidence

          The domain

          Public records you can look up:

          • RDAP or WHOIS: registration date, registrar
          • MX records: which servers accept its mail
          • TXT records: SPF, DMARC, SaaS verification tokens
          • Hosting

          Free text

          The local part

          Meaningful only to the receiving mail system, so on its own it proves very little about who is behind the address.

            Domain registered 4 days ago

            A strong signal

            A new domain imitating an established vendor. MX pointing at a bulk-mail relay for a supposed corporate vendor is also odd.

              MX records at a mainstream hosted provider tell you little. The registration date of the domain is usually the single most useful fact.
              HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT11 / 52

              Local-part conventions that change how you read an address

              • Plus addressingjane+shop@example.com reaches jane@example.com on many providers. A tag shows which service leaked it.
              • Dot-insensitivitySome freemail ignores dots, so j.ane and jane reach one mailbox. Attackers use it for look-alike registrations.
              • Role accountsbilling@, accounts@ and info@ are shared mailboxes: no attribution to one person.
              • Display name vs addressThe display name is arbitrary text. 'Chief Financial Officer' can come from any address.
              The display name trap is the one that fools busy finance staff. Always read the address behind the name.
              HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT12 / 52
              Active SMTP probing is unreliable and often abusive. Prefer passive, low-footprint checks.

              Catch-alls accept anything; other servers bounce later. Probing can get your IP blocklisted, touches a third party without consent and can alert an adversary.

              Probing means connecting to the target's mail server and issuing RCPT TO. Servers hide which mailboxes exist on purpose, so the answer is not trustworthy anyway.
              HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT13 / 52

              Prefer passive checks, and know each one's limit

              TechniqueWhat it tells youLimits
              MX lookupDomain can receive mailSays nothing about the specific mailbox
              RDAP/WHOISRegistration date, registrarPrivacy services hide registrant details
              Your own mail logsPrior legitimate historyOnly covers your organization
              SMTP RCPT probingServer's response to a recipientCatch-alls and deferred bounces make it unreliable; intrusive
              Public searchWhere the address is publishedAbsence proves nothing

              For vendors, verify through a channel you already trust, such as the phone number in your contract.

              Other passive options: look for the address on a company contact page, a directory, a code commit or a conference speaker list. The highlighted row is the one to avoid.

              Section 3 of 9

              Email header analysis and public association

              The headers are the primary forensic artifact. The body is secondary.

              HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT15 / 52

              Read the Received chain bottom to top

              Each server that handles a message prepends a Received header.

              1. 1The oldest hop is at the bottomEvery server prepends its own line, so the story starts at the base.
              2. 2The top line is yoursAdded by your own gateway: the only one you can fully trust.
              3. 3Earlier lines are claimsLines added by other servers can be forged by the original sender.
              4. 4Find the first foreign hopThe first hop your infrastructure did not write: note the connecting IP and HELO name.
              Trust decreases as you go down the chain. Treat the lower lines as claims to be tested, not facts.
              HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT16 / 52

              Five header fields and what each one tells you

              FieldWhat it isWhat to look for
              FromThe address the user seesFully attacker-controlled
              Return-PathEnvelope sender (MAIL FROM): where bounces goSPF is evaluated against this domain
              Reply-ToWhere your answer will goA Reply-To that differs from From is a classic BEC sign
              Message-IDUsually ends with the originating system's domainA mismatch with the From domain is informative
              Authentication-ResultsThe verdicts your own gateway recorded (RFC 8601)Read all three results, not only spf
              From is the field the user trusts and the one the attacker controls completely. The gateway's Authentication-Results is the one you can rely on.
              HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT17 / 52

              Pass, pass, fail: reading spf=pass alone misleads

              A trimmed header from a message that claims to be the CFO.

              • SPF and DKIM pass for the sending service's domain, not for ourcorp.example
              • DMARC needs alignment with the visible From domain, so it fails
              • Reply-To sends your answer to a freemail mailbox
              The attacker used a legitimate bulk-mail provider to impersonate a domain. Reading only spf=pass would mislead you. The full phishing methodology lives in the phishing lesson.
              HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT18 / 52

              Find where an address is publicly associated

              Look for context that is openly published, and record the source and time of each finding.

              • Quoted web searchThe exact address in quotation marks finds contact pages, forum posts and old documents.
              • Code hosting commitsCommit metadata often contains author email addresses.
              • Domain-pattern servicesHunter publishes an organization's format (first.last@) and where addresses were seen.
              • Certificates and passive DNSCertificate transparency logs and passive DNS for the associated domain.

              No results does not mean innocence: it may simply be a new, disposable address.

              Example: a vendor whose staff all use first.last@ but whose invoice comes from accounts-dept@ is worth a callback.

              Section 4 of 9

              Breach data checked the right way

              Know your exposure through notification services, never through stolen dumps.

              HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT20 / 52

              What a Have I Been Pwned entry tells you

              HIBP records which breaches an address appears in. It does not publish the stolen content.

              FieldWhat it tells you
              Name and titleThe breached service
              Breach date, date addedWhen it happened and when the service learned of it
              PwnCountThe number of accounts in the dataset
              DataClassesWhat was exposed: email addresses, passwords, IP addresses, phone numbers
              FlagsIsVerified, IsSensitive (not publicly searchable), stealer-log categories
              Read DataClasses first: an entry with Passwords is a credential-reuse risk, an entry with only email addresses is a lower concern.
              HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT21 / 52

              A breach hit is an exposure signal, not a compromise

              What 'pwned' means

              This address was present in a dataset that was exposed. Many hits come from consumer sites where an employee used a work address years ago.

                What it does not mean

                A hit alone does not establish any of these:

                • The account is compromised today
                • Your organization was breached
                • The password is still valid

                Defenders care because reused passwords turn someone else's breach into your credential-stuffing problem.

                Overreading a hit leads to a false incident. Dismissing it ignores real reuse risk. The right reaction sits in between: a proportionate, targeted response.
                HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT22 / 52

                Monitor your own organization with domain search

                This is exposure monitoring of assets you own.

                1. 1Prove control of the domainA DNS TXT record, a file on the web server, or a message to an address at that domain.
                2. 2See which addresses appearThe service shows which addresses at your domain are in known breaches.
                3. 3Subscribe to notificationsYou are told about future breaches that include your addresses.

                Individual employees can also check and subscribe for their own address.

                Domain verification is what makes this lawful and defensible: you only see exposure for domains you control.
                HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT23 / 52

                Defensive habits for breach data

                • Pwned Passwords (k-anonymity)Send the first five characters of the SHA-1 hash, get suffixes with counts, compare locally.
                • Do not traffic in dumpsBuying, downloading or searching stolen dumps may be unlawful, breaches privacy duties, funds criminals and risks malware.
                • Act on hits for your own usersForce a reset if reuse cannot be excluded, ensure MFA, and review sign-in logs.
                • Never test a leaked credentialLogging in with one, or validating a vendor employee's exposed password, is unauthorized access.

                Organizations use Pwned Passwords to refuse known-breached passwords at creation or reset.

                Notification services deliver the defensive value, who is exposed, without the harm. A colleague proposing to buy a dump should be redirected here.

                Section 5 of 9

                Username enumeration: Sherlock and holehe

                People and attackers reuse handles. Hits are leads, not conclusions.

                HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT25 / 52

                What each tool reveals, and what it cannot

                ToolInput and methodA hit meansIt cannot tell you
                SherlockUsername; fetches public profile URLsA page exists at that nameWhether the page belongs to your subject
                holeheEmail; probes sign-up or reset responsesThe address is registered at that serviceWho the user is, or what they do there
                Manual searchEither; search engines, site searchContext, content, languageCompleteness

                A hit establishes that an account or page exists, and nothing more.

                Neither tool tells you that the person behind two profiles is the same human. The highlighted row, holehe, needs the most caution.
                HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT26 / 52

                Two approaches, two levels of caution

                Reads public pages

                Sherlock

                Requests the profile URL pattern on several hundred sites and judges existence by HTTP status, an error message or a redirect, as a browser would.

                  Exercises account forms

                  holehe

                  Probes sign-up or password-reset flows. Requests come from your IP, may notify the account holder and may breach a service's terms.

                  • Confirm policy and counsel allow it
                  • Only for in-scope addresses: yours or an authorized indicator
                  Use holehe for your own organization's addresses, or an attacker indicator inside an authorized investigation. Never for curiosity about a person.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT27 / 52

                  Why a hit can be wrong

                  False positives and false negatives are both common with these tools.

                  • Common namesjsmith88 can belong to thousands of different people on thousands of sites.
                  • Placeholder pagesSites that return HTTP 200 or a generic page for any name create false hits.
                  • Blocked checksAnti-bot protection, rate limits and changed layouts look like 'not found'.
                  • Recycled namesA different person may have created the account after the original was deleted.
                  • Brand-style handlesDifferent people reuse a brand-style handle.
                  Both false hits and missed hits occur. Neither tells you the truth by itself.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT28 / 52
                  A hit is a candidate lead, never a conclusion.

                  Corroborate with an independent signal: photo, writing style, linked sites, creation timeline or a mutual reference. State confidence as low, medium or high, and why.

                  Accepting a long list of Sherlock hits as one person risks accusing innocent people. Corroborate first.

                  Section 6 of 9

                  People search and professional footprint

                  Due diligence on counterparties, and an honest look at how easy it is to impersonate your own people.

                  HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT30 / 52

                  Public profiles serve two defensive purposes

                  Due diligence

                  Answers a business question about an organization, not a dossier on a private person.

                  • Contact has a consistent professional profile
                  • Company is in an official business registry
                  • Address and directors match
                  • Correspondence domain matches its own site

                  Exec-impersonation exposure

                  Attackers build BEC lures from the same public material you can see.

                  • Executives and finance staff listed publicly
                  • Guessable email format such as first.last@
                  • Posts revealing travel, projects, acquisitions
                  • Look-alike profiles with the same photo and bio

                  The output

                  A defensive control, not a list of targets.

                  • Mail rules for display-name impersonation
                  • Enforced DMARC on your domain
                  • Payment-change callback procedure
                  • Takedown process for fake profiles
                  Awareness training with realistic examples also belongs in the output. The aim is to reduce how easy it is to impersonate your staff.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT31 / 52

                  Profiles can lie, and some methods are off limits

                  • Profiles can be fakeA photo, long job history and many connections can still be fake. Check account age, connections and the employer's own site.
                  • No fake personasDeceptive friend requests often breach platform terms and the law.
                  • No automated scrapingIt commonly violates platform terms. Use the manual interface or licensed products.
                  • Stay in scopeA staff member's family, home address and personal accounts do not belong in a vendor invoice report.
                  Private individuals who are not part of the business question are out of scope, whatever is technically findable.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT32 / 52
                  Could you explain this lookup to the person concerned and to a regulator?

                  If you cannot say why it was necessary for a legitimate security purpose, do not do it.

                  Use this as the quick gut-check students carry into every investigation.

                  Section 7 of 9

                  Pivoting responsibly and keeping a lawful trail

                  Pivoting is governed by scope and confidence, not curiosity.

                  HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT34 / 52

                  A disciplined pivot in five steps

                  Each pivot widens the investigation and the risk of mistake.

                  1. 1State the questionE.g. is this invoice sender connected to the genuine vendor, or to an attacker?
                  2. 2Start from the artifactThe sender address in the message, with the headers preserved.
                  3. 3Pivot only if it changes the decisionThe domain's registration date can. The sender's hobby site cannot.
                  4. 4Corroborate before you chainNo pivot from a Sherlock hit without one independent signal, or errors compound.
                  5. 5Stop when answeredMore data is not more safety, it is more liability.
                  Errors compound: a wrong profile leads to wrong addresses, which lead to a wrong conclusion about a real, innocent person.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT35 / 52

                  The pivot map and the typical error at each hop

                  FromToTypical sourceTypical error
                  Email addressDomainSplit at the @ signTreating freemail domains as organizational evidence
                  DomainRegistrant, hosting, sibling domainsRDAP, passive DNS, certificate logsPrivacy services hide owner; shared hosting links unrelated sites
                  Email local-partUsername candidateSame string reusedCommon strings match many people
                  UsernamePublic profilesSherlock, manual searchFalse positives, recycled names
                  ProfileContact details, linked sitesPublic bio textStale or deliberately planted information
                  Walk the first two rows: the email-to-domain hop is the highest-value pivot in the lesson. Later hops get weaker and riskier.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT36 / 52

                  Keep a trail a colleague can reproduce

                  A reviewer must be able to see that your work was lawful.

                  • Case and purposeThe ticket number and why you looked.
                  • Time in UTCWhen each step happened.
                  • Exact query and toolThe tool and version, and the source URL or service.
                  • Saved evidenceA screenshot or saved copy of the public page. Hash saved files.
                  • Result and confidenceWhat you found and how sure you are.
                  • What you did not pursueRecord what you deliberately left alone.

                  Store evidence in the case system with restricted access and apply retention limits.

                  The same record protects the analyst: if the work is questioned later, the trail shows purpose and proportionality.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT37 / 52

                  Operational hygiene: protect the case and yourself

                  Do

                  Keep your corporate identity out of look-ups on adversary-controlled sites.

                  • Use a dedicated workstation or browser profile
                  • Keep evidence in the case system, restricted
                  • Hand criminal leads to law enforcement or legal

                  Never

                  Each of these changes the case or exposes you.

                  • Click links from the message on a normal workstation
                  • Reply to the suspect to test them
                  • Log in with any credentials you find

                  If the trail points to a criminal actor, hand off rather than investigating further yourself.

                  Replying to a phishing email to test the sender changes the case and can tip them off.

                  Section 8 of 9

                  Privacy, legality and data minimization

                  The data is public, but collecting, combining and storing it about people is regulated.

                  HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT39 / 52

                  Public means accessible without bypassing a control

                  Defensive intent does not exempt you from the rules.

                  Public

                  Accessible to anyone without bypassing a control. A web page, a public directory, a published commit.

                    Not public

                    Behind a login you are not entitled to, or obtained by guessing, leaked credentials, trickery or a misconfigured system.

                    • A leaked database on a forum is stolen data that is merely reachable
                    This is the line students get wrong most often. Reachable is not the same as public.
                    HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT40 / 52

                    Five privacy principles that bind a defender

                    As in GDPR and similar laws.

                    • Lawful basis, purpose limitA legitimate reason such as fraud protection, and use the data only for that purpose.
                    • Data minimizationCollect only what the question needs: domain evidence, not a home address.
                    • Storage limitationDelete or archive when the case closes, per your retention schedule.
                    • AccuracyWrong matches harm real people. Record uncertainty and correct errors.
                    • Security, accountabilityRestrict access to case files and record why you collected them.
                    Example of purpose limitation: data from a vendor-fraud check must never be reused for an unrelated HR curiosity.
                    HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT41 / 52

                    Legal exposure to know about

                    RiskWhy it matters
                    Computer misuse lawsLogging in with a found credential, even to confirm it, is unauthorized access
                    Stolen personal dataPossessing or distributing it can be an offense in many jurisdictions
                    Terms of serviceScraping and account probing can mean bans, civil claims or reputational damage
                    Stalking, harassment, doxxing lawsApply to profile-building about private individuals regardless of intention
                    Employment and works-council rulesMay restrict investigating your own staff: involve HR and legal first

                    A general overview, not legal advice. Your counsel and privacy officer decide where the line sits.

                    Aim to characterize infrastructure and behavior, not compile a life story. If evidence starts describing family, health, location or political views, stop, record that you stopped, and escalate to legal.
                    HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT42 / 52

                    A five-question checklist before each look-up

                    1. 1Is it necessary?Does it serve a legitimate security purpose?
                    2. 2Is it public?Reachable by anyone without bypassing a control.
                    3. 3Is it proportionate?You collect only what the question needs.
                    4. 4Is it documented?Source, time and reason are in the case record.
                    5. 5Would the subject be comfortable?Imagine the person seeing this note.
                    Print this one. It condenses the privacy section into a habit.

                    Worked example: the lookalike invoice

                    A forwarded invoice, one swapped letter pair, and a lawful, documented investigation.

                    HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT44 / 52

                    Spot the lookalike: r plus n imitates m

                    Invoice 40817 for 18,400 euros, urgent, new bank account. Claimed sender: Dana Cohen of Fabrikam Parts.

                    fabrikam-parts.example

                    Genuine supplier

                    Domain created 11 years ago. Its own site lists only this domain and a different phone number.

                      fabrikarn-parts.example

                      The sender

                      The letters r and n side by side imitate an m. The display name matches a real contact, but the address differs from earlier mail.

                        SPF, DKIM, DMARC pass

                        Authentication

                        All pass because the attacker owns the lookalike. A pass proves the mail came from that domain, not that it is the vendor.

                          This is the trap: students see three passes and relax. A pass only proves origin from the domain the attacker registered.
                          HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT45 / 52

                          The investigation, step by step

                          The ticket question: spoofed, attacker-controlled, or a compromised real mailbox?

                          1. 1Scope and safetyState the question. Save the original as .eml. Do not reply or open the attachment.
                          2. 2Read the identifierfabrikarn-parts.example is a lookalike of fabrikam-parts.example.
                          3. 3Read the headersAll auth passes, Reply-To matches From, first external hop is a rented server.
                          4. 4Check public sourcesRDAP, MX, SPF and DMARC, certificate transparency, the vendor's own site.
                          5. 5Check your exposureDomain search shows no new hits. Do not look up the vendor's employees.
                          6. 6Decide, then actAttacker-controlled lookalike, high confidence. Hold payment, call a known number.
                          Not spoofing of the real domain: authentication on the real domain would fail or be absent. A compromised genuine mailbox is less likely but not fully excluded until the vendor confirms.
                          HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT46 / 52

                          Public-source checks on the lookalike domain

                          CheckFindingInterpretation
                          RDAP registration dateCreated 5 days agoNew; the real vendor domain was created 11 years ago
                          RegistrantPrivacy-protectedNo attribution from this source
                          MX recordsPresent, hosted mail serviceDomain can send and receive
                          SPF and DMARCPublished, p=noneSet up by someone who planned mail use
                          Certificate transparencyCertificate issued 5 days agoMatches the registration burst
                          Vendor's real siteLists only the genuine domain and a different phone numberContact data does not support this sender
                          Hunter-style patternVendor staff use the genuine domain with first.lastFormat is copied, domain is not

                          Together with the changed bank details and urgency, this supports an attacker-controlled lookalike, high confidence.

                          Each row alone is weak. The convergence of new registration, letter swap, changed bank details and urgency is what makes the conclusion defensible.
                          HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT47 / 52

                          Act: hold, verify, block, document

                          You never needed to contact the sender, probe the mailbox, or profile any person.

                          • Hold the paymentDo not pay invoice 40817 to the new account.
                          • Call on a trusted numberUse the phone number in the signed contract, not the one in the email.
                          • Block and searchBlock the domain at the gateway and proxy; search mail logs for other recipients.
                          • Request takedownFile takedown requests with the registrar.
                          • Share the indicatorsAdd the domain and sending IP to the threat-intel platform with a confidence level.
                          • Write up the trailSources, times, queries and the decision.
                          Close the loop with the three questions from the start: the lookup supported a decision, used the least intrusive sources, and stayed within the law.
                          HACKTHESOCLesson 123Identifier OSINT48 / 52

                          Key takeaways

                          1. 01Identifier OSINT yields leads and probabilities, not proof: a username match is a hypothesis and a breach hit is an exposure signal.
                          2. 02Read Received headers bottom to top, and read all of SPF, DKIM and DMARC: passes for another domain still leave a DMARC fail.
                          3. 03A passing SPF, DKIM and DMARC proves origin, not identity: a lookalike domain can pass everything, so check its age and contact data.
                          4. 04Check exposure with domain search on assets you own; never buy, download or test stolen dumps or leaked credentials.
                          5. 05Treat every Sherlock or holehe hit as a candidate lead: corroborate independently and state confidence as low, medium or high.
                          6. 06Pivot only when it can change the decision, collect only what the question needs, document every step, and stop when it is answered.
