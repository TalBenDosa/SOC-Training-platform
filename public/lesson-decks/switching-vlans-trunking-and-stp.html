How a switch forwards frames

Every frame triggers a learn step and a forward step, keyed by VLAN and MAC address.

HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP6 / 53

Every frame triggers learn, then forward

A hub repeats everything. A switch makes this decision for every frame.

  1. 1Learn the sourceRecord source MAC, ingress port and VLAN in the CAM table. Entries age out after 300 seconds of silence.
  2. 2Known unicastDestination in the table for that VLAN: send out that port only. If it lives on the ingress port, filter (drop).
  3. 3Unknown unicastFlood out every port in the same VLAN except the ingress port. The owner replies and the switch learns it.
  4. 4Broadcast and multicastFF:FF:FF:FF:FF:FF is always flooded in the VLAN. Multicast floods too unless IGMP snooping limits it.
Stress that learning uses the source address and forwarding uses the destination. Unknown unicast flooding is how first contact works, and it never leaves the VLAN.

The MAC address table, one entry per VLAN and MAC

  • The table is keyed by VLAN plus MAC, so each VLAN has its own independent table
  • Ask: does the switch know this MAC, on which port, and is that the port you expect?
Walk the three columns that matter: VLAN, MAC, port. These three questions drive most Layer 2 troubleshooting: does the switch know this MAC, on which port, and is that the port I expect?

Why we segment: four reasons

Each port is its own collision domain, but the VLAN stays one broadcast domain: every ARP request and DHCP discover reaches every host.

  • PerformanceBroadcasts interrupt every host CPU in the broadcast domain.
  • Fault containmentA loop or a broadcast storm stays inside one broadcast domain.
  • SecurityUsers, servers, guests and phones sit in separate segments.
  • PolicyACLs and QoS are applied where segments meet at a Layer 3 boundary.
On full-duplex links collisions no longer occur, so the VLAN is the domain that matters. Subnetting and routing are separate lessons.
HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP9 / 53

Loops and floods show up as MAC flapping

One MAC seen on two ports in quick succession is a Layer 2 warning sign.

  • Same MAC alternating between two ports: suspect a loop
  • Fake-MAC flooding fills the CAM table; port security is the control (section 8)
A loop makes one MAC appear to jump between ports. The table also has finite size: an attacker who floods thousands of fake source MACs can fill it so the switch falls back to flooding and lets the attacker sniff traffic.

VLANs and access ports

A VLAN is a logical broadcast domain defined in the switch, not by cabling.

HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP11 / 53

A VLAN is a logical broadcast domain

Floors

Physical LAN

Hotel floors fixed in concrete: who can reach what depends on where the cable runs.

    Key cards

    VLAN

    Colored key cards decide which guests reach which rooms, regardless of floor. One physical switch acts as many independent switches.

    • Different VLANs cannot exchange frames directly
    • They must pass through a Layer 3 device
    • Access port: one data VLAN, untagged frames
    The end device (PC, printer, camera) never knows a VLAN exists. The switch assigns membership internally as the frame enters the access port.
    HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP12 / 53

    VLAN IDsWhere they liveNotes

    1Normal rangeThe default VLAN, cannot be deleted
    1-1005vlan.dat in flash, not the running configuration1002-1005 are legacy FDDI and Token Ring entries, also undeletable
    1006-4094Extended rangeNeeds VTP transparent mode (or VTP v3) on most platforms
    A common surprise: you can read the running config and not see your normal-range VLANs, because they live in the VLAN database file vlan.dat.

    Create VLANs, assign access ports, verify

    • Trunk ports never appear in show vlan brief: a missing port may be a trunk
    • A port in a nonexistent VLAN is auto-created or left inactive, depending on release
    show vlan brief is the command you will use most in this module. If a port you expect is missing from the list, check whether it is a trunk.

    Voice VLANs and port hygiene

    • Two VLANs on one portA phone sits between switch and PC: the PC is untagged in the data VLAN, the phone tagged in the voice VLAN.
    • Learning the voice VLANThe switch tells the phone which VLAN to tag with using CDP or LLDP-MED.
    • Voice configAdd switchport voice vlan 150 to an access port that has switchport access vlan 10.
    • Parking VLANNever leave user ports in VLAN 1. Put idle ports in a dedicated unused VLAN and shut them down.
    • What to askWhich VLAN is this port in, and is it active on both switches? Is it an access port or a trunk?
    Hygiene point for the SOC: unused ports left in VLAN 1 and enabled are an open invitation. Parking VLAN plus shutdown removes it.

    Trunking and 802.1Q tagging

    A trunk carries many VLANs on one link. 802.1Q is the shipping label on every box.

    HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP16 / 53

    The 802.1Q tag adds 4 bytes after the source MAC

    Tag fieldSizePurpose
    TPID16 bitsFixed value 0x8100, marks the frame as tagged
    PCP3 bitsPriority, used by QoS
    DEI1 bitSingle-bit field carried in the tag
    VID12 bitsThe VLAN ID the receiving switch reads

    The maximum frame grows from 1518 to 1522 bytes.

    A trunk is normally between two switches, or from a switch to a router or hypervisor. Analogy: a freight truck with a label on every box so the dock knows which department it belongs to.
    HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP17 / 53

    The native VLAN must match on both ends

    Native VLAN 999

    Both ends agree

    Untagged frames mean the same VLAN on both switches. Best practice: an unused VLAN as native on every trunk.

    • Also reduces double-tagging VLAN hopping exposure

    Native 1 vs native 999

    Mismatch

    Switch A sends untagged frames meaning VLAN 1, switch B treats untagged as VLAN 999. Traffic leaks from one VLAN into another.

    • CDP logs NATIVE_VLAN_MISMATCH on Gi0/24 (1) vs (999)
    One VLAN on an 802.1Q trunk is sent untagged: the native VLAN, VLAN 1 by default. Cisco switches warn about a mismatch through CDP, which is a log the SOC can collect.
    HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP18 / 53

    Configuring a trunk

    • Newer switches support only dot1q and reject the encapsulation command
    • Allowed list limits the VLANs on the trunk; default is 1-4094
    • nonegotiate stops DTP frames on the trunk
    The encapsulation command exists only on platforms that also support the obsolete ISL protocol. By default a trunk carries VLANs 1-4094, so limiting the list is good hygiene.
    HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP19 / 53

    Allowed list and DTP: two classic traps

    • Allowed list replacesswitchport trunk allowed vlan 30 overwrites the whole list with just VLAN 30.
    • Edit with keywordsUse add, remove or except, e.g. switchport trunk allowed vlan add 30.
    • DTP negotiationDynamic auto and dynamic desirable negotiate; many Catalyst models default to dynamic auto. Two auto ports never trunk.
    • The attackA host that sends DTP frames can talk a user port into becoming a trunk and receive every VLAN's traffic.
    • Hardcode every portmode access on user ports, mode trunk and nonegotiate on trunks.

    Ask: is the port really trunking, and do both ends match on native VLAN?

    Read show interfaces trunk in four blocks: mode and native VLAN, allowed, allowed and active, and forwarding and not pruned. A VLAN missing from the second block is not allowed; one missing from the third is not defined or not forwarding on this switch.

    VTP and its dangers

    VTP copies the VLAN database over trunks. One wrong move can erase a campus's VLANs.

    HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP21 / 53

    VTP modes: who edits, who applies, who forwards

    Create VLAN 40 once on a server switch and every switch in the VTP domain learns it.

    ModeCreates and edits VLANsApplies received updatesForwards updates
    ServerYesYesYes
    ClientNoYesYes
    TransparentLocal onlyNoYes (v1 and v2)
    OffLocal onlyNoNo
    VTP is Cisco-proprietary. The convenience is real, but the cost is the next slide.
    HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP22 / 53

    The revision wipe, step by step

    Higher number wins, whichever database is more correct.

    1. 1Lab switch, revision 85Same domain name, client or server mode, only VLAN 1 and a few lab VLANs.
    2. 2Plugged into productionThe production switches sit at revision 40.
    3. 3Higher revision winsReceivers overwrite their VLAN database completely: no merge, no judgment.
    4. 4VLANs deletedProduction loses VLANs 10, 20, 150 and the rest.
    5. 5Users go offlineAccess ports in those VLANs go inactive. Client mode does not protect you.
    Each change on a server increments a 32-bit configuration revision number. A receiving switch with a matching domain name (and password, if set) accepts any higher revision. Clients advertise too, which is why client mode is no defense.
    HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP23 / 53

    Three ways to prevent the wipe

    • Go transparent or offRun VTP transparent (or off) everywhere and manage VLANs per switch with automation or templates.
    • Harden VTPIf you use VTP, set a password and prefer version 3, which adds a primary server concept.
    • Reset before connectingChange the domain name to a throwaway value and back, or switch to transparent and back.

    Pruning (vtp pruning on the server) stops needless flooding; an allowed list does it with more control.

    Many shops run transparent mode everywhere. A curated switchport trunk allowed vlan list gives the same result as VTP pruning with more control, which is why those designs pair transparent mode with explicit allowed lists.
    HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP24 / 53

    Transparent mode always reports revision 0

    • A transparent switch reports revision 0, so it can never send a bogus higher number
    • Ask: what mode, which domain, what revision? Could a new device carry a higher one?
    Before connecting any previously used switch, ask: what VTP mode and domain is it in, what is its revision, and could it carry a higher revision than production?

    Section 5 of 9

    Spanning Tree: loops, root and port roles

    STP (802.1D) blocks some ports so redundant links form a loop-free tree.

    HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP26 / 53

    Why Ethernet needs STP

    Broadcast storm

    The problem

    An Ethernet frame has no TTL. In a triangle of three switches, a broadcast is flooded back and forth forever.

    • Bandwidth and CPU are consumed
    • MAC tables thrash
    • The network goes down in seconds

    Block some ports

    The fix

    STP logically blocks some ports so the topology becomes a loop-free tree, keeping the blocked links ready as backups.

      BPDUs and bridge ID

      How switches coordinate

      Switches exchange BPDUs every 2 seconds by default. The bridge ID is a priority plus the switch MAC.

      • Priority holds the VLAN ID (sys-id-ext)
      • Set in steps of 4096; default 32768
      • VLAN 10 therefore shows 32778
      Redundancy is essential, since one cable failure should not isolate a building, but Ethernet has no built-in way to stop looping frames. The flapping log from section 1 is a symptom of this.
      HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP27 / 53

      Election: root, root port, designated, blocked

      1. 1Root bridgeLowest bridge ID wins. On a priority tie, the lowest MAC wins, usually the oldest switch. Set priority deliberately.
      2. 2Root portEach non-root switch picks the port with the lowest path cost: 100 Mb/s is 19, 1 Gb/s is 4, 10 Gb/s is 2.
      3. 3Designated portOn each segment, the port on the switch with the best path to the root forwards toward the root.
      4. 4Blocked (alternate) portEverything else blocks.
      Root port ties break on the lowest sender bridge ID, then the lowest sender port ID. The oldest switch winning by MAC is rarely the switch you want as root.
      HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP28 / 53

      802.1D port states and timing

      802.1D stateLearns MACsForwards dataDuration
      BlockingNoNoup to 20 s (max age)
      ListeningNoNo15 s
      LearningYesNo15 s
      ForwardingYesYesstable
      DisabledNoNoadmin down

      A port coming up needs about 30 seconds, up to 50 after an indirect failure, before it forwards.

      This delay is what the next section fixes. Only the forwarding state both learns MACs and forwards data.
      HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP29 / 53

      Set the root on purpose, then read the result

      • PVST+ runs a tree per VLAN: split root duty between two core switches
      • Gi0/24 is the root port (Root FWD); Gi0/23 is the blocked alternate (Altn BLK)
      Cisco runs PVST+ by default, one tree per VLAN, which allows load sharing: be root for some VLANs on one core switch and for the others on the second. Ask: where is the root, and are the blocking ports the ones I expect?

      PortFast, BPDU Guard and RSTP

      Classic STP is slow and trusts every BPDU. Three features fix that.

      HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP31 / 53

      RSTP (802.1w): same election, about one second

      Convergence takes about a second on healthy links instead of 30 to 50 seconds.

      • Fewer statesDiscarding, learning and forwarding replace blocking, listening and disabled.
      • New rolesAlternate (backup for the root port) and backup (standby for a designated port on a shared segment).
      • HandshakePoint-to-point full-duplex neighbors use proposal and agreement messages and skip the timers.
      • Active keepaliveEvery switch sends its own BPDU each hello; three missed hellos mean the neighbor is gone.
      • Edge portsPorts connected to end hosts go straight to forwarding.

      Cisco rapid-pvst mode is the usual choice.

      In 802.1D only the root originates BPDUs; in RSTP every switch does. Interoperability with legacy 802.1D switches is automatic but slows those segments down.
      HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP32 / 53

      PortFast and BPDU Guard on an access port

      • PortFast skips listening and learning and goes straight to forwarding
      • The global commands apply PortFast and BPDU Guard to all access ports at once
      • Server trunk to a hypervisor: spanning-tree portfast trunk. Never on a switch link.
      A port facing a PC does not need to wait 30 seconds, because no loop can start behind a single host. Forgetting PortFast is a classic fault: PCs time out on DHCP during boot because the link is up but the port is not yet forwarding.
      HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP33 / 53

      Four guards, four jobs

      Edge ports

      PortFast

      Skips listening and learning on host-facing ports. Never enable on a link to another switch: it can create a loop STP no longer prevents.

        Access ports

        BPDU Guard

        A PortFast port should never receive a BPDU. If one arrives, the port is shut down immediately (err-disable).

          Toward the access layer

          Root Guard

          Refuses to let a switch behind the port become root. A superior BPDU makes the port root-inconsistent until it stops.

            Unidirectional links

            Loop Guard

            Protects against a unidirectional link failure, where BPDUs stop but the link looks up and a blocked port could start forwarding.

              An unexpected BPDU on an edge port means someone connected a switch: maybe a cheap desk switch with a loop, maybe an attacker advertising a superior bridge ID to become root.
              HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP34 / 53

              BPDU Guard in the logs: what the SOC sees

              Recover: remove the offending device, then shutdown and no shutdown the interface, or let errdisable recovery do it after the timer.

              Ask: what device sent the BPDU, and was it supposed to be there? Remove the offending device first, then recover the port.

              EtherChannel: LACP, PAgP or static

              Several physical links become one logical Port-channel, so STP sees one link.

              HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP36 / 53

              LACP is the preferred bundling method

              More bandwidth, and sub-second failover when a member link fails.

              MethodStandardModesNotes
              LACPIEEE 802.3ad / 802.1AXactive, passiveWorks across vendors. Preferred choice.
              PAgPCisco proprietarydesirable, autoCisco to Cisco only.
              StaticnoneonNo negotiation, no error detection.
              Analogy: four single-lane roads become one four-lane highway with a single name. STP sees one link, so it does not block the extra cables.
              HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP37 / 53

              When a bundle forms, and when it never does

              At least one initiator

              Forms

              A bundle forms when at least one side initiates.

              • active with active or passive
              • desirable with desirable or auto
              • on with on (nothing checks the far end)

              No initiator or mismatch

              Never forms

              Neither side initiates, or the protocols do not match.

              • passive with passive
              • auto with auto
              • LACP on one side, PAgP on the other
              • on with any non-on mode

              Mode on pairs only with mode on.

              A miscabled static bundle can create a loop because nothing checks the far end. Member ports need identical speed, duplex, VLAN or trunk mode, native VLAN and allowed list, or the mismatched port is suspended.
              HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP38 / 53

              Configure the bundle, then read the summary

              • Shared settings go on the Port-channel interface and propagate to members
              • Po1(SU): Layer 2 channel in use. An (I) or (s) member flag means stand-alone or suspended
              Ask: which protocol is on each side, are all members bundled (P), and do member and Port-channel settings match?
              HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP39 / 53

              Load balancing and limits

              • Hash picks the linkThe switch hashes source/destination MAC or IP (or combinations) to choose a member. Check with show etherchannel load-balance.
              • One flow, one linkAll packets of a flow take the same link, which preserves ordering.
              • Speed ceilingOne large transfer uses one member only. Four 1 Gb/s links do not make a 4 Gb/s TCP stream.
              • Identical membersSpeed, duplex, trunk mode, native VLAN and allowed list must match, or the port is suspended.
              • Up to 8 active membersLACP can hold extra standby links. Bundling across two switches needs StackWise, VSS or vPC.
              Choose the hash that spreads your traffic best. The cross-switch technologies are outside this lesson.

              Worked troubleshooting: same VLAN, no ping

              PC-A cannot ping PC-B in VLAN 10, but other VLANs work. Go bottom-up: link, VLAN, trunk, MAC table.

              HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP45 / 53

              Steps 1 and 2: access ports are fine, the trunk drops VLAN

              • Both access ports are in VLAN 10, active on both switches: the fault is in between
              • Trunk is up on 802.1Q, but SW1 allows only 20,150, so VLAN 10 never leaves SW1
              PC-A is on SW1 Gi0/1 and PC-B on SW2 Gi0/2, both meant for VLAN 10 in the same subnet. SW2's allowed list still shows 10,20,150. It fits an engineer who ran switchport trunk allowed vlan 20,150 without add.
              HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP46 / 53

              Step 3: the MAC tables show where the frame stopped

              Local host only

              SW1, VLAN 10

              Learned 0050.7966.6800 on Gi0/1 and nothing from PC-B.

                Local host only

                SW2, VLAN 10

                Learned 0050.7966.6801 on Gi0/2 and nothing from PC-A.

                  ARP never crossed

                  What happened

                  PC-A's ARP request, a broadcast, flooded in VLAN 10 on SW1 but never crossed the trunk. PC-B never saw it, so no reply came back.

                    Each switch learned only its local host in VLAN 10. This confirms the diagnosis from the trunk output.
                    HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP47 / 53

                    Step 4: add VLAN 10 back and verify

                    • The add keyword keeps the existing list intact
                    • PC-B's MAC now appears on trunk port Gi0/24
                    After a ping succeeds, PC-B's MAC appears on the trunk port Gi0/24 of SW1. Note the add keyword: without it the list would be replaced again.
                    HACKTHESOCLesson 102VLANs, trunks and STP48 / 53

                    Other causes with the same symptom

                    • VLAN missing on one switchAbsent from the allowed-and-active block of show interfaces trunk and from show vlan brief. Create it.
                    • Native VLAN mismatchUntagged traffic lands in different VLANs on each side, and the CDP mismatch log appears. Make both ends match.
                    • STP blockingshow spanning-tree vlan 10 reveals a blocked trunk.

                    Always compare both ends.

                    The same three commands rule these out. Two correct-looking switches can disagree on a single list, and the show commands tell you exactly where.

                    Key takeaways

                    1. 01A switch learns source MACs per VLAN, sends known unicast out one port, and floods unknown unicast and broadcasts within the VLAN.
                    2. 02VLANs are defined in the switch: access ports carry one untagged VLAN, trunks carry many using 802.1Q tags.
                    3. 03Hardcode port modes, match the native VLAN, and use the add keyword, because allowed vlan alone replaces the list.
                    4. 04VTP lets the higher revision overwrite a VLAN database with no merge. Prefer transparent mode, a password, or VTP v3.
                    5. 05Set the STP root on purpose, use RSTP, and put PortFast with BPDU Guard on edge ports and Root Guard toward the access layer.
                    6. 06Prefer LACP for EtherChannel, keep member settings identical, and troubleshoot by comparing both ends with show commands.

                    Check your understanding, question 1 of 3

                    A trunk carries VLANs 10, 20 and 150. An engineer types switchport trunk allowed vlan 30 to add VLAN 30. What is the result?

                    Without the add keyword the command overwrites the allowed list, so only VLAN 30 crosses the trunk and VLANs 10, 20 and 150 lose connectivity. IOS neither appends nor rejects it; the add keyword exists for this.

                    Choose an answer, or press R to reveal it.

                    Check your understanding, question 2 of 3

                    A used lab switch in your VTP domain (matching name) has revision 85 and few VLANs. Production is at revision 40. It is connected by a trunk. What happens?

                    VTP compares only the revision number. The higher revision replaces the receiving switch's VLAN database completely, deleting VLANs the lab switch lacks. Role does not matter, clients advertise too, and VTP never merges.

                    Choose an answer, or press R to reveal it.

                    Check your understanding, question 3 of 3

                    Access port Gi0/5 has PortFast and BPDU Guard enabled. A user plugs a small managed switch into the wall jack and BPDU Guard fires. What state is the port in?

                    BPDU Guard err-disables the port the moment a BPDU arrives, and it stays down until shutdown then no shutdown, or an errdisable recovery timer expires. Root-inconsistent is Root Guard; the 50-second blocking is classic STP timing.
