Domains, trees, forests, and what a trust is

The building blocks, and why the forest is the real boundary.

HACKTHESOCLesson 126AD trusts and cross-forest6 / 51

Four terms every trust discussion rests on

Domain

Administrative and replication unit with its own directory partition, DCs, krbtgt account and SID prefix.

    Tree

    Domains sharing a contiguous DNS namespace, e.g. corp.example.com and emea.corp.example.com.

      Forest

      One or more trees sharing a single schema, one Configuration partition and a global catalog.

        Trust

        A relationship letting a DC in one domain accept an authentication done by a DC in the other.

          A domain SID prefix is S-1-5-21 followed by three 32-bit numbers.

          A trust lets a user from one side be granted access to resources on the other side. Keep the vocabulary tight, the rest of the lesson depends on it.
          HACKTHESOCLesson 126AD trusts and cross-forest7 / 51
          The forest, not the domain, is the security boundary.

          If one domain in a forest is fully compromised, assume the forest root is reachable.

          Inside a forest every DC replicates the Configuration and Schema partitions, and a child Domain Admin controls the DCs that mint tickets. Microsoft's guidance is that domain boundaries cannot contain a determined, privileged attacker.
          HACKTHESOCLesson 126AD trusts and cross-forest8 / 51

          What a trust looks like as data

          Each trust is a trustedDomain object (TDO) in the System container of the domain partition.

          • trustPartner, flatNameThe DNS and NetBIOS names of the other side.
          • trustDirection1 inbound, 2 outbound, 3 bidirectional.
          • trustType, trustAttributesBit flags for transitivity, forest trust, selective authentication and SID filtering state.
          • securityIdentifierThe domain SID of the other side, which the filter uses later.
          • Inter-realm trust keyShared secret in the TDO, rotated about every 30 days. It protects referral tickets, so it is high-value.

          Ask: which trusts exist, in which direction, who administers the other side, and what is granted to foreign principals?

          A trust inventory is a detection prerequisite. You cannot call a cross-realm ticket anomalous if you never learned which realms are expected to talk. Ticket forgery and DCSync have their own lessons; here the focus is the trust boundary.

          Trust types, direction, and transitivity

          Six trust types, and the four answers that tell you what an attacker could have forged.

          HACKTHESOCLesson 126AD trusts and cross-forest10 / 51

          Six trust types and the properties a defender needs

          Trust typeHow it is createdTransitiveSID filtering
          Parent-childAutomatic with a new child domainYesNot applied (same forest)
          Tree-rootAutomatic when a new tree joinsYesNot applied (same forest)
          ShortcutManual, shortens the referral pathYesNot applied (same forest)
          ExternalManual: one domain, other forestNoOn by default (quarantine)
          ForestManual: mergers, partner forestsAcross both forests, not onwardOn by default
          RealmManual: MIT or other KerberosConfigurableNot applicable

          Parent-child and tree-root are two-way; the others can be one-way or two-way.

          Everything inside a forest is transitive and unfiltered. Filtering starts at the forest edge, which is why external and forest trusts are where the controls live.
          HACKTHESOCLesson 126AD trusts and cross-forest11 / 51

          Direction is the part people invert

          In a one-way trust where A trusts B, A is the trusting domain and B the trusted domain.

          Trusting domain (A)

          Holds the resources. Users in B can be authorized to resources in A.

            Its compromise matters

            Trusted domain (B)

            Its identities are believed by the trusting side, so its weakest point is A's exposure.

              The diagram arrow

              Points from trusting to trusted, while access flows the opposite way.

                A resource forest that trusts an acquired company's forest is exposed to that company's weakest domain.

                Warn students that diagrams invite the wrong reading. Ask which side holds the resources, then ask whose compromise hurts.
                HACKTHESOCLesson 126AD trusts and cross-forest12 / 51

                Transitivity decides how many doors an attacker has

                If A trusts B and B trusts C, then A trusts C automatically, for transitive trusts only.

                • Inside a forestParent-child and tree-root are transitive, so every domain implicitly trusts every other.
                • Forest trustsTransitive across both forests' domains but do not chain: forest 1 to 2 to 3 does not make 1 trust 3.
                • External trustsNon-transitive and name exactly one domain, so blast radius is a single domain pair.
                • Shortcut trustsSpeed up authentication but add a new path and a new trust key, and are often forgotten.
                When scoping, a transitive path gives more doors; a non-transitive external trust limits the pair. Every extra trust is an extra shared secret and an extra place for tickets to cross.
                HACKTHESOCLesson 126AD trusts and cross-forest13 / 51

                Four trustAttributes bits worth reading on every TDO

                FlagWhat it tells you
                0x20 within-forestNot SID-filtered
                0x8 forest-transitiveFiltered by default
                0x4 quarantined externalSID filtering is on
                0x10 cross-organizationSelective authentication is enabled

                Triage a cross-domain event by classifying first: same or different forest, direction, transitive path used, filtering applies or not.

                Reading these four bits across all TDOs is a fast, high-value audit that takes minutes. The four classification answers tell you what an attacker could have forged and what the control should have stripped.

                Section 3 of 9

                How authentication crosses a trust

                Referrals, inter-realm TGTs, the PAC and the SID filtering control point.

                HACKTHESOCLesson 126AD trusts and cross-forest15 / 51

                A user in ACQ opens a share in CORP: five hops

                ACQ is the trusted domain, CORP the trusting domain.

                1. 1User gets a TGT in ACQNormal logon: event 4768 on the ACQ DC.
                2. 2Client asks its own KDCThe ACQ KDC lacks the CORP service account but knows a trust exists.
                3. 3ACQ KDC returns a referralInter-realm TGT for krbtgt/CORP, sealed with the trust key. 4769 on the ACQ DC.
                4. 4CORP DC issues the ticketDecrypts the referral with the trust key, then a 4769 on the CORP DC.
                5. 5File server builds the sessionEvent 4624, logon type 3, for an account whose domain is ACQ.

                Across several domains step 3 chains: each hop is a separate inter-realm TGT and a separate 4769.

                The referral shows up as a 4769 whose service name looks like krbtgt/CORP rather than a normal host or cifs SPN. That odd service name is your marker of a trust crossing.
                HACKTHESOCLesson 126AD trusts and cross-forest16 / 51

                Kerberos proves who you are; the PAC says what you may do

                What the PAC holds

                Placed in the ticket by the home KDC: user SID, primary group, group SIDs, plus an ExtraSids list.

                  sIDHistory

                  Built for migrations so a migrated user keeps access to the old SID. Its values ride in ExtraSids.

                    At the resource side

                    The DC copies PAC SIDs into the access token, so a SID in the PAC is treated as real group membership.

                      Privilege rides in the PAC, not in the logon event. That is why the ticket contents matter more than the visible logon.
                      HACKTHESOCLesson 126AD trusts and cross-forest17 / 51

                      What the trusting side can check, and the one control that

                      • Trust-key signatureVerifiable: proves the ticket came from the partner KDC.
                      • The group listNot independently verifiable: the trusting DC must believe the partner.
                      • SID filteringCompares PAC SIDs with the domain SID in the TDO and strips foreign or privileged built-in SIDs. 4675 may be logged.
                      • Inside a forestFiltering is not applied to the same degree, so a child domain can reach its parent.
                      • TGT delegationDisabled by default across forest trusts since the 2019 updates. Verify the setting, do not assume.
                      The partner's honesty is the security assumption, so the receiving side needs a control that rejects SIDs the partner has no business asserting.

                      Trust discovery (T1482): normal or suspicious

                      Cheap, quiet enumeration, and the context that separates admin work from reconnaissance.

                      HACKTHESOCLesson 126AD trusts and cross-forest19 / 51

                      Four ways trusts get enumerated, none of them blocked

                      MITRE ATT&CK T1482, Domain Trust Discovery, is usually among the first actions after a foothold.

                      • LDAP queriesSearch for trustedDomain objects, or the trustAttributes and trustDirection attributes.
                      • Netlogon RPCReturns the trust list; used by nltest and the .NET and Win32 domain APIs.
                      • PowerShellGet-ADTrust and Get-ADForest, which are LDAP and ADWS under the hood.
                      • CollectorsBloodHound ingestors pull trusts with group and ACL data in one sweep.

                      Any authenticated user can read trust objects: no access-denied, no failed logon. Detect by who, from where, and what else.

                      The goal is planning: which neighbor can I reach, what does it trust, where do privileged foreign principals live. Discovery alone is low severity.

                      Baseline first: normal admin work versus suspicious

                      Normal

                      A small set of admin hosts, change windows and named service accounts. Monitoring agents and cloud sync products also read TDOs on a schedule.

                      • Domain admins and migration teams
                      • Scheduled reads by known agents

                      Suspicious

                      The same commands, wrong context.

                      • Non-IT workstation or server with no AD role
                      • Minutes after a first-seen logon or odd process tree
                      • Burst covering all trusts and foreign principals
                      • Host later authenticates across that trust
                      A document handler spawning a shell, or enumeration alongside privileged groups and SPNs, raises the odds. A burst covering everything is typical of an automated collector.

                      Why one owned domain threatens its neighbors

                      Attacker intent, described so you can recognize it, without tooling.

                      HACKTHESOCLesson 126AD trusts and cross-forest24 / 51
                      The attacker's question: what does a neighbor believe about me?

                      Starting position: full control of one domain via its krbtgt secret, a Domain Admin credential, or replicated directory secrets.

                      Control of a domain is control of the KDC that vouches for that domain's users. This section deliberately avoids tooling and procedure.
                      HACKTHESOCLesson 126AD trusts and cross-forest25 / 51

                      Two paths: child to root, and across forests

                      Child to forest root

                      Intra-forest

                      No SID filtering, so the parent DC accepts ExtraSids. Enterprise Admins (RID 519) there makes child compromise forest-root compromise.

                      • SID-History injection: T1134.005
                      • Forged ticket, Golden Ticket variant: T1558.001

                      Trust key or home krbtgt

                      Cross-forest

                      Same wish: have the other forest's DCs believe privileged SIDs. The filter setting decides the outcome.

                      • Filtering off or relaxed: foreign SIDs honored
                      • Filtering on: foreign SIDs stripped, little privilege

                      Filtering turns a catastrophic assumption (the partner never lies) into a bounded one (the partner speaks only for itself).

                      The intra-forest result is the practical proof that the forest is the boundary. Cross-forest, filtering can end up relaxed, for example sIDHistory explicitly enabled on the trust for a long-finished migration.
                      HACKTHESOCLesson 126AD trusts and cross-forest26 / 51

                      What filtering does not stop: three complementary controls

                      An attacker who owns the trusted domain can act as any principal legitimately granted access, with no forgery.

                      ControlWhat it limits
                      SID filteringWhich identities the partner can claim
                      Selective authenticationWhich resources those identities can even request
                      Least-privilege grantsWhat the permitted identities can do

                      A trusted-forest group in a local administrators group on your servers is reached with no forgery at all.

                      No single control is enough. Compromise of the trusted forest reaches legitimately granted access regardless of filtering.
                      HACKTHESOCLesson 126AD trusts and cross-forest27 / 51

                      Adjacent trust objectives to keep in mind

                      • Trust key theftReplicating the trust account object looks like DCSync targeting a trust account (T1003.006).
                      • sIDHistory persistenceA privileged SID added to an ordinary account so it is quietly privileged.
                      • Existing nestingAbusing foreign security principals and cross-domain group nesting already in place.

                      The value is in PAC contents and ticket path, invisible in user-facing logs. Compare claims with the directory and with history.

                      This is why detections must compare what the ticket claims against what the directory says is true and what is normal for that account and trust.

                      How to read the key signals

                      Strongest on a filtered trust

                      Event 4675

                      Legitimate users do not carry foreign privileged SIDs, so it is rarely benign.

                      • Benign: stale sIDHistory from an old migration
                      • Benign: broken group nesting; check the SID is a real group

                      Needs cross-forest logs

                      4769 without 4768

                      ACQ's DCs logged no 4768 for user X in the window, so the TGT was not issued by a real KDC exchange.

                      • Allow for renewal and TGT lifetime

                      Ticket versus directory

                      Mismatched identity

                      A user that does not exist in the claimed domain, or a domain name in the wrong case or form, is a documented forged-ticket tell.

                      • Visible in 4769 and 4624 fields
                      The 4675 benign cases both resolve by checking whether the SID is a real group. Top-privileged SIDs are never the stale migration artifact.

                      Design principles to teach your engineers

                      • Trust to your standardTreat a weakly administered acquired, partner or lab forest as if it were already hostile.
                      • Prefer a resource forestFiltered, selectively authenticated forest trusts beat default external trusts.
                      • Grant by group, audit groupsFew domain-local groups scoped to resources; review the ForeignSecurityPrincipals container regularly.
                      • End sIDHistory after migrationNever leave it enabled on a trust; clean it once old SIDs are not referenced in ACLs.
                      • Minimize privileged groupsDomain Admins from one forest should not sit in local administrator groups of another.
                      • Trusts in change controlEvents 4706, 4707, 4716 and 4865-4867 need a matching ticket, or they become an alert.
                      Microsoft has moved away from recommending dedicated administrative forests for most organizations, toward the enterprise access model with privileged access workstations. A trust to an admin forest concentrates risk and needs the strictest monitoring.

                            Incident response when a trust is abused

                            Scope by trust type first, then contain, then reset every credential that can still forge.

                            HACKTHESOCLesson 126AD trusts and cross-forest38 / 51

                            Scope is driven by the trust type, not the evidence so far

                            Domain Admin or equivalent

                            Any domain in a forest

                            Treat the entire forest as compromised. Forged SIDs are honored, so the attacker can be Enterprise Admin without logging on to the root.

                              Trust was used

                              Filtered forest or external

                              The trusted side is hostile; the trusting side is exposed to identities granted there. Scope by what foreign principals reach, plus 4675 or privileged access.

                                Filter did not protect

                                Filtering off or sIDHistory on

                                Assume the filter did not protect you and widen scope to everything the forged SIDs could access.

                                  Decide scope before the evidence is complete. Scoping too narrowly on the trust type is the costly mistake.
                                  HACKTHESOCLesson 126AD trusts and cross-forest39 / 51

                                  Containment sequence in practice

                                  1. 1Preserve evidenceDC logs from both sides, images of the pivot host, TDO attributes. Record before changing.
                                  2. 2Cut the path, not the businessSelective authentication with an empty allow list, or remove the trust if acceptable.
                                  3. 3Restore SID filteringEnable it and remove any sIDHistory exception no longer needed.
                                  4. 4Disable or reset accountsThose carrying suspicious SIDs; remove unexpected sIDHistory values.
                                  5. 5Hunt for persistenceBoth forests: new privileged accounts, GPO or task changes, AdminSDHolder, extra trusts.
                                  Removing a trust cuts the shared secret path but also breaks legitimate access, so coordinate with application owners. Notify the partner through a defined contact; contracts often set notification obligations.
                                  HACKTHESOCLesson 126AD trusts and cross-forest40 / 51

                                  Credential resets: cover every secret that can still forge

                                  CredentialWhy it mattersReset approach
                                  krbtgt of each domain with KDC-level accessForged tickets signed with it stay valid until rotatedReset twice per domain, with replication and max ticket lifetime between
                                  Inter-realm trust passwordsA forged referral using the trust key survives krbtgt resetsReset on both sides with the partner's admins; verify the trust
                                  Privileged and service account secretsCredentials seen in memory or replicatedRotate with the tiering model, Tier 0 first
                                  Directory service restore and break-glass credentialsUsed for persistenceRotate and verify storage
                                  After containment, decide on rebuild. When forest-level compromise with high privilege is confirmed, forest recovery or rebuild is the only way to be certain, and the trust is re-established last with filtering and selective authentication on from day one.

                                  A krbtgt reset does not kill trust-key forgeries, and a trust reset does not kill krbtgt forgeries. Cover both.

                                  One krbtgt reset is also not enough: the previous password is retained for validation, and the second reset removes it.

                                  These are the two easy-to-miss points, and they are exactly what the third quiz question tests.

                                  Key takeaways

                                  1. 01The forest, not the domain, is the security boundary: Domain Admin anywhere in a forest puts the whole forest in scope.
                                  2. 02Classify every cross-domain event first: same or different forest, direction, transitive path, and whether SID filtering applies.
                                  3. 03The trusting DC verifies the trust-key signature but must believe the PAC, so SID filtering is what bounds the partner.
                                  4. 04Trust discovery (T1482) is cheap and silent: judge it by who asks, from where, and what the account does next.
                                  5. 05Event 4675 and 4769 without 4768 are strong signals; the sequence across two domains is the detection.
                                  6. 06Reset krbtgt twice and the trust password on both sides: each kind of forgery survives the other reset.
