Insider threat is the category where a wrong classification costs a real person, not just the organisation.

Gigabytes to a personal cloud could be theft, a well-meaning backup, or an attacker using a compromised account. Same telemetry, three responses.

An analyst who reflexively treats all three as 'the employee did this' will hurt someone unfairly at least once. Define exfiltration, DLP, UEBA and data classification up front.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil5 / 80

Three terms this whole lesson depends on

  • ExfiltrationData leaving the organisation's control without authorisation, by email, USB, cloud upload or any other channel. It is the actual theft step.
  • DLP (Data Loss Prevention)Tooling that inspects data in motion or at rest and blocks or flags it when it matches a sensitive pattern or an explicit label.
  • UEBAUser and Entity Behaviour Analytics: ignores content, learns what normal looks like for each user and flags sharp deviations.

DLP asks what the data is. UEBA asks whether the behaviour is normal for this person.

A credit-card number is a pattern; 'Confidential' is a label. UEBA catches the cases DLP is structurally blind to, which is why the lesson needs both.

Three different problems wearing one label

The first job of any investigation is working out which one you are looking at.

HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil7 / 80

Malicious, negligent, or compromised

CategoryWhat it isAppropriate response
Malicious insiderA trusted person deliberately taking data or sabotaging systems; real but rarestInvestigation and disciplinary or legal action
Negligent insiderNo malicious intent: personal-cloud backup, a spreadsheet emailed home, a misconfigured shareRetraining, a policy conversation, possibly a process fix
Compromised accountAn attacker with a stolen credential or hijacked session, appearing to be the employeeTreat as account compromise, not employee wrongdoing
Negligent telemetry can look identical to malicious: same destination, same volume, even off-hours if the employee just works evenings. Treating every anomaly as malicious is a costly overreaction.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil8 / 80

One upload to personal cloud, three different stories

A workstation sends gigabytes of sensitive files to a personal cloud account. The telemetry is the same; the truth is not.

Malicious

A departing employee takes a client list on the way to a competitor. Deliberate, for gain, grievance or reward.

Negligent

A well-meaning employee backs up files to personal Google Drive before a laptop replacement, not understanding the policy.

Compromised

The employee never controlled it: an attacker compromised the account three days ago and now impersonates them perfectly.

Ask the room which story they would assume first. Most say malicious, which is the rarest of the three. This is the reflex the lesson is trying to break.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil9 / 80

Compromised-account giveaways are contextual, not identity-based

To the authentication system it is the employee's session, so look around it.

  • New location or deviceActivity from a location or device the employee has never used.
  • Out-of-pattern timingTiming inconsistent with any pattern the employee has ever shown.
  • Independent denialContacted through a channel independent of the account, the employee has no idea the activity occurred.
Confusing this category with a malicious insider is exactly the mistake that turns incident response into a wrongful accusation.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil10 / 80

Work out which category before anyone is named

  1. 1Read the device and locationHas this employee ever used this device or this location? A never-seen one points toward compromise.
  2. 2Compare the timingDoes the activity fit any pattern the employee has ever shown, or someone who just works evenings?
  3. 3Contact independentlyReach the employee by phone, in person or via their manager, never through the account in question.
  4. 4Pick the response that fitsDiscipline for malicious, retraining for negligent, account-compromise handling for compromised.
Identity telemetry cannot separate these: to the authentication system it is the employee's session. The separation comes from context around the session and from the independent contact.

The CERT taxonomy: four malicious crime types

Fraud, IP theft, sabotage and espionage behave differently and need different evidence.

HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil12 / 80

Four crime types, four different first places to look

Crime typeMotiveTypical perpetratorStrongest indicator
IT sabotageRevenge after a negative work eventTechnical staff, often sysadminsAccess retained past termination
FraudFinancial gainOften non-technical, business-process accessBusiness-process anomaly
IP theftAdvantage at a new employerEngineers, scientists, programmersThe pre-departure access spike
EspionageBenefit to a foreign entityAny role; may be recruited or coercedCoordination with an external party
From Carnegie Mellon's CERT National Insider Threat Center, which has catalogued cases since 2002. Corroborating evidence differs: a termination date, an approval log, a resignation date, external contact.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil13 / 80

How each crime type unfolds

IT sabotage

Planned in advance: deleting data, disabling systems, a logic bomb set for after departure. Saboteurs often still hold active credentials.

Fraud

Slow and incremental, like skimming small amounts over months, via approvals, records or refunds rather than a technical foothold.

IP theft

Departing technical staff copy source code, designs or research shortly before resigning, usually to gain an edge at a new employer.

Espionage

Rarest, highest stakes. Often coordinated with an external handler, so referred to counterintelligence or law enforcement.

Sabotage exploits unsynchronised termination and access revocation, which is exactly what same-day offboarding targets.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil14 / 80

Each crime type points to different corroborating evidence

Crime typeEvidence to pull firstWhy it matters
IT sabotageTermination or demotion date against access-revocation timeSaboteurs often still hold active credentials because the two were not synchronised
FraudApproval, refund and record-change logsFraud uses business-process access, so technical telemetry alone may look clean
IP theftResignation date against data-access volumeThis is the crime type the pre-departure spike was built to catch
EspionageEvidence of external contact or coordinationA compelling case is referred to counterintelligence or law enforcement
Fraud is slow, small and incremental, so the strongest detective control is often an unusual pattern of approvals or refunds rather than an endpoint signal.

The crime type does not replace the investigation. It tells you where to look first.

CERT found that treating malicious insiders as one group obscures more than it reveals: motives, profiles and mitigations differ sharply.

Know what telemetry each channel produces before you trust the silence.

Every channel has a blind spot built in

USB leaves no packet capture at all: the evidence lives entirely in endpoint telemetry, if device auditing was configured.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil19 / 80

Email, cloud sync and browser upload hide in everyday use

T1567

Email

Every employee uses it daily. Watch for a large attachment to an external, especially personal, address. Logs give sender, recipient, subject and attachment metadata, rarely content.

T1567.002

Cloud sync

Highest volume in real cases. Encrypted HTTPS to Google Drive, Dropbox or personal OneDrive, domains that also carry legitimate enterprise traffic.

T1567

Browser upload

Webmail, file-sharing or paste sites, or an attacker web form. Needs no sync client, just a browser and drag-and-drop on any managed device.

If the network does not block or inspect consumer cloud domains, a sync upload triggers no content inspection at all. The cloud-storage query later in this section is how you see it.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil20 / 80

USB leaves its evidence only on the endpoint

Exfiltration Over USB (T1052.001) produces no packet capture at all.

  1. 1Device insertedA USB insertion event is recorded on the workstation.
  2. 2Volume mountedA new removable volume appears with its own drive letter.
  3. 3Large copies to itFile-write events from network shares or local drives to the removable-volume path.
  4. 4Only if configuredEDR or Windows auditing captures these when explicitly set up. Without device control, little remains after the fact.
That is why USB is one of the hardest channels to investigate retrospectively. Section 4 names the exact events and the query that joins mount to file writes.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil21 / 80

Two channels people forget, one of them a near-proof of intent

  • DNS tunnelling (T1048.003)Data encoded into DNS query subdomains and sent a little at a time, looking like name resolution without DNS-specific inspection.
  • Why it signals intentIt needs real technical sophistication and is essentially never accidental: nobody backs up a spreadsheet this way by mistake.
  • PrintingNot in ATT&CK, still common. Print servers log document name, user, page count and destination printer.
  • When printing mattersA customer list or source code printed shortly before a resignation date.
The audit flagged T1048 as the parent; T1048.003 (Exfiltration Over Unencrypted Non-C2 Protocol) is the specific ID used here. Printing is the reminder that not every channel is digital-first.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil22 / 80

Which devices talk to consumer cloud storage, and how often

  • Counts connections per device, account and hour
  • DeviceNetworkEvents has no byte counter
  • For volume, use the proxy or firewall sent-bytes field (bytes_out / sentbyte)
Run the same hour against the proxy or firewall log to get upload volume. Exclude your approved enterprise tenants of the same providers.

Finding bulk downloads and syncs per user per hour

  • Counts distinct files, not events: sync clients retry and inflate raw counts
  • Sites and IPs show breadth: many libraries or an unexpected address
  • The 200 threshold is a placeholder: tune it to each user's history and peers
Measure volume as distinct files per hour against the user's own history and peers: more robust than a raw count, because sync clients retry. A burst of FileSyncDownloadedFull after syncing a whole library moves thousands of files with no download click.

  • Three operations cover anonymous and specific-address external sharing
  • TargetUserOrGroupName: does the recipient look like the employee's own address?

A forwarding rule is the quietest channel: once set, it exfiltrates every future message with no further action.

It appears as New-InboxRule or Set-InboxRule with ForwardTo, ForwardAsAttachmentTo or RedirectTo, or Set-Mailbox with ForwardingSmtpAddress.

Mailbox-level forwarding is something users can set themselves in Outlook on the web. The same artifact is a classic compromised-account indicator, so it does not tell you which insider category you are in.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil31 / 80

Finding inbox rules and mailbox settings that forward mail out

  • 30 days, not 14: a rule set weeks ago is still exfiltrating today
  • Parameters holds the destination: is it outside the company?
  • ClientIP helps separate the employee from a compromised session
Read the full Parameters value: the forwarding destination sits inside it alongside the rule's other settings. An internal address is not exfiltration.

USB on the endpoint: three sources of evidence

  1. 1Windows auditingEvent 6416 (Audit PNP Activity) records insertion; 4663 (Audit Removable Storage) each file access, path in ObjectName.
  2. 2Not on by defaultNeither subcategory is enabled out of the box. If nobody turned them on, those events do not exist.
  3. 3Defender for EndpointDeviceEvents with UsbDriveMounted: drive letter, product and serial in AdditionalFields. Join DeviceFileEvents.
  4. 4Purview Activity ExplorerFileCopiedToRemovableMedia with SHA-256, matched sensitive types, the copying app and the USB serial.
Activity Explorer also records FileUploadedToCloud, FilePrinted and FileCopiedToNetworkShare, and with 'Always audit file activity for devices' on it records common Office, PDF and archive types even when no policy matched.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil33 / 80

Joining a USB mount to the files written onto it

  • The mount event supplies the drive letter from AdditionalFields
  • Only writes to that drive letter within two hours of the mount count
  • Sample shows up to 10 file names to judge what left
The two-hour window ties the copies to that specific insertion. Read the sample names before the count: 50 holiday photos and 50 source files are very different findings.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil34 / 80

A Git push is the modern USB stick for engineers

Git sends packed objects, not files, so file-based DLP never sees it. Look in three layers.

Endpoint

git.exe with remote add, remote set-url or push to a remote that is not the corporate host.

Proxy

A POST to a path ending /git-receive-pack (the push) toward github.com, gitlab.com or bitbucket.org. /git-upload-pack is a clone or fetch.

Git platform audit

A spike in clones or archive downloads of repositories outside the person's team in the days before the push.

Direction matters on the proxy: receive-pack means data going up to the remote, upload-pack means data coming down. Only the first is exfiltration.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil35 / 80

Finding git pointed at a non-corporate remote

  • Replace the example host with your corporate Git host
  • A bare git push names an alias, not a host: noisy on its own
  • The remote add or set-url line that created the alias is the strongest lead
Corroborate any hit across the three layers before treating it as exfiltration: endpoint command line, proxy receive-pack POST, and the Git platform's own audit log.

Section 5 of 11

What DLP catches, and what it structurally cannot

Content inspection has inherent gaps, not vendor shortcomings.

HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil38 / 80

How DLP decides: matching content against rules

  1. 1Pattern matchingStructured data such as credit-card or national ID numbers, recognised by their format.
  2. 2Document fingerprintingMatches a file against a known sensitive file that was registered in advance.
  3. 3Keyword and label matchingMatches documents that were explicitly tagged, such as 'Confidential'.
  4. 4Act on the matchBlock outright or flag, depending on how each policy was configured.
This works whether DLP is a dedicated platform or the module inside a mail and collaboration suite. Within its scope it catches a large share of negligent and unsophisticated malicious exfiltration.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil39 / 80

Inside and outside DLP's reach

DLP catches

Content matched against rules: patterns, fingerprints, labels.

  • Hundreds of SSNs attached to personal email
  • A 'Confidential' label going to an unapproved cloud
  • A known, fingerprinted file leaving

DLP cannot catch

Anything with no matching file or pattern to inspect.

  • Paraphrased or retyped figures
  • A phone photo of the screen
  • An encrypted or password-protected archive
  • Novel IP never fingerprinted

Treat DLP alerts as one input among several, never the sole insider detection.

An encrypted archive can at best be flagged by metadata, an unusually large encrypted upload, which is a much weaker and noisier signal than content matching.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil40 / 80

Each blind spot is structural, not a vendor shortcoming

MethodWhy content inspection misses it
Paraphrased or retyped figuresThe information passed through a human mind and came out reworded: no file or pattern to match
Phone photo of the screenThe exfiltration never touches a monitored system, network or device
Encrypted or password-protected archiveThe engine cannot see inside; at best metadata flags an unusually large encrypted upload
Novel IP never fingerprintedFingerprinting needs the content registered before it can be recognised leaving
New source code, a not-yet-filed patent draft or an unreleased design are the most valuable things to steal and the least likely to be fingerprinted.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil41 / 80

Labels decide both detection and severity

DLP acts only on labels

In Purview: sensitivity labels (typically Public, General, Confidential, Highly Confidential) applied by users or auto-labelling, plus sensitive information types matched by content.

Severity follows the label

The same 500 MB upload is routine for public marketing assets and a major incident for Highly Confidential source code.

Gaps are findings

Unlabelled data where labelled data should be is exactly the content DLP will let through without a match.

The label, or the sensitive information types matched, is one of the first fields to read on any DLP or exfiltration alert.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil42 / 80
Treat DLP alerts as one input among several, never as the sole insider detection.

Behavioural indicators look at patterns of access and activity rather than content, which is exactly what catches the cases DLP is blind to.

A quiet DLP console is not evidence that nothing left. It may only mean nothing left in a form DLP can read.

Section 6 of 11

Clipboard, screenshots and steganography

Three channels that defeat content inspection by design.

HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil44 / 80

How each channel slips past content inspection

Clipboard

Copy from a sensitive document, paste into a personal webmail draft, messaging app or note service. No file moves, so file-based DLP and fingerprinting see nothing.

Screenshot

A native OS screenshot or snipping tool, then the image is saved or emailed. To the engine it is just an image, unless OCR runs on outbound images.

Steganography

Data hidden in the low-order bits of an image, audio or video file. The carrier passes the eye and file-type checks.

Many DLP deployments do not run OCR by default because of processing overhead, and steganalysis has to inspect byte-level statistics rather than the rendered content.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil45 / 80

Hard to catch, and the best control for each

ChannelWhat makes it hard to catchBest available control
ClipboardNever touches a file; copy and paste onlyEndpoint DLP with clipboard-event monitoring (high tuning cost)
ScreenshotContent is visual, not text-matchable, unless OCR is appliedDLP with OCR on outbound image attachments
SteganographyPayload in a carrier file's low-order bits; carrier looks normalSteganalysis tooling; behavioural and volume indicators as fallback
Clipboard monitoring would flag a copy from a Restricted document followed by a paste into an unmanaged browser tab, but few deployments run it because of noise.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil46 / 80
When content inspection is structurally blind, behaviour is often the only signal left.

Steganography, like DNS tunnelling, is close to impossible to do by accident: finding it is a strong signal of intent.

A 'vacation photo' emailed home that carries a customer spreadsheet defeats every content control in this lesson. The DLP engine only ever sees an image.

Section 7 of 11

Behavioural indicators and the pre-departure pattern

Patterns of access and activity, not the content being accessed.

HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil48 / 80
The pre-departure access spike has the strongest evidence base of any single indicator.

Access and download volume rise in the days to weeks before an exit, often reaching well outside the person's normal scope.

A sales employee pulling the full customer database rather than their own accounts, or an engineer downloading repos they never opened. Mature programs correlate HR departure dates against access volume.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil49 / 80

What the spike looks like: reaching outside normal scope

Normal for the role

Access that matches the job and the person's history.

  • Sales: queries on their own assigned accounts
  • Engineer: the repositories they work in
  • Steady volume within their baseline

Pre-departure spike

Read access they always had, used in a way they never have.

  • Sales: pulls the full customer database
  • Engineer: downloads repos never opened before
  • Volume rises in the days to weeks before exit
The point is not that the access was forbidden. It was permitted all along; what changed is the scope and the timing relative to a departure date.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil50 / 80

How a mature program uses the departure date

  1. 1HR reports the eventA resignation, a termination notice or another known departure date.
  2. 2Look back over the windowPull that person's data-access and download volume for the preceding days and weeks.
  3. 3Compare to their baselineIs the volume and scope materially above what they normally touch?
  4. 4Weight it accordinglyA spike in this window counts for more than the same spike with no departure on record.
This correlation needs HR data flowing to the insider-risk team in a documented, sanctioned way. It is a designed control, not an analyst browsing HR files on a hunch.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil51 / 80

Other indicators gain weight only in combination

  • Outside job functionAccess to systems or data with no role or project change to explain it.
  • Shift to off-hoursOff-hours activity from someone whose pattern is strictly business hours.
  • Disproportionate bulkOne analyst pulling an entire multi-year customer database in a session, not incremental queries.
  • Evasion toolingA personal VPN, incognito mode for non-business uploads, or pausing an EDR or DLP agent before an exfil event.
None is conclusive alone. Pausing an EDR or DLP agent shortly before an exfiltration event is among the heavier ones, but still needs the surrounding evidence.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil52 / 80

Resist confirmation bias: one indicator, many innocent stories

Single indicatorInnocent explanationsTreat it as
One off-hours loginA genuinely urgent deadline, a time-zone differenceA prompt to gather context
One large downloadA legitimate but unusual project needA prompt to gather context
Access outside job functionA change in role or project assignmentA question: check for that change first

Once one indicator is found, the pull is to build on it. Gather context instead.

Confirmation bias is the failure mode here: an analyst who has decided the person is guilty reads every later event as proof.

Section 8 of 11

The ethical and legal weight of naming an employee

A process that can end a career needs a higher standard of care than malware triage.

HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil54 / 80
Once an investigation names an employee, you are taking part in a process that can end a career.

It can cost someone their job, damage their reputation or lead to criminal referral, so the standard of care is higher than for triaging a malware alert.

Most of this course stays technical from start to finish. This section cannot, and analysts who treat it as overhead are the ones who create legal exposure.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil55 / 80

Write the fact, not the conclusion

The fact-based note is verifiable, defensible and useful to HR and Legal. The conclusion pre-judges a case where the employee has a right to explain, and may even be a compromised-account victim.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil56 / 80

Why the conclusion note becomes a liability

Fact-based note

Times, volume, device, destination, baseline and HR context. Verifiable, defensible and useful to HR and Legal without editorialising.

Conclusion-based note

A verdict written before the process, including the employee's own explanation, which they are entitled to give. If wrong, it harms them and exposes the organisation.

The worst case: the 'thief' in the note was the innocent victim of a compromised account.

Read the fact-based note again: every clause can be checked against a log or an HR record. The conclusion cannot be checked against anything.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil57 / 80

What HR and Legal bring that a SOC analyst cannot

ContextWhy it matters
Protected class or protected activityAnti-retaliation compliance applies regardless of what the technical evidence shows
Policies and signed agreementsWhat acceptable use and data handling the employee actually agreed to
Monitoring and privacy lawVaries enormously: US at-will norms versus EU/GDPR-influenced works councils
Evidentiary standardChain of custody required if the matter reaches a court or employment tribunal
Bring them in early, not after you have reached a conclusion. This is not box-checking: it is context the SOC structurally cannot have and should not try to substitute for.

Offboarding as a security control

Theft spikes around departure, while access is still valid.

HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil72 / 80

The two moments of risk

The notice period

Legitimate access, shifted loyalty. "Keeping a copy of my projects" happens here. Heightened monitoring must be documented and HR-sanctioned, never ad-hoc snooping.

Access outliving employment

Accounts not disabled on the last day, devices still enrolled, forgotten SaaS logins, shared credentials never rotated.

Good offboarding is a checklist executed on time: disable all access on the last day, ideally through one identity provider, reclaim and wipe devices, rotate shared secrets, and review recent activity before deleting the account.
HACKTHESOCLesson 32Insider threat and exfil73 / 80

Offboarding is a checklist executed on time, not a favour

  1. 1Disable all access on the last dayAD account, email, VPN and every SaaS app, ideally through one identity provider so one disable cascades.
  2. 2Reclaim and wipe devicesIncluding personal devices still enrolled in management.
  3. 3Rotate shared secretsAny shared credential the person knew, so it cannot outlive their employment.
  4. 4Review recent activity firstCheck for the pre-departure pattern before the account is deleted, while evidence is intact.
IT sabotage cases most often exploit the gap this checklist closes: credentials still active after termination. Forgotten SaaS logins are the usual hole when there is no single identity provider.
