Network types by scale: PAN, LAN, CAN, MAN and WAN

A network type answers one question: how big is this network, and who owns it?

HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices6 / 53

Five classic scales, from one person to a continent

Scale and ownership decide latency, who can hand you logs, and how far you can trust the wire.

TypeRange and ownerLatencyWhere you meet it
PAN (Personal)About 1 to 10 m (class 2), ~100 m (class 1); one personA few msPhone tether, Bluetooth peripherals, smartwatch pairing
LAN (Local)Floor or building, copper to 100 m per segment; the organizationWell under 1 msSwitch port logs, VLANs, lateral movement between workstations
CAN (Campus)Several buildings, 1 to 5 km; the organization owns or leases fiberAbout 1 msUniversity or hospital campus, building fiber, campus Wi-Fi
MAN (Metropolitan)City or metro, 5 to 50 km; carrier or municipality, leased outAbout 1 to 5 msMetro Ethernet or dark fiber linking offices in one city
WAN (Wide)Countries and continents; carriers, capacity is leasedAbout 10 to 300 ms (satellite to 600)Site-to-site VPN, MPLS or SD-WAN, the internet edge
The dividing line is ownership as much as distance. The same Ethernet frames run in a LAN, over metro fiber and across a WAN circuit; what changes is distance, the operator and the SLA.

Who owns the segment decides what you can see

LAN and campus cabling

You own it

You can put sensors anywhere on your own LAN and campus cabling.

    Carrier MAN and WAN

    You lease it

    You see only what arrives at your edge device (router or firewall) plus whatever the carrier chooses to report.

      Plus equipment

      Latency is physics

      Light in fiber covers about 200 km per millisecond. Tel Aviv to the US East Coast cannot beat about 80 ms; real paths measure 100 to 130 ms.

        A LAN has no such delay, so latency is a clue to the path a packet really took.

        Boundaries are fuzzy: a large hospital may call its campus a LAN. What matters is the question you are asking: who runs this segment and can I get its logs?
        HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices8 / 53

        Two misconceptions that mislead analysts

        • 'WAN means the internet'No. A WAN is any wide-area network. A private MPLS circuit between two company offices is a WAN and never touches the public internet.
        • 'A PAN is harmless'A phone hotspot or paired Bluetooth device bypasses the corporate firewall. A laptop on the LAN plus a hotspot is an unmonitored bridge.

        Scenario: a workstation sends data via a hotspot. It never crossed the firewall, so pivot to endpoint telemetry.

        Empty firewall logs here are expected, not suspicious. The tether path simply does not pass the firewall.
        HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices9 / 53

        Three questions to ask about any network path

        1. 1Which scale does it cross?Did it stay inside one LAN, cross the campus backbone, or leave through the WAN edge?
        2. 2Who owns the segment?Is that the team or provider that can give me logs?
        3. 3Is the latency consistent?A reply in 2 ms from a host claimed to be on another continent should make you question the claim.
        These three questions return in every later section in new forms. Latency is the one students forget.

        WLAN, SAN and the internet as a network of networks

        Three types defined by the medium, the purpose, or how many networks are joined together.

        HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices11 / 53

        WLAN, SAN and the internet in one view

        IEEE 802.11

        WLAN

        A LAN whose last hop is radio (Wi-Fi 5, 6/6E, 7).

        • Indoor range 30 to 50 m per access point
        • Shared half-duplex air, CSMA/CA
        • Each SSID usually maps to a VLAN

        Block storage

        SAN

        A dedicated high-speed network connecting servers to block storage, separate from the user LAN.

        • Fibre Channel, iSCSI (TCP 3260), NVMe over Fabrics
        • Well under 1 ms, lossless
        • Not a NAS: a NAS serves files via SMB or NFS

        Network of networks

        The internet

        Tens of thousands of autonomous systems, each with an AS number, exchanging reachability via BGP.

        • No one owns it
        • Best effort: no guaranteed delivery or path
        • Untrusted by definition
        A Wi-Fi laptop and a wired desktop can sit in the same broadcast domain, because the SSID is mapped onto a VLAN of the wired LAN.
        HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices12 / 53

        Radio adds identifiers and threats that wired ports lack

        Anyone within radio range can hear a WLAN signal.

        TermWhat it means for you
        SSIDThe named network clients join; one SSID can be broadcast by dozens of access points
        BSSIDIdentifies the radio, usually the access point's MAC address
        Rogue AP, evil-twin SSIDAn unauthorized access point, or a copied SSID that lures clients
        Forged deauthenticationManagement frames that kick a client off; mitigated by 802.11w protected management frames
        Ask: which SSID and access point did this device use, and is that SSID one we operate?
        HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices13 / 53
        Ransomware operators go after storage arrays, snapshots and backup targets.

        Watch for SAN zoning changes, deleted snapshots and unexpected admin logins to a storage array. iSCSI flows from a user subnet are almost always wrong.

        Ask: is this storage or backup traffic crossing a boundary it should never cross?
        HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices14 / 53

        Intranet, extranet and VPN are not the same thing

        TermWhat it is
        IntranetA private network using internet technology (web, email), reachable only by the organization
        ExtranetAn intranet slice deliberately shared with partners or customers, usually behind authentication
        VPNNot a network type: a virtual overlay that encrypts and encapsulates traffic over a public one

        Ask: which AS and country does this external address belong to, and does that fit the business relationship?

        Every external source address is controlled by someone you do not know, which is why the internet is treated as untrusted.

        Topologies: bus, star, ring, mesh, tree and hybrid

        A topology is the shape of a network, and there are two views of it.

        HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices16 / 53

        Physical shape versus logical flow

        Cables and devices

        Physical topology

        How cables and devices are actually laid out: what you would see in a wiring closet.

          Data flow

          Logical topology

          How data actually flows between nodes, regardless of the cabling.

            A network can be physically one shape and logically another. Mixing the two up is the most common mistake.

            Hold this thought for the Ethernet section: a hub is physically a star but logically a bus.
            HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices17 / 53

            The classic shapes and how each one fails

            ShapeHow it worksWeakness
            BusOne shared backbone cable (10BASE5, 10BASE2 coax, 50-ohm terminators)Constant collisions; a break or one bad NIC corrupts the segment
            StarEvery node has its own cable to a central hub or switchThe central device is a single point of failure
            RingEach node connects to exactly two neighbors in a loopSurvives one break only with dual rings or a ring protocol
            MeshMany links; full mesh links every node to every otherMost resilient, most expensive
            TreeA star of stars: access, distribution, coreUpstream failure isolates everything beneath it
            HybridA combination of the aboveDepends on the mix

            Every real enterprise network is hybrid: star in the closet, tree across the building, partial mesh across the WAN.

            Rings survive in carrier networks: Token Ring (802.5) passed a token, FDDI used two counter-rotating fiber rings, and SONET/SDH rings and Ethernet Ring Protection (G.8032) restore service in tens of milliseconds.
            HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices18 / 53

            Full mesh grows with the square of the node count

            • Cost and complexity climb quickly as nodes are added
            • Partial mesh is the practical choice for WANs
            Try a quick class check: 8 sites need 8 x 7 / 2 = 28 links. That is why real WANs use a partial mesh.
            HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices19 / 53

            Why the analyst cares: topology tells you where traffic must

            Tree

            North-south traffic funnels through the core, so a sensor there sees a lot.

              Mesh

              Many paths, so a sensor on one link may miss half the conversation (asymmetric routing).

                Star

                The central device is where you place visibility, and where an attacker aims to cause an outage.

                  Ask: what is the single point of failure? If this link fails, will my sensor still see the path?

                  Topology answers 'why did this device see, or miss, that traffic'.

                  Modern Ethernet and the spine-leaf data center

                  Why today's wiring is a star, why loops are dangerous, and how data centers are built.

                  HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices21 / 53

                  From coax bus to switched star

                  StageWiringLogical behavior
                  Early Ethernet10BASE5 and 10BASE2 coax, every station on one wireA true bus
                  1990: 10BASE-T on a hubIEEE twisted pair: a star centered on a hubThe hub repeats every signal to every port: one shared collision domain
                  Switched EthernetA star: one cable per device to a switch portDedicated full-duplex path per port; frames go only toward their destination

                  Why it won: a failed cable hits one device, bandwidth is not shared, and VLANs, port security and 802.1X work per port.

                  Structured cabling: wall jack, patch panel, switch, with an MDF and an IDF per floor. Copper runs are limited to 100 m, longer runs use fiber.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices22 / 53

                  The loop problem and Spanning Tree

                  1. 1Add redundant uplinksA single link failure becomes survivable.
                  2. 2Redundant links form loopsEthernet frames have no hop counter, so a broadcast circulates forever and multiplies.
                  3. 3A broadcast stormIt can saturate the network within seconds.
                  4. 4Spanning Tree blocks portsIEEE 802.1D (Rapid STP is 802.1w) blocks redundant ports and unblocks one when a link fails.

                  Signs of a loop: broadcast flood, switch CPU spikes, MACs flapping between ports, topology-change messages.

                  A loop can be accidental, such as someone plugging both ends of a patch cable into one switch, or deliberate.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices23 / 53

                  Spine-leaf: two hops, parallel paths, simple scaling

                  Built for east-west traffic (server to server), not just users to servers.

                  • ECMP spreads traffic over all spines; no link is blocked as STP would
                  • Add spines for bandwidth, add leaves for more ports
                  The fabric is routed at Layer 3 between leaf and spine, which is why STP is not blocking links. Isolation is usually an overlay such as VXLAN with a BGP EVPN control plane.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices24 / 53

                  What spine-leaf means for the analyst

                  East-west blind spot

                  East-west traffic inside a data center usually never touches a perimeter firewall. Without microsegmentation or an internal firewall, lateral movement can be nearly invisible to network logs.

                    Overlay encapsulation

                    A capture on a spine shows the outer addresses of the leaf devices, not the real servers. You need to know where decapsulation happens.

                      Ask: where does east-west traffic get inspected? Which devices see the original addresses, and which see only tunnel endpoints?

                      Tell students to ask for the network diagram first when a data-center alert has no firewall evidence.

                      Section 5 of 9

                      Layer 1 and 2 devices: NIC, hub, bridge and switch

                      Devices differ by how much of each packet they read. These read the least.

                      HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices26 / 53

                      Four devices at the physical and data-link levels

                      NIC

                      The endpoint's door into the network: card, chip, USB dongle, Wi-Fi radio or virtual NIC.

                      • 48-bit MAC; first 24 bits (OUI) name the vendor
                      • Promiscuous mode accepts everything: how sniffers work
                      • MAC can be spoofed or randomized

                      Hub

                      Layer 1 multiport repeater: repeats a signal out of every other port.

                      • One collision domain, half-duplex, shared bandwidth
                      • Every host sees every frame: sniffing is trivial
                      • Obsolete; not the same as a passive TAP

                      Bridge

                      Layer 2: learns which MAC lives on which side and forwards frames only when needed.

                      • Split big collision domains into smaller ones
                      • Survives as Linux bridges, virtual switches
                      • An access point bridges Wi-Fi to Ethernet

                      Switch

                      A multiport bridge in hardware (ASICs), the standard Layer 2 device.

                      • MAC table (CAM): MAC, VLAN, port
                      • Known unicast out one port
                      • Unknown unicast and broadcast flood the VLAN
                      A 10 Mbps hub divides 10 Mbps among everyone. Hubs still turn up in old industrial gear.
                      HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices27 / 53

                      How a switch learns and forwards

                      1. 1Frame arrivesThe switch reads the source MAC and records MAC, VLAN and port in its table.
                      2. 2Look up the destination MACThe switch checks the table for where that address lives.
                      3. 3Known unicastThe frame goes out one port only.
                      4. 4Unknown unicast or broadcastFlooded out all other ports in the VLAN.

                      Entries age out (300 seconds by default on many Cisco switches) and tables hold a limited number of entries.

                      The limited table size is exactly what the next slide's attack exploits.
                      HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices28 / 53

                      MAC flooding turns a switch back into a hub

                      Normal switch

                      The table holds real MACs. Known unicast leaves one port, so one host cannot passively hear another's traffic.

                        Under MAC flooding

                        A tool such as macof sends thousands of frames with random source MACs. The table fills and unicast is flooded like a hub, so the attacker sniffs.

                          The fix

                          Port security: limit the number of MACs allowed per port.

                            Signature in the logs: thousands of new source MACs learned on one port within seconds.

                            This is the pattern in the third quiz question.
                            HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices29 / 53

                            Telemetry from NICs and switches, and the device that gives

                            DeviceWhat you get
                            NICEndpoint inventories: adapter name, MAC, link speed
                            Managed switchSyslog (port events, MAC flapping, STP changes, 802.1X), SNMP counters, some flow records

                            Ask: which switch and port is this MAC on? When did it first appear, and did it move between ports?

                            A rogue unmanaged switch under a desk is a classic way to extend a network around controls.

                            Layer 3, wireless and edge devices

                            The seams of the network: router, L3 switch, access point, WLC and modem.

                            HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices31 / 53

                            Router and Layer 3 switch: both route, in different places

                            Layer 3

                            Router

                            Forwards packets between different networks by destination IP, using the routing table (longest matching prefix).

                            • Each interface is its own broadcast domain
                            • Default gateway: first hop out of the network
                            • Routes: connected, static, or dynamic (OSPF, BGP)
                            • ACLs are stateless, not a stateful firewall

                            Heart of most campus networks

                            Layer 3 switch

                            A switch that also routes in hardware, at wire speed.

                            • Inter-VLAN routing through SVIs, one per VLAN
                            • More ports, lower cost per port than a router
                            • Fewer WAN interface types, weaker VPN

                            With no ACL on the L3 switch, any VLAN reaches any other and nothing is logged, because traffic never crosses the firewall.

                            A VLAN is a segmentation boundary only until something routes between VLANs.
                            HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices32 / 53

                            Access point, WLC and modem

                            • Access point (AP)Bridges 802.11 radio to wired Ethernet and maps each SSID to a VLAN.
                            • WLCManages many APs, commonly over CAPWAP tunnels (RFC 5415, UDP 5246 and 5247).
                            • What the WLC centralizes802.1X client authentication with RADIUS, roaming, RF management, and rogue AP detection (WIPS).
                            • ModemConverts between your digital signals and the access medium: DOCSIS, DSL, or an ONT for fiber.
                            • Residential gatewayModem, router, switch, AP and basic firewall in one box. Remote workers' visibility starts at the VPN or endpoint agent.
                            People say 'modem' when they mean the whole box. You cannot see into consumer gateways at home.
                            HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices33 / 53

                            Edge devices: the boundary each creates and what it logs

                            DeviceMain layerBoundary it createsTypical telemetry
                            Router3Broadcast domain per interfaceACL hits, interface and routing events, config changes
                            L3 switch2 and 3Per-VLAN domains plus routingACL logs, port events
                            AP / WLC1 and 2Radio edgeAssociation, authentication, rogue AP alerts
                            Modem / gateway1 and 2 (more in combos)Provider edgeLittle, mostly link status

                            Ask: which device routes between these two segments, and does it log? Is the client on a managed AP or an unknown one?

                            Seams are where telemetry and attacks concentrate.

                            Section 7 of 9

                            Firewall, load balancer, proxy and IDS/IPS

                            Devices that decide whether and how traffic passes. They produce the logs a SOC reads most.

                            HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices35 / 53

                            Firewall, load balancer and proxy

                            Firewall

                            Enforces policy between zones (inside, DMZ, outside) with stateful inspection and a session table.

                            • Return traffic for allowed connections is automatic
                            • Top-to-bottom rules; final rule is implicit deny
                            • Sees only traffic that crosses it

                            Load balancer

                            One virtual address in front of a server pool; health checks remove failed members.

                            • L4 uses IP and port; L7 reads HTTP, may end TLS
                            • Backend sees the LB address as client
                            • Recover the real IP from X-Forwarded-For

                            Proxy

                            Acts on behalf of someone else and opens its own connection to the destination.

                            • Forward proxy: logs outbound web access, may cache
                            • Reverse proxy: fronts servers, often hosts a WAF
                            • Destination sees the proxy's address
                            Proxy logs are where the real user-to-URL link lives.
                            HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices36 / 53
                            Denies show attempts. Allows show what actually got through, and are usually the more important logs.

                            NGFWs add application identification, user identity, URL filtering, TLS inspection and intrusion prevention.

                            Firewall telemetry: allow and deny decisions, session start and end, bytes, rule name, zones, application and user.
                            HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices37 / 53

                            IDS watches a copy; IPS sits in the path

                            IDS (passive)

                            Analyzes a copy of traffic from a SPAN port or a TAP and raises alerts. It cannot stop anything.

                            • Alerts carry signature ID, message, addresses, action
                            • A match shows an attempt, not success

                            IPS (inline)

                            Traffic passes through it, so it can drop or reset a flow.

                            • Fails open: traffic continues, no protection
                            • Fails closed: traffic stops

                            Detection is by signature (Suricata or Snort rules) and by anomaly. Check whether the traffic was blocked upstream and whether the target responded.

                            An IDS alert about an exploit attempt does not prove compromise. Correlate with firewall, server or load balancer logs and endpoint telemetry.

                            Section 8 of 9

                            Collision domains and broadcast domains

                            Two terms for how far a transmission reaches. Mixing them up leads to the wrong diagnosis.

                            HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices40 / 53

                            A collision domain is not a broadcast domain

                            Half-duplex shared media

                            Collision domain

                            Devices sharing a medium where two transmitting at once corrupt each other: bus, hubs, Wi-Fi.

                            • A hub: all ports form one
                            • A bridge or switch: one per port
                            • Full duplex: collisions cannot happen

                            Layer 2 reach

                            Broadcast domain

                            Devices that receive a Layer 2 broadcast (FF:FF:FF:FF:FF:FF) sent by any one of them.

                            • A switch floods broadcasts within the VLAN
                            • A VLAN splits one switch into several domains
                            • A router interface bounds a domain

                            A collision counter above zero on a modern switch port points to a duplex mismatch or an old device.

                            Routing between VLANs lets two broadcast domains communicate without merging.
                            HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices41 / 53

                            Counting domains by device

                            Device or setupCollision domainsBroadcast domains
                            Hub with N ports11

                            Wi-Fi AP on one channel1 (shared air)1 per SSID-to-VLAN mapping

                            HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices43 / 53

                            Why domain size matters, and two mistakes to avoid

                            Big domain

                            Every host processes every broadcast, and a loop turns it into a storm.

                              Small domain

                              A smaller blast radius, and traffic must cross a Layer 3 device where it can be filtered and logged.

                                'A switch separates networks'

                                It separates collision domains, not broadcast domains.

                                  'VLANs are secure alone'

                                  Bad trunks, native VLAN tricks or double tagging (VLAN hopping) can leak; an unfiltered L3 switch routes freely.

                                    Same-domain attacks: ARP spoofing, rogue DHCP servers, LLMNR and NBNS poisoning, discovery scans.

                                    Ask: are the attacker and the victim in the same broadcast domain? If not, which routed device did the traffic cross, and what does its log show?

                                    Where the analyst sees each device in the logs

                                    A device logs only what it forwards, decides or observes. Know what its story leaves out.

                                    HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices45 / 53

                                    What switch and router logs look like

                                    Two typical high-level messages.

                                    • Switch: an unexpected MAC on a port, often followed by err-disabled
                                    • Router: the attempt reached the router and the ACL filtered it
                                    Other typical content: AP/WLC logs association, auth reason codes and rogue APs; firewalls log one record per session or deny; load balancers and proxies hold the client identity others lost; IDS/IPS logs signature ID, severity and alert versus block.

                                    Blind spots every analyst should remember

                                    • Same VLAN, same switchTraffic never reaches a firewall or router. Only the switch, an endpoint agent, or a segment sensor sees it.
                                    • Encrypted or tunneledVPN and VXLAN hide inner details from devices on the path.
                                    • NAT, proxy, load balancerHosts appear under the intermediary's address. Find its own log to recover the client.
                                    • No logs at allConsumer gateways and unmanaged switches produce nothing.
                                    • Clocks must agreeA few minutes of drift misleads correlation. Confirm NTP before building a timeline.
                                    Time synchronization is the blind spot most often skipped in a rush.
                                    HACKTHESOCLesson 98Network types and devices48 / 53

                                    A reusable questioning framework

                                    1. 1Which device saw this?And at what point in the path?
                                    2. 2What does it see and decide?And what is invisible to it?
                                    3. 3Who else should have logged it?Which neighboring device saw the same activity from another angle?
                                    4. 4Does the topology explain it?Star, tree or spine-leaf: why this device did or did not see the traffic.
                                    5. 5Which broadcast domain?Which domain are the endpoints in, and which routed device sits between?

                                    Scenario: a workstation scans internal hosts but the firewall shows nothing. Same VLAN, so check the switch, endpoint agent and any internal sensor.

                                    The conclusion is never that the scan did not happen. It is that this device could not have seen it.

                                    Key takeaways

                                    1. 01A network type answers how big the network is and who owns it, and ownership decides who can hand you logs.
                                    2. 02Topology is the shape: star in the closet, tree across the building, partial mesh across the WAN, spine-leaf in the data center.
                                    3. 03Hubs repeat to all ports, switches forward by MAC, routers by IP, firewalls by policy: each sees only what crosses it.
                                    4. 04A switch gives each port its own collision domain; only a VLAN or a router interface bounds a broadcast domain.
                                    5. 05Same-VLAN and east-west traffic never reaches a perimeter firewall, so pivot to the switch, endpoint agent or sensor.
                                    6. 06An alert shows an observation, not an outcome: ask which device saw it, what it cannot see, and who else logged it.

                                    Check your understanding, question 1 of 3

                                    You investigate a suspected scan from one workstation to many others. Perimeter firewall logs show nothing. All hosts share one VLAN on one access switch. What is the best conclusion?

                                    Same-VLAN frames are switched at Layer 2 and never reach a firewall or router, so an empty firewall log is expected. Absence of evidence from one device is not evidence of absence. Use the switch, an agent or a sensor.

                                    Choose an answer, or press R to reveal it.

                                    A switch floods unicast out of many ports, a sniffer captures other hosts' conversations, and logs show thousands of new source MACs learned on one port within seconds. What is happening?

                                    Thousands of random source MACs from one port filling the CAM table is MAC flooding (for example macof). A full table makes the switch flood unicast, and port security is the fix. STP blocking, routing tables and hubs do not explain it.

                                    A router has three interfaces. Each connects to a separate switch carrying a single VLAN with 40 hosts. How many broadcast domains exist, and why?

                                    Routers do not forward Layer 2 broadcasts, so each interface with its switch and VLAN is its own broadcast domain. Switches flood broadcasts inside the VLAN rather than stopping them, and a router can separate more than two networks.
