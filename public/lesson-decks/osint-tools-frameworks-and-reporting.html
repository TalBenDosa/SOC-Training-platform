Tools are collectors, not analysts: they return candidates, and every candidate still needs a source, a timestamp and a judgment.

This lesson covers the workflow around the lookups: tool categories, entity graphs, safe automation, structured sharing, analytic rigor and the final report.

Set the frame early. The tool prints a result; the analyst explains why it is true. Single-indicator lookups belong to the enrichment tools lesson, this one is the wider workflow.

HACKTHESOCLesson 125OSINT tools and reporting8 / 47

Questions to ask before you run anything

  • What exact question?Name the question you are answering before you pick a tool.
  • Least intrusive source?Which source can answer it with the smallest footprint?
  • Does it touch the target?Will this module reach the target's systems, or only third-party data?
  • What happens to the output?What will I do with it, and who needs it?

Pitfall: tool over-reliance. Treat output as a lead list and verify important items by a second independent source.

An analyst who only trusts what a tool printed cannot explain why a result is true. That is the habit to break.

An investigation drawn as nodes and relationships, and how much each relationship is worth.

HACKTHESOCLesson 125OSINT tools and reporting10 / 47

Entities, edges, transforms and pivots

The vocabulary is tool-independent: you can build the same graph in a notebook or on a whiteboard.

The nodes

Entity

Domains, IPs, emails, people or aliases, organizations, certificates, ASNs, social accounts, file hashes.

    The relationship

    Edge

    Resolves-to, registered-by, hosted-on, issued-to, follows, mentions. Record where it came from and when.

      A function

      Transform

      Takes one entity and returns related ones: a domain in, IPs, registrant, nameservers and certificates out.

        A decision

        Pivot

        The analyst follows one edge to a new entity and asks the next question.

          Maltego popularized this vocabulary. Note that a good graph stores provenance on every edge, not just the link.
          HACKTHESOCLesson 125OSINT tools and reporting11 / 47

          Read the structure first, not the pretty icons

          • Tight clustersNodes closely connected to each other suggest a shared operator or shared template.
          • Bridge nodeConnects two clusters: the most interesting object, it might prove one actor runs both.
          • Hub with hundreds of edgesUsually not a discovery: typically shared hosting, a CDN, or a registrar default.
          • Current or historical?A passive DNS record from three years ago may belong to the previous owner of the IP.
          • Planted on purpose?Ask whether an adversary could have created this link deliberately.
          Each bullet is a question to ask of any graph. The hub item is the one beginners misread most often.
          HACKTHESOCLesson 125OSINT tools and reporting12 / 47

          Not every edge is equally strong evidence

          Ask whether the shared attribute is unique to one operator or common to many.

          Shared attributeStrengthWhy
          Same non-privacy registrant emailStrongUnique to a person or team unless the data was forged
          Same analytics or tracking ID in page sourceStrongTied to one account, rarely shared
          Same unusual TLS certificate or keyStrongReuse is a deliberate or lazy operator choice
          Same favicon hash or page templateModerateKits are sold and copied
          Same nameserver pairModerate to weakCommon among customers of one provider
          Same IP on shared hostingWeakThousands of unrelated sites live there
          Same registrar or TLDVery weakMillions of customers
          Have students sort a few made-up shared attributes into the table before showing it. The lesson's rule: strength depends on how many unrelated parties could share the attribute.
          HACKTHESOCLesson 125OSINT tools and reporting13 / 47

          Twelve domains, one IP: a weak link, then a strong case

          A graph is a set of hypotheses to test, never a finding by itself.

          1. 1The graph looks like one operationTwelve lookalike domains appear to connect through a single IP.
          2. 2Check the IPIt is a budget host with thousands of tenants, so the link is weak.
          3. 3Pivot to stronger attributesThe registrant email of three domains and the analytics ID of five.
          4. 4The case rests on two strong edgesTwo independent, unique links replace the one weak, shared one.
          This is also the answer to quiz question 1. The right reaction to a weak edge is not deleting it but looking for stronger, independent ones.

          Section 3 of 8

          Automating responsibly: limits, keys, terms, noise

          Automation multiplies both speed and mistakes. Four disciplines keep it safe.

          HACKTHESOCLesson 125OSINT tools and reporting15 / 47

          Four disciplines for safe automation

          Rate limits

          Exceeding a request budget gets your key suspended and your IP blocked. Throttle, cache results, prefer bulk endpoints.

            API keys

            Keys are credentials. Use a key store, never scripts or tickets. One key per project, lowest privilege, rotate on exit.

              Terms of service

              Public describes visibility, not permission. Read the terms, use official APIs, document the legal basis.

                Passive vs active

                The most important distinction: does the target's own system see you?

                  A leaked OSINT key can be abused in your name. Work that violates terms can taint the evidence and expose the organization.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 125OSINT tools and reporting16 / 47

                  Passive keeps you invisible; active tells the target

                  Passive (target is not touched)Active (target's systems see you)
                  Search engines and cached or archived pagesVisiting the suspect site from your corporate network
                  Certificate transparency logsPort or service scanning of the target
                  Passive DNS and historical WHOIS or RDAPDNS brute-force against the target's nameservers
                  Third-party URL-scan snapshots, breach notification servicesProbing login or reset forms to test if an email exists
                  Public code repositories and paste sitesSending email to the target or opening tracking links
                  Rows are not matched pairs; read the left column as a list and the right column as a list. Ask students to sort a new action into one side or the other.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 125OSINT tools and reporting17 / 47
                  An adversary running a phishing site reads its access logs.

                  A burst from your address range, a scanner user agent, or a lookup from a security vendor's netblock tells them. They rotate, wipe the kit, or serve a clean decoy.

                  Your evidence disappears. Active touching of third-party systems may also be unlawful without authorization.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 125OSINT tools and reporting18 / 47

                  Working rule: passive first

                  1. 1Query passive sourcesStart with data the target cannot see you collect.
                  2. 2Snapshot before any direct visitUse a third-party snapshot or an isolated, non-attributable environment, and only if needed.
                  3. 3Choose each moduleDisable default-on active modules in SpiderFoot and theHarvester.
                  4. 4Log what ran, scan only your ownRecord which modules ran; scan only assets you own or are authorized to test.
                  This rule is the answer to quiz question 2. Passive first, direct contact only when the case needs it.

                  Managing and sharing: MISP, STIX, TAXII, IOCs

                  Findings in one analyst's notes die when the analyst leaves. Give them a durable home.

                  HACKTHESOCLesson 125OSINT tools and reporting20 / 47

                  MISP vocabulary: events, attributes, objects and flags

                  MISP is an open-source threat intelligence platform. Distribution settings control who may see an event.

                  TermWhat it is
                  EventAn incident, campaign or investigation
                  AttributeA typed value: domain, ip-dst, email-src, url, sha256
                  ObjectRelated attributes grouped, e.g. a file with its name, hash and size
                  Tags and galaxiesTags carry classification such as TLP; galaxies map to ATT&CK techniques or named actors
                  to_ids flagSays whether an attribute is suitable for automated detection
                  WarninglistFlags known-benign values (large cloud providers, public DNS) so they do not become IOCs
                  CorrelationNew values auto-match everything stored: today's find can light up last year's event
                  Warninglists are the guard against false-positive IOCs. Correlation is why storing findings centrally pays off.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 125OSINT tools and reporting21 / 47

                  STIX is the what, TAXII is the how

                  A JSON language

                  STIX 2.1: the what

                  Describes intelligence as objects joined by relationship objects.

                  • Indicator, threat-actor, campaign, infrastructure
                  • Malware, identity, observed-data, report
                  • Pattern: [domain-name:value = 'acme-payrol1-portal.example']
                  • An indicator also carries validity dates

                  The HTTPS transport

                  TAXII 2.1: the how

                  Servers expose collections; clients fetch or push STIX bundles.

                  • Same content, standard delivery
                  • Machine-readable sharing between partners
                  Keep it simple for beginners: STIX describes, TAXII delivers. The pattern shown is the lesson's own example indicator.

                  Section 5 of 8

                  Analytic rigor: hypotheses, confidence, bias

                  Tools produce data. Rigor turns data into a judgment you can defend.

                  HACKTHESOCLesson 125OSINT tools and reporting25 / 47

                  ACH: only the diagnostic evidence separates the hypotheses

                  List hypotheses, list evidence, mark each item consistent, inconsistent or neutral, then focus on diagnosticity.

                  EvidenceH1: Criminal credential-phishing crewH2: Mass commodity kit campaignH3: Authorized internal simulation
                  Domain registered 6 days agoConsistentConsistentConsistent
                  Page lists our company name and HR brandingConsistentInconsistentConsistent
                  Persona profile created 3 weeks agoConsistentNeutralInconsistent
                  No simulation ticket with the awareness teamConsistentConsistentInconsistent

                  Row one fits every hypothesis, so it proves nothing. Rows two to four separate H1 from H2 and H3. Try to disprove, not confirm.

                  ACH forces you to compare explanations instead of collecting support for your favorite. The hypothesis with the fewest inconsistencies survives. Tie this to quiz question 3.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 125OSINT tools and reporting26 / 47

                  Likelihood and confidence are different ideas

                  How probable

                  Likelihood

                  How probable the event is, stated with a standard word of estimative probability.

                    How solid

                    Confidence

                    How solid the evidence base behind the judgment is: low, moderate or high.

                      Never 'could' or 'might'

                      Readers hear anything from 5 to 95 percent in those words, so they cannot stand in for an estimative term.

                        Example: likely, with moderate confidence, because the evidence is consistent but comes from only two independent sources.

                        Full lesson example: 'We assess it is likely that the domain is part of a targeted credential-phishing effort, with moderate confidence, because the evidence is consistent but comes from only two independent sources.'
                        HACKTHESOCLesson 125OSINT tools and reporting27 / 47

                        Words of estimative probability

                        TermApproximate probability
                        Almost no chance1 to 5 percent
                        Very unlikely5 to 20 percent
                        Unlikely20 to 45 percent
                        Roughly even chance45 to 55 percent
                        Likely55 to 80 percent
                        Very likely80 to 95 percent
                        Almost certain95 to 99 percent
                        The highlighted row is the term used in the worked example's bottom line. Standard words keep every reader aligned.
                        HACKTHESOCLesson 125OSINT tools and reporting28 / 47

                        Rate sources separately from claims, and name your biases

                        • Source reliabilityRated A (completely reliable) to F (cannot be judged).
                        • Information credibilityRated 1 (confirmed) to 6 (cannot be judged), separately from the source.
                        • Circular reporting counts onceA vendor (A) can pass on an unverified claim (4). Five blogs repeating one tweet are one source.
                        • Biases to nameConfirmation, anchoring, availability, mirror imaging, and attribution bias (jumping to a famous actor).
                        • CountermeasuresACH, a second analyst's review, writing disconfirming evidence into the report, no actor name without strong links.
                        An anonymous forum poster (F) can post something you independently confirm (2). Reliability and credibility are separate scores.

                        The OSINT report: structure and audience

                        If the reader cannot act on it, or cannot tell known from guessed, the investigation had no value.

                        HACKTHESOCLesson 125OSINT tools and reporting30 / 47

                        A durable report structure and how each part fails

                        SectionContentsCommon failure
                        Bottom line (BLUF)One to three sentences: assessment, confidence, decision neededBuried at the end after pages of method
                        Objective and scopeQuestion asked, requester, time window, what was out of scopeScope creep into unrelated people or systems
                        Methodology and sourcesTools and versions, passive or active, collection dates, source ratings'Open sources were used' with nothing specific
                        Findings with evidenceNumbered findings, each with source, timestamp, evidence referenceInference written as fact
                        Assessment and confidenceHypotheses considered, estimative term, confidence, key assumptionsMissing confidence, or 'might be' language
                        LimitationsWhat could not be seen, stale data, single-source items, gapsOmitted, which hides risk
                        Recommendations, appendicesPrioritized actions with owners; IOC table, graph export, evidence log, TLPVague advice like 'be vigilant'; IOCs without expiry
                        The lesson lists recommendations and appendices as two rows; they are merged here to fit. BLUF is highlighted because quiz question 4 tests it.
                        HACKTHESOCLesson 125OSINT tools and reporting31 / 47

                        Write for the requester

                        The same facts change shape depending on the reader.

                        SOC manager or responder

                        The BLUF, the IOCs and the immediate actions.

                          Executive

                          Business impact, a decision, and a one-line confidence statement.

                            Legal or privacy team

                            Data-handling detail and any personal data involved.

                              Takedown or abuse desk

                              Exact URLs, timestamps and evidence that satisfy the registrar's requirements.

                                An external sharing partner wants a STIX bundle and a TLP marking. Write for the primary reader; put detail in appendices.

                                Ask the class who the primary reader is before they write a single line. Detail is not removed, it is moved to appendices.
                                HACKTHESOCLesson 125OSINT tools and reporting32 / 47

                                Separate fact from judgment with plain labels

                                • ObservedWhat you saw.
                                • ReportedWhat a source claimed.
                                • AssessedYour inference, carrying an estimative term and a confidence level.
                                • Unsourced claimsAny sentence the reader might challenge needs a reference. If you cannot cite it, downgrade it to a lead or leave it out.
                                The common failure is inference written as fact. The three labels make the difference visible at a glance.
                                HACKTHESOCLesson 125OSINT tools and reporting33 / 47

                                Defensible documentation: anyone can reproduce your work

                                A colleague, auditor or court should be able to repeat what you did.

                                Record for every item

                                Keep a running log as you work, instead of reconstructing it afterward.

                                • The URL or query
                                • Access time in UTC
                                • Tool name and version
                                • Module or settings used

                                Preserve the evidence

                                Capture originals and keep them read-only.

                                • Screenshot plus archived page
                                • A hash of the saved file
                                • The analyst who collected it
                                • Work from copies

                                Make it safe to share

                                Add a distribution marking such as TLP. Defang indicators in text (hxxp, [.]) so the report cannot be clicked into a live compromise.

                                  Defanging protects the reader: a report should never be one click from a live compromise.

                                  Legal, ethical, and OPSEC recap

                                  Every stage, from the first search to the final report, carries obligations.

                                  HACKTHESOCLesson 125OSINT tools and reporting35 / 47

                                  Seven principles that apply to the whole workflow

                                  PrincipleIn practiceFailure example
                                  Public-onlyOnly what is accessible without bypassing controls, fake credentials or deceptionLogging in to a private forum with a borrowed account
                                  AuthorizationTouch only systems you own or are authorized to test; stay passive to third partiesPort scanning an adversary's server
                                  MinimizationCollect only what the objective requires; apply a retention periodSaving a whole family's social media for a domain case
                                  Non-attributable researchDedicated browser profile or VM, separate egress, no personal or corporate accountsOpening the phishing page from your laptop on the corporate network
                                  Evidence integrityCapture originals, hash them, keep an immutable copy, log every handling stepEditing a screenshot or losing the original file
                                  Safe handlingTreat downloads as hostile; defang indicators; analyze in isolationClicking a live link from a report
                                  ProportionalityMatch the intrusiveness of the method to the severity of the questionA deep personal profile built for a low-risk spam message
                                  Use the failure column as a quiz: cover the first column and ask which principle each failure breaks. Non-attributable research also means no contact with the adversary.
                                  HACKTHESOCLesson 125OSINT tools and reporting36 / 47

                                  Naming a person is not the same as naming a domain

                                  Personas and attribution

                                  Names can be stolen, photos reused, profiles forged to frame someone. Do not circulate an identity as the offender without strong, independent evidence. Pass it to counsel or law enforcement.

                                    Sock-puppet policy

                                    Interacting with a threat actor through a created persona is an operation, not research. It needs written approval, a defined purpose and legal review. Outside routine OSINT.

                                      Data protection

                                      Personal data in leaked sets stays personal data. Apply privacy law (GDPR where it applies), restrict access, record why, delete at case close. Notify owners through normal process.

                                        Doxxing an innocent person causes real harm and legal exposure. Do not redistribute leaked personal data; report to the affected account owners through the organization's normal process.
                                        HACKTHESOCLesson 125OSINT tools and reporting37 / 47

                                        Four questions before every action

                                        • Am I authorized?For this specific action, not just the investigation in general.
                                        • Is the method proportionate?Does the intrusiveness match the severity of the question?
                                        • Could this harm a bystander?Could the collection identify or harm someone who is not the target?
                                        • Can I prove how I got it?Can I show how the evidence was obtained and that it was not altered?
                                        These four questions are the practical test that turns the principles table into a habit.

                                            Key takeaways

                                            1. 01Choose the tool category from the question: link analysis, automated recon, scripted recon, harvester or directory.
                                            2. 02A graph is a set of hypotheses: weigh each edge by uniqueness and age, and pivot to strong, independent links.
                                            3. 03Stay passive first: public means visible, not permitted. Guard API keys, and respect rate limits and terms.
                                            4. 04Turn findings into IOCs with source, confidence, TLP and expiry; STIX is the what, TAXII is the how.
                                            5. 05Use ACH and estimative terms; separate likelihood from confidence, and count circular reporting once.
                                            6. 06Open with the BLUF, label Observed, Reported and Assessed, state limitations, and keep evidence defensible.
