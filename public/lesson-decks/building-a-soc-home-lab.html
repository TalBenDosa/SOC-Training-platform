Why build a lab, and the isolation principle

A place where you control both sides, and where nothing can escape.

HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab6 / 52

A lab lets you do what production never will

That feedback loop shows which fields matter, which events are noisy, and which blind spots exist.

  • Practice detectionsWrite a query, trigger the behavior, confirm it fires, then tune it.
  • Practice investigationsReplay an incident and pivot from the alert to the host, the process tree and the user.
  • Break things on purposeDisable a log source and learn what you lose. Restore a snapshot and try again.
  • Learn the plumbingHow audit policy, agents, forwarding and parsing connect, which dashboards rarely show.
None of these is possible in a production environment or a job interview. The lab is the only place you can fail cheaply.

Isolation is four layers applied together

Network

VMs sit on a host-only or internal virtual network with no route to your LAN or the internet.

    Host

    Disable shared folders, clipboard and drag-and-drop so nothing hops from a lab VM to your real machine.

      Data

      Fake users, fake data, throwaway passwords. Never reuse a real password or copy in real company data.

        State

        Snapshots make any mistake reversible in minutes.

          Nothing in the lab may reach, or be reached from, networks you do not own and control.

          Why it matters: the lab DC has weak passwords, an unpatched evaluation OS and attack tools. On your home network it is a vulnerable server you built for free. Attack traffic aimed outside the lab can also be illegal.
          HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab8 / 52
          Before every session ask: what can this VM reach, and what can reach it?

          Then a third: what happens if it is compromised? Unless the first two answers are only the other lab VMs, stop and fix the network.

          Authorization here is simple: you own the lab and every machine in it, and everything you run stays inside. It is the same scoping logic professional testing guidance such as NIST SP 800-115 demands.

          Virtualization basics: hypervisors, VMs and snapshots

          The software that runs your lab machines, and the feature that keeps them trustworthy.

          HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab10 / 52

          Three hypervisors cover almost every home lab

          A VM is a complete computer simulated in software. The hypervisor is the program that creates and runs it.

          HypervisorCost for learnersNotes
          VirtualBoxFree, open sourceEasy network modes (NAT, NAT Network, host-only, internal). Great default choice.
          VMware Workstation / FusionFree for personal usePolished performance and snapshot tree. Flexible network editor.
          Hyper-VIncluded in Windows Pro, Enterprise, EducationNative and fast, but not on Windows Home; its virtual switches work differently.

          Pick one and stay with it. Mixing hypervisors multiplies networking confusion without teaching more security.

          Analogy: the hypervisor is an apartment building manager. One physical building (your PC), separate apartments (VMs), each with its own locked door, sharing the plumbing of CPU, RAM and disk.
          HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab11 / 52

          Check the foundations before you install anything

          • Hardware virtualizationEnable VT-x, AMD-V or SVM in firmware. If VMs will not start or crawl, check this first.
          • One hypervisor at a timeRunning two at once can conflict on Windows, because Hyper-V features can take over the virtualization hardware.
          • Templates and linked clonesBuild one patched Windows client, snapshot it, then make linked clones for each client you need.
          • Reset identity on full clonesRun Sysprep or equivalent on full clones that join a domain; duplicate IDs cause trust errors.
          Templates and clones save hours. The duplicate machine identifier problem is a classic cause of confusing domain trust errors.
          HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab12 / 52

          Snapshot discipline keeps a lab you can trust

          1. 1Right after installTake clean-install once the OS is installed and patched, before anything else.
          2. 2After each milestoneSnapshot domain-built, telemetry-ready and siem-connected as you reach them.
          3. 3Before every attack simulationTake one immediately before, and roll back after.
          4. 4Name by what is trueUse a descriptive name and a date, never snapshot1.
          Snapshot discipline is what separates a lab you can trust from one that decays. Descriptive names mean you can pick the right restore point at 11pm.
          HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab13 / 52

          Snapshots are not backups: treat the lab as cattle

          Snapshots are not backups

          They depend on the base disk, grow over time and slow the VM if chained too deep.

          • Keep a handful, delete stale ones
          • Export a full appliance for a real backup

          The lab as a pet

          Months of undocumented changes, no snapshots, and one forgotten update that breaks everything.

            The lab as cattle

            Rebuild any VM from a known snapshot in minutes.

            • Keep a text file: IP plan, hostnames, lab passwords
            • Never reuse those passwords anywhere real
            Keep the notes file only inside the lab or in your own notes. The aim is that no single VM is precious.

            Virtual networks and sizing your lab

            The network mode decides whether your lab is safe; the budget decides whether it runs.

            HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab15 / 52

            Five network modes, one clear pitfall

            Names vary slightly across hypervisors, but the behaviors are the same ideas.

            ModeVM to VMVM to internetUse in a SOC lab
            NATNo (isolated per VM)Yes, through hostTemporary, for patching and downloads
            NAT NetworkYesYesShared updates when lab VMs need to talk and download
            Host-onlyYesNoMain lab network; reach the SIEM from your browser
            InternalYesNoStrictest lab network, VMs only
            BridgedYesYesAvoid for any vulnerable or attack-tooling VM

            Bridged makes the VM a real device on your LAN: a DC with weak passwords becomes a peer of your phone and TV.

            Bridged feels convenient because everything just works, which is exactly the trap. Attack simulation traffic leaves the lab. Treat it as forbidden for anything running the lab domain.
            HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab16 / 52

            Two adapters per VM: one for the lab, one only when needed

            Always on

            Adapter 1: the lab network

            Host-only or internal. Carries all lab traffic: domain logons, DNS and log shipping.

              Only when needed

              Adapter 2: NAT

              Switch it on for updates or tools, then disconnect it again.

                Preferred

                Better still: no internet

                Download installers on your host and move them in with a temporary read-only ISO, so lab VMs never need internet.

                  This is the recommended pattern for a beginner. The second adapter is a deliberate, temporary exception, not a permanent setting.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab17 / 52

                  A private addressing plan with static IPs

                  • Domain controllers must have static IPs
                  • Clients must use the DC as their DNS server
                  • Most domain-join failures are really DNS failures
                  A client using your router or a public resolver cannot find the domain. Check DNS before anything else when a join fails.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab18 / 52

                  Size the lab before you build it

                  VMvCPURAMDisk (thin)
                  Domain controller (Windows Server)24 GB50 GB
                  Windows 10 or 11 client24 GB60 GB
                  Optional Linux box12 GB25 GB
                  SIEM or log collector2 to 46 to 8 GB80 GB or more

                  Around 16 to 18 GB of RAM in total: a 32 GB host is comfortable; 16 GB works with the DC, one client and a light collector.

                  Under-sizing is the other common failure. A DC starved of RAM promotes slowly and times out on logons, producing errors that look like attacks. Use an SSD, thin-provision disks, and reserve a few cores and several GB for the host.

                  Section 4 of 9

                  The core lab topology and what each VM is for

                  The minimum set of roles whose logs a real analyst reads every day.

                  HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab20 / 52

                  Five VMs: identity, endpoints and a place where logs land

                  VMRoleKey logs it produces
                  DC01Domain controller (AD DS, DNS): central authenticationSecurity events: logons, Kerberos, account and group changes
                  WS01Windows client, domain joined: the typical victim endpointProcess creation, PowerShell, Sysmon activity, local logons
                  WS02 (optional)Second Windows client: lateral movement practiceNetwork logons, remote service creation, share access
                  LIN01 (optional)Linux server: a different log styleSSH logons, sudo usage, syslog
                  SIEM01Log collector and search (free stack)Receives everything and stores it

                  All five share the same isolated network. Start with a DC, one client and a collector, then add the rest.

                  The DC is the most valuable log source. The Linux box exists for exposure to a second log format, not Linux administration: a failed login looks very different there than on Windows.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab21 / 52

                  The shape mirrors what attackers do

                  With these roles you can follow a whole story from the workstation to the DC in the logs.

                  1. 1Land on an endpointA typical intrusion starts on WS01.
                  2. 2Steal or guess credentialsThe attacker obtains a way to authenticate.
                  3. 3Authenticate somewhere elseOn WS02 or a server.
                  4. 4Reach the domain controllerDC01 is the eventual goal.
                  Each VM teaches something different about telemetry: the DC shows identity events, the client shows behavior, the collector shows the pipeline.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab22 / 52

                  Four planning questions before you add a VM

                  • Which practice?Which detection or investigation do I want to practice? Build only the VMs that produce its evidence.
                  • Which log to force?Is there a source I want to be forced to configure, such as the DC audit policy? Include that VM.
                  • Does it fit in RAM?Can it all run at once? If not, run in tiers: DC and collector always on, clients on demand.
                  • Who needs internet?Ideally none, except briefly for updates.

                  Name hosts like a real company (DC01, WS01, SIEM01) with a domain such as lab.local.

                  Realistic structure makes your queries transferable to real environments, while the names themselves stay boring and ordinary, the way production names are.

                  Section 5 of 9

                  Standing up Active Directory (conceptual steps)

                  Learn the order and the reason for each step; follow vendor documentation for the clicks.

                  HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab24 / 52

                  Six steps from evaluation image to a joined client

                  1. 1Get an evaluation imageOfficial Microsoft Evaluation Center only (Server trial commonly 180 days). Verify the hash.
                  2. 2Install and make it reachableDesktop experience, hostname DC01, static IP, own address as preferred DNS.
                  3. 3Add the AD DS role, then promotePromotion is separate: new forest and domain (lab.local), DSRM password, DNS.
                  4. 4Create structureOUs, fake users, security groups, and one account used only for administration.
                  5. 5Join a clientPoint DNS at the DC, confirm lab.local resolves, join, reboot, log on as a domain user.
                  6. 6Snapshot the resultName it domain-built with the date, on every VM.

                  Snapshot clean-install after step 2 and dc-promoted after step 3.

                  The trial can be extended a limited number of times with a rearm command, or you rebuild from your snapshot. The exact screens change between Windows Server versions; the order does not.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab25 / 52

                  Step 4: OUs that look like a small company

                  • Organizational unitsWorkstations, Servers, Users and a Service Accounts OU.
                  • Fake usersA handful of ordinary users, such as a finance user and an IT helpdesk user.
                  • Security groupsHelpdesk and Finance, plus one account used only for administration.
                  • Why OUs matterGroup policy attaches to OUs, and you will use it to push audit settings.
                  Use realistic but fake passwords that meet default complexity, and keep them out of anything you share publicly. Later you can add a user to a privileged group on purpose and see the events it creates.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab26 / 52

                  If the domain join fails, check in this order

                  1. 1Network reachabilityCan the client reach the DC at all?
                  2. 2DNS points at the DCThe client must resolve lab.local.
                  3. 3Time differenceKerberos tolerates only small clock drift.
                  4. 4CredentialsOnly then suspect the account or password.
                  Most mysterious join failures are DNS failures. Working in this order saves hours of chasing the wrong thing.
                  HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab27 / 52

                  Four pitfalls to know before you build

                  Cloning without reset

                  Cloning a DC or client without resetting machine identity gives duplicate SID or trust problems.

                    Mixed DNS on the DC

                    DNS pointing at itself plus a public resolver causes slow, intermittent resolution.

                      Time drift

                      Suspended VMs drift and break Kerberos. Sync time sources after resume.

                        Role without promotion

                        Installing the role but forgetting to promote means no domain exists yet.

                          Each of these produces errors that look mysterious. Knowing them in advance turns a lost evening into a two-minute fix.

                          Generating telemetry: auditing, Sysmon and forwarding

                          A fresh domain logs far less than you expect. Turn the useful events on in three layers.

                          HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab29 / 52

                          Three layers turn a quiet domain into useful telemetry

                          Group Policy

                          1. Windows auditing

                          Advanced Audit Policy categories, set centrally with Group Policy on the right OUs.

                            Rich detail

                            2. Sysmon

                            A free Sysinternals service and driver that logs what built-in logs lack. Its power is its configuration file.

                              Get logs off the VM

                              3. Forwarding

                              An agent or Windows Event Forwarding sends the Security, Sysmon and PowerShell channels to the collector.

                                Many valuable events are simply off by default, and a lab where you never turn them on teaches you nothing about detection. Logs sitting on each VM are useless to a SOC.
                                HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab30 / 52

                                Start with these five audit areas

                                Audit areaExample eventsWhy it helps
                                Logon and Logoff4624 success, 4625 failureBrute force, odd logon times, lateral movement
                                Account Management4720 user created, 4728 or 4732 group changePersistence and privilege changes
                                Process Creation4688Records every new process
                                Command line in process creationAdds the full command line to 4688Turns a bare process name into a story
                                PowerShell loggingScript block logging 4104Reveals what scripts actually did

                                The command line is a separate setting. Also raise the Security log maximum size, or it wraps within hours.

                                Two details trip people up: Process Creation records the process but not the command line, and a small default Security log overwrites evidence within hours in a busy lab.
                                HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab31 / 52

                                Sysmon is only as good as its configuration

                                • No config, little valueRunning Sysmon without a config logs little and misses the point.
                                • SwiftOnSecurityA well-maintained baseline: a readable, noise-reduced set.
                                • olafhartong sysmon-modularModular configs mapped to MITRE ATT&CK; merge only the rules you want.
                                • Install and updateInstall with the accept-EULA and install switches plus the config; update with the configuration switch.
                                • Where events landMicrosoft-Windows-Sysmon/Operational. ID 1 process creation, 3 network connection, 11 file creation, 13 registry value set.
                                Install Sysmon on every Windows VM. A config that logs everything produces noise that hides the signal, so start curated.
                                HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab32 / 52

                                Change one thing, then verify

                                1. 1Apply one changeEnable one policy or config, then force a policy update on the client.
                                2. 2Generate a harmless test eventCreate something small on the client that you can look for.
                                3. 3Check Event Viewer firstConfirm it appears locally before blaming the pipeline.
                                4. 4Confirm on the collectorTake the telemetry-ready snapshot only when the event shows up there too.

                                More is not better: start with a curated config and add rules when you have a reason.

                                Checking locally first splits the problem in half. If it is in Event Viewer but not on the collector, the fault is the pipeline.

                                Section 7 of 9

                                The monitoring side: a free SIEM or log collector

                                Where a pile of VMs turns into a mini SOC. No expensive product needed.

                                HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab34 / 52

                                Two free routes to a collector

                                Built in

                                A: Windows Event Forwarding

                                A server runs the Windows Event Collector service and other machines push events using subscriptions.

                                • No third-party software
                                • Teaches how Windows log transport works
                                • Searching is basic: Event Viewer or exports

                                Search and visualize

                                B: Open stack, e.g. Elastic

                                Elasticsearch stores logs and Kibana searches them. Elastic Agent or Winlogbeat ship the channels.

                                • Integrations parse logs into named fields
                                • Alternatives: Splunk free tiers and Wazuh
                                • Same shape: agent, transport, parse, index, search
                                Either route is fine for learning. Route B gives far better investigation practice because you can query by field and time instead of reading events one by one.
                                HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab35 / 52

                                The pipeline you will debug in real life

                                1. 1SourceThe Windows channel on a lab VM.
                                2. 2CollectionAn agent or subscription reads new events.
                                3. 3TransportSent across the lab network, ideally encrypted even here.
                                4. 4ParsingRaw XML or text split into fields: user, host, process, command line, source address.
                                5. 5Storage and searchIndexed so you can query by field and time.
                                Encryption in the lab builds good habits. When a log is missing in production, you walk this same chain stage by stage.
                                HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab36 / 52

                                Keep the collector small, short-lived and locked down

                                • Hungriest VMGive the search engine enough memory and expect a slow first start.
                                • Short retentionDays to a couple of weeks keeps disk under control with verbose Sysmon data.
                                • Strong lab passwordDo not leave the web interface on defaults.
                                • Host-only exposureReachable by you, not by anything else. Never publish its port outside the host.
                                The collector holds copies of everything in the lab, so it deserves the same isolation as the rest.
                                HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab37 / 52
                                Before any exercise, search the last five minutes of events from each VM.

                                A silent host is a pipeline problem, not a detection problem. Check in order: audit policy applied, agent running, clock drift, Sysmon config dropping it.

                                Answering these in order is exactly how production log-source health checks work.

                                Safe attack simulation for learning

                                A detection you have never triggered is a guess.

                                HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab39 / 52
                                Use benign, publicly documented test tools, only inside the isolated lab.

                                Atomic Red Team from Red Canary: small tests, each mapped to a MITRE ATT&CK technique, with a description and a cleanup step.

                                Examples of atomic tests: creating a scheduled task, a harmless listing of running processes, an encoded PowerShell command. Caldera chains more adversary-like behavior, but start with atomics because each is small enough to reason about.
                                HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab40 / 52

                                Six rules for safe simulation

                                • Isolated lab onlyConfirm the network mode first. If a VM is on bridged networking, fix that.
                                • Snapshot first, roll back afterThis also keeps your telemetry baselines clean.
                                • Read the test firstKnow what it does, what artifacts it creates and its cleanup command.
                                • No real malwareA home lab is not hardened for live malware analysis. Stay with benign simulators.
                                • Security tools will reactWindows Defender may flag or block some atomics. Decide deliberately.
                                • Write down what you ran and whenTimestamps are your ground truth; know the exact minute of each action.
                                Run the cleanup, then still consider reverting. In a real SOC you correlate by time, so in the lab you should always know the minute of each action.
                                HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab41 / 52

                                When Defender reacts to a test, decide deliberately

                                Exclude the test folder

                                Inside the lab VM only, so the test can run.

                                  Keep protection on

                                  Study the alert. That is itself good SOC practice.

                                    Never weaken protections

                                    On a machine that is also reachable from outside the lab.

                                      Both of the first two are legitimate choices. The third is the one that breaks the isolation principle.
                                      HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab42 / 52

                                      Expect, look for, conclude: a framework for every test

                                      QuestionExample: scheduled-task persistence test
                                      What technique is this?Scheduled Task creation for persistence
                                      What logs should it create?Sysmon process creation for the task tool, Security event for task creation if auditing is on
                                      Which fields identify it?Parent process, command line, user, host
                                      Did my query catch it?Yes or no, then tune
                                      What is a benign lookalike?An admin or installer legitimately creating tasks

                                      If a test produces no logs at all, you found a visibility gap: a success of the lab, not a failure of the test.

                                      The last row is the heart of detection engineering. Every lab behavior has a legitimate cousin in production; tell them apart by who, from where, at what time and with what parent.

                                      Your first exercise, and faster ways to build the lab

                                      Two harmless events, found in the collector, then a written finding.

                                      HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab44 / 52

                                      The first exercise from snapshot to revert

                                      1. 1Snapshot pre-exercise-1Needs the DC, a joined client with Sysmon and auditing, and the collector receiving logs.
                                      2. 2Part 1: a failed logonEnter a wrong password for a lab user 3 or 4 times on WS01. Note the time and account.
                                      3. 3Part 2: a suspicious processOpen cmd, start PowerShell, run a harmless command or a Base64-encoded one. Note the time.
                                      4. 4Part 3: close the loopWrite a one paragraph finding, save the search as a query, repeat the activity to confirm.
                                      5. 5Revert to the snapshotReturn the lab to a clean state.
                                      The finding should say what happened, give evidence with timestamps and event IDs, and say what you would check next. An Atomic Red Team command line obfuscation test produces the encoded command safely.
                                      HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab45 / 52

                                      Part 1: read the failed logon like an investigator

                                      Search the Security channel for Event ID 4625 in the minutes after your attempts.

                                      • TargetWhich account was targeted, and on which machine did the failure occur?
                                      • Logon typeInteractive at the console differs from a network logon over a share.
                                      • SourceWhat source address or workstation is recorded?
                                      • Status and sub statusDo they say bad password or unknown user?
                                      • Count and spacingA burst against one account is the shape of password guessing.
                                      Have students compare their written-down time and account name with what the log shows. Every question here is answered by a field.
                                      HACKTHESOCLesson 114Building a SOC home lab46 / 52

                                      Part 2: what to examine in the process event

                                      Search Sysmon Event ID 1, and Security 4688 if command line auditing is on.

                                      Look atAsk yourself
                                      Parent processIs this parent normal for this child?
                                      Image path, user and hashesWould a regular user run this?
                                      Full command lineDoes it make sense, or is it encoded or unusually long?
                                      What happened nextA network connection (Sysmon 3) or a file write (Sysmon 11) right after?
                                      These are the investigator questions from the SOC loop applied to one process. Encoded or unusually long command lines are the cue to look closer.

                                            Key takeaways

                                            1. 01Isolation first: no lab VM may reach or be reached from networks you do not own; use host-only or internal, never bridged.
                                            2. 02Snapshot at every milestone and before every attack simulation. Snapshots are not backups.
                                            3. 03Core layout: DC01, WS01, optional WS02 and LIN01, and SIEM01 on a private subnet with static IPs and the DC as DNS.
                                            4. 04Build the domain in order: image, install, promote, OUs and users, join a client. Failed joins are usually DNS.
                                            5. 05Telemetry has three layers: audit policy, a configured Sysmon, and forwarding. Verify each change reaches the collector.
                                            6. 06Run benign Atomic Red Team tests only in the isolated lab, read them first, and record the exact time of every action.
