What enrichment is, and why a bare indicator says nothing

Ask an indicator who owns it, what it has done, and who has flagged it.

HACKTHESOCLesson 56Enrichment tools6 / 64

An indicator is just a string until you ask about it

An IOC (Indicator of Compromise) is any evidence tied to possibly malicious activity.

  • File hashA short fingerprint that uniquely identifies one specific file.
  • URLA full web address, path included, such as a link in a phishing email.
  • Domain nameThe registered name, such as secure-login-update.com.
  • IP addressA network address such as 203.0.113.45: by itself it says nothing.
The license plate analogy: the string is meaningless until a lookup says who owns it, what it has done, and whether anyone flagged it.
HACKTHESOCLesson 56Enrichment tools7 / 64

Four tools, four questions

ToolThe question it answers
VirusTotal (VT)Has anyone seen this file, URL or domain, and did security tools flag it?
WHOISWho registered this domain, and how old is it?
Passive DNS (pDNS)What IPs has this domain used, and what else shares its infrastructure?
Reputation feedsIs this indicator already on a known-bad list from others' observations?
An IOC, Indicator of Compromise, is a hash, URL, domain or IP. 203.0.113.45 tells you nothing until you ask questions about it.
HACKTHESOCLesson 56Enrichment tools8 / 64
No single tool gives a certain verdict. Each is one piece of the picture.

Like a detective gathering statements, fingerprints and footage, run all four, read each critically, then combine them into a confident call.

Enrichment sits at the start of almost every investigation: the alert alone does not carry the context you need.

Reading VirusTotal without over-trusting it

Usually the first stop: has anyone seen this, and did anything flag it?

Read the detection ratio carefully

Low count

Not a clean bill of health. Fresh and targeted malware often starts at zero. 0/71 means unknown, not safe.

High count

Not automatic doom. Engines copy each other; a few hits on a common tool can be false positives.

Labels

Trojan.GenericKD is vague. A specific family like Emotet or Cobalt Strike is actionable.

Beginners treat the ratio like a score. Experienced analysts read it.

WHOIS and domain age as a signal

The creation date is the field that matters most.

HACKTHESOCLesson 56Enrichment tools18 / 64

WHOIS: a domain's public registration record

  • Registered and updatedWhen the domain was created and last changed.
  • ExpiresWhen the registration runs out.
  • RegistrarThe company the domain was bought through.
  • RegistrantContact details, frequently hidden today behind privacy protection services.
The worked case: a user reports an email whose link points to secure-login-update[.]com. The next slide is the WHOIS answer.

WHOIS on a phishing link's domain

  • Privacy protection is common for legitimate registrants too: not damning alone
Extreme freshness is strong supporting evidence the domain exists for a short-lived campaign.

HACKTHESOCLesson 56Enrichment tools21 / 64

Domain age in daily work

  • Newly Registered DomainAn NRD, registered yesterday or days ago, is a meaningful risk signal in a phishing link or outbound connection.
  • Registrar and registrantA lax-abuse registrar adds suspicion; a registrant matching known-bad domains is a pivot.
  • Years-old business domainMore likely a false positive than a fresh campaign.
  • Automate itMany SIEM and TI platforms flag NRDs, commonly younger than 30 days.
Corroborating evidence, not a sole verdict: old domains can be compromised or bought. Record creation date and registrar in the ticket.
HACKTHESOCLesson 56Enrichment tools22 / 64

Domain age is a strong signal, not a verdict

Why fresh is suspicious

Attackers register throwaway domains for phishing and command-and-control, use them briefly, then abandon them. Real businesses usually have domains years old.

Why age can mislead

Old domains can be compromised or bought, and some legitimate services spin up new domains.

Fresh registration + poor reputation + suspicious content turns a maybe into a confident escalation.

Use WHOIS as corroborating evidence. Always write the creation date and registrar into the ticket so the next analyst sees why it looked suspicious.

Section 4 of 10

Passive DNS and pivoting infrastructure

Not what it resolves to now, but what it has resolved to over time.

HACKTHESOCLesson 56Enrichment tools24 / 64

How passive DNS builds its history

  1. 1Sensors watch DNSSensors around the internet observe DNS resolutions as they happen.
  2. 2Mappings are storedEach domain to IP answer is saved with the dates it was seen.
  3. 3You query historyNot 'what does it resolve to now' but 'what has it resolved to over months'.
  4. 4You pivotAsk what else has lived on that IP, then follow the leads.
Pivoting means expanding from one known indicator to the attacker's wider infrastructure. It only works because the history is stored.
HACKTHESOCLesson 56Enrichment tools25 / 64

Three ways to read a pivot

Domain to IP

Earlier IPs the malicious domain used: new leads to investigate and block.

IP to domains

Dozens of domains with the same naming pattern: possibly a whole phishing kit.

Shared hosting caution

Thousands of unrelated domains means shared or CDN hosting: weak evidence. A small dedicated server is strong.

Pivoting means expanding from one known indicator to the attacker's wider infrastructure.

One email becomes four domains and one IP

  • Same 'verify / update / reset your account' pattern, registered within days
  • Almost certainly one attacker's kit reused across campaigns
Then search your own logs for all of them to find every other host that contacted this infrastructure.

Section 5 of 10

Reputation feeds and cross-referencing

What the community has already observed about behaviour over time.

HACKTHESOCLesson 56Enrichment tools29 / 64

Reputation feeds: what the community already saw

Curated lists scoring IPs, domains and URLs from others' observed activity: spam, malware hosting, botnet C2.

  • AbuseIPDBCrowd-sourced reports of abusive IPs (spam, brute-forcing, scanning), giving an abuse confidence score.
  • URLhausA feed that tracks URLs actively distributing malware.
  • Vendor feedsCommercial platforms, often built into SIEMs, that merge many sources into one score.
Where VT says what engines think of a specific file or URL, feeds say how an IP or domain has behaved over time.
HACKTHESOCLesson 56Enrichment tools30 / 64

A reputation hit on the same IP

  • Sources: AbuseIPDB (crowd-sourced), URLhaus (malware URLs), vendor feeds
  • Fresh WHOIS + shared infrastructure + this hit: three independent sources agree
Where VT judges a specific file or URL, reputation feeds describe an IP's or domain's observed behaviour.

No hit means not yet reported: unknown, not safe, exactly like 0/71.

Feeds disagree, go stale, and can taint shared IPs. Check two or three independent feeds and look for agreement.

Cross-referencing is what separates a confident verdict from a guess.
HACKTHESOCLesson 56Enrichment tools33 / 64

Why one feed is never enough

Feeds disagree

Different sources see different traffic and score the same IP differently.

Feeds go stale

An old listing may describe a previous owner of the address.

Shared IPs get tainted

One misbehaving tenant on a shared host can taint the whole address.

So cross-reference

Check two or three independent feeds and look for agreement.

A hit adds real weight. Absence of a hit means not yet reported: unknown, never safe.

Putting it together: workflow and OPSEC

A repeatable loop, run quietly.

HACKTHESOCLesson 56Enrichment tools35 / 64

The enrichment loop

  1. 1Hash and search VTRead the labels and the Relations tab for contacted domains and IPs.
  2. 2WHOIS the domainNewly registered? Note the registrar.
  3. 3Passive DNSHistorical mappings and co-located domains.
  4. 4Reputation feedsFor every indicator, including everything pivots surfaced.
  5. 5Search your own logsFind any other affected hosts.
  6. 6DocumentEach indicator, each tool's answer, your verdict.
The trigger in the lesson's example: an EDR alert on a workstation that connected to an unfamiliar domain and downloaded a file.

OPSEC: do not tip off the attacker

  • Passive firstQuery stored data (hash search, pDNS, feeds) before touching attacker infrastructure.
  • Beware unique URLsLinks like .../track?id=USER123 carry a victim token; submitting or clicking it tells the attacker.
  • Sandboxes, deliberatelyIsolated, anonymized egress, never your corporate IP, identifiers stripped first.
  • DefangWrite hxxp://evil[.]com in tickets and chat so nobody clicks by accident.
A tipped-off attacker may burn the infrastructure, rotate domains, or accelerate the attack.
HACKTHESOCLesson 56Enrichment tools39 / 64

A victim-specific link: what is safe to do

The reported link carries a token unique to the targeted user.

ActionSafe?Why
Search the bare domain on VT, WHOIS, pDNSYesQueries stored data; never touches the attacker
Paste the full tokenized URL into VirusTotalNoThe page gets fetched; the attacker sees the token used
Click the link from a corporate machineNoReveals your network and confirms detection

Shodan: enriching infrastructure, not files

A search engine for devices: open ports, banners, exposed services.

HACKTHESOCLesson 56Enrichment tools41 / 64

Shodan indexes devices, not web pages

  • What it doesContinuously scans the whole public internet and indexes what answers on open ports.
  • What it recordsOpen ports, service banners, software versions, exposed devices.
  • Versus GoogleGoogle indexes web pages; Shodan indexes servers, routers, webcams, industrial controls.
Every earlier tool enriches something the attacker sent you. Shodan describes the attacker's server itself.

Cheap VPS fits disposable phishing infrastructure. The cert date matching the WHOIS date ties it to the same cluster. Internet-facing RDP is a common ransomware entry point.

Shodan is dual-use

Your own ranges

Find forgotten, unpatched or unauthorized exposed services before an attacker does: attack-surface management.

Attacker infrastructure

Reveal services, software versions and fingerprints tied to an adversary's server.

Lookups query Shodan's own scan history, so they never touch the attacker's server.

It does not replace VT, WHOIS or pDNS; it answers what none of them can.

URLScan.io: watch a page load without visiting

A sandboxed headless browser records everything the page does.

HACKTHESOCLesson 56Enrichment tools46 / 64

What a URLScan.io report captures

An isolated headless browser loads the URL like a normal visitor, so your machine never touches the page.

  • ScreenshotThe rendered page exactly as a victim would see it.
  • DOM and scriptsThe page's underlying HTML structure and any JavaScript executed.
  • Every contactAll domains and IPs contacted, including hidden redirects and background requests.
  • Cookies and brandsCookies set, and brands the page appears to impersonate from hundreds tracked.
VirusTotal can score a URL but does not always show what the page does when it loads. URLScan fills that gap.

Scaling up: STIX/TAXII, RDAP and API limits

From one-at-a-time lookups to thousands of indicators a day.

HACKTHESOCLesson 56Enrichment tools51 / 64
Manual lookups do not scale to thousands of indicators a day.

STIX and TAXII are the standards that let enrichment run automatically, at a scale no team could match by hand.

Everything so far was one indicator at a time. This section explains why some alerts arrive already enriched.
HACKTHESOCLesson 56Enrichment tools52 / 64

A STIX indicator carries its context with it

  • STIX is the what: JSON objects linking indicators, malware, actors
  • TAXII is the how: the API that delivers STIX in near-real time
  • Simplified: real STIX 2.1 carries each relationship as a separate relationship object
A SIEM, EDR or TIP subscribed to TAXII ingests thousands of indicators a day. That is why some alerts arrive pre-enriched and novel ones still need the manual workflow.
HACKTHESOCLesson 56Enrichment tools53 / 64

How a TAXII feed pre-enriches an alert

  1. 1Intel is written as STIXStructured Threat Information eXpression: JSON objects linking indicators, malware and actors.
  2. 2TAXII delivers itTrusted Automated eXchange of Intelligence Information: an API sharing STIX in near-real time.
  3. 3Your tools subscribeA SIEM, EDR or Threat Intelligence Platform (TIP) ingests thousands of indicators a day.
  4. 4Correlate automaticallyIndicators are matched against your logs with no manual lookup.
  5. 5Alert arrives enrichedFor example 'known malicious (source: TAXII feed X)'.
A truly novel, first-seen indicator is not in any feed yet, which is why the manual workflow still matters.
HACKTHESOCLesson 56Enrichment tools54 / 64

Two operational notes

RDAP

WHOIS's successor. ICANN sunset legacy WHOIS for gTLDs in early 2025. Same fields, especially creation date, in structured JSON.

Know your API limits

VT free API: roughly 500 requests a day, 4 a minute, and usually no commercial use. AbuseIPDB free: around 1,000 a day. Treat these as approximate and check current terms. Over-limit scripts fail silently.

Silent failures mid-incident are a frustrating way to lose enrichment coverage. High-volume teams move to paid tiers or TAXII feeds.

Look it up outside, or match it inside?

External lookup

Public tools apply: VT, WHOIS/RDAP, passive DNS, feeds, Shodan, URLScan.

  • File hashes (MD5, SHA-1, SHA-256)
  • IP addresses and domains
  • URLs
  • Email senders

Your own telemetry

Host-based artefacts are matched against your EDR and SIEM, not the public internet.

  • File paths
  • Registry keys
  • Mutexes
  • Service names
Recognising the category is the first step of any enrichment, because it rules half the tools in or out immediately.

Key takeaways

  1. 01Enrichment turns a bare hash, URL, domain or IP into a decision by asking several sources the same questions.
  2. 02VT ratios need reading: 0/71 means unknown, not safe; labels matter more than the number.
  3. 03WHOIS creation date flags newly registered domains; passive DNS pivots to the wider infrastructure.

  5. 05Shodan, URLScan.io and STIX/TAXII add server context, page behaviour and scale.
  6. 06OPSEC: passive first, defang, never submit or click a victim-specific URL.
